## 1. Before Pixar: the Lucasfilm Graphics Group (1979-1985)

- **1979:** George Lucas hires Ed Catmull (from the New York Institute of Technology computer graphics lab) to run the computer division of Lucasfilm. The graphics unit is later called the Graphics Group. [Wikipedia "Pixar" — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pixar]
- **Two founders, not one:** Alvy Ray Smith (also ex-NYIT) and Ed Catmull ran the group together. Smith calls them the two founders of Pixar. [Alvy Ray Smith, "The Real Story of Pixar", IEEE Spectrum, 3 Aug 2021 — https://spectrum.ieee.org/the-real-story-of-pixar]
- **Where the name comes from:** Pixar was named after the group's hardware, the Pixar Image Computer. Smith says the name came from a made-up Spanish-sounding verb meaning "to make pictures". [Alvy Ray Smith, "PIXAR = 'To make pictures'" — https://www.alvyray.com/Pixar/PixarMyth3.htm; Wikipedia "Alvy Ray Smith"]
- **John Lasseter arrives:** a Disney animator who was let go by Disney in 1983 after pushing a computer-animation project. He joined the Lucasfilm group in late 1983/early 1984 (sources vary). His "faster or cheaper?" exchange with a Disney executive is **his own anecdote**: tell it as "Lasseter says". [Wikipedia "John Lasseter"] **VERIFY** exact month.
- **The Adventures of André & Wally B. (1984):** the group's first character short, with Lasseter animating. Pioneering motion blur and squash-and-stretch in computer animation. Call it "a landmark", not "the first". [PIXAR_analysis.md Section 3; Wikipedia]
- **Lucas wants out:** after his 1983 divorce Lucas needed cash and put the computer division up for sale. Smith says a General Motors/Philips deal fell through before Jobs got it. Jobs first offered in **August 1985**, and Lucasfilm initially ignored him. [IEEE Spectrum, Smith, 2021]

## 2. The $10 million deal (Monday, 3 February 1986)

- **Date:** the deal closed on **3 February 1986** (a Monday). About 40 people were spun out as a new corporation in Richmond (Point Richmond), California. [Wikipedia "Pixar"]
- **The money:** Jobs paid **$5 million to Lucasfilm** for the technology and put **$5 million in cash** into the new company. Total: $10 million. [Wikipedia "Pixar"; IEEE Spectrum, Smith 2021]
- **Human beat (great for the cold open):** Smith writes that Jobs' first check was $5 million, and Catmull immediately signed it over to Lucasfilm to buy the technology. So half the "investment" walked straight out the door. [IEEE Spectrum, Smith 2021]
- **Ownership:** Jobs took **70%**; employees held 30%. [IEEE Spectrum, Smith 2021]
- **Jobs' situation:** he had been pushed out of Apple in September 1985 and was starting NeXT at the same time. [Wikipedia "Steve Jobs"]
- **Titles in 1986:** Catmull president, Smith executive vice president; Jobs chairman (Feb 1986 to Nov 1988) and CEO from Feb 1986. [Pixar 10-K405, filed 31 Mar 1997, "Mr. Jobs ... Chief Executive Officer since February 1986" — https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1002114/0000891618-97-001519.txt; Wikipedia "Alvy Ray Smith"]

## 3. A hardware company that couldn't sell hardware (1986-1991)

- **The product:** the Pixar Image Computer, launched 1986. Smith: Pixar marketed it as "supercomputers for pixels". [IEEE Spectrum, Smith 2021]
- **Price:** about **$135,000** each, plus a ~$35,000 Sun or SGI workstation needed to run it. [Wikipedia "Pixar Image Computer" — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pixar_Image_Computer] **VERIFY** (secondary).
- **Sales:** fewer than **300** units by April 1990 (one source says only 120 by 1988). Buyers were medical imaging, government and intelligence users, not Hollywood. [Wikipedia "Pixar Image Computer"; Cult of Mac "Today in Apple history: Pixar stops selling..." — https://www.cultofmac.com/apple-history/pixar-image-computer] **DISPUTED** 120 vs <300; say "a few hundred at most".
- **April 1990:** Pixar sold the hardware business to Vicom Systems; 18 of its ~100 staff went with it. [Wikipedia "Pixar"]
- **1991 layoffs:** about 30 more people let go, including president Chuck Kolstad, leaving 42 employees. [Wikipedia "Pixar"]
- **Money pit:** after repeated refinancings Jobs put in **about $50 million in total** (Smith: "more than $100 million in today's money"). [IEEE Spectrum, Smith 2021] Isaacson also gives "close to $50 million", more than half of what he had taken out of Apple (book, via secondary source; VERIFY).
- **Each check cost the staff:** Smith says Jobs would berate management, then write another check, and every round shrank the employees' share. [IEEE Spectrum, Smith 2021]
- **6 March 1991:** Jobs bought out the remaining employee shares and owned Pixar outright. [Wikipedia "Pixar"; IEEE Spectrum, Smith 2021]
- **Alvy Ray Smith leaves (1991):** after a clash with Jobs that Smith calls "the whiteboard incident" (Jobs, in Smith's words, "in full bullying, tyrannical mode"). He co-founded Altamira Software, later bought by Microsoft. [IEEE Spectrum, Smith 2021; Wikipedia "Alvy Ray Smith"]
- **Profit and loss from the SEC filing (in thousands of dollars):** net loss **1992: $(3,378)**, **1993: $(1,173)**, **1994: $(2,372)**; net income **1995: $1,627**; **1996: $25,319**. Shareholders' equity was negative in 1992-1994 (e.g. $(1,675) at end 1994). [Pixar 10-K405 filed 31 Mar 1997, Selected Financial Data — https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1002114/0000891618-97-001519.txt]
- **Late 1994:** when CFO Lawrence Levy joined, Jobs was covering payroll with personal checks and Pixar had negative retained earnings of about $50 million. [Computer History Museum blog "Pixar's True Story", 5 Dec 2025 — https://computerhistory.org/blog/pixars-true-story/]

### Jobs tried to get rid of it (near-sales)
- Smith: in the late 1980s Jobs would have sold Pixar "to anyone to make himself whole and unembarrassed". He also tried to fold Pixar into NeXT; NeXT's co-founders refused. [IEEE Spectrum, Smith 2021]
- As late as **1994**, Jobs discussed selling Pixar to **Hallmark Cards, Microsoft co-founder Paul Allen and Oracle's Larry Ellison**. [Wikipedia "Pixar", ref 45]
- Isaacson-based summaries also name Microsoft, Alias and Silicon Graphics as interested buyers. [Shortform summary of Isaacson — https://www.shortform.com/blog/steve-jobs-and-pixar/] **VERIFY** in the book before naming Microsoft.
- Safe script line: "More than once, Jobs went looking for a buyer. Nobody paid what he wanted."

## 4. Luxo Jr. (1986), told without showing it

- **Premiere:** SIGGRAPH, Dallas, **17 August 1986**, to an audience of about 6,000. Running time under two minutes. [Westport Tech Museum, "Today in film history, August 17th 1986" — https://www.westporttechmuseum.com/blogs/techtastic/today-in-film-history-august-17th-1986-pixars-luxo-jr-premieres-at-siggraph; CHM blog "Pixar's Luxo Jr.", Chris Garcia, 1 May 2013 — https://computerhistory.org/blog/pixars-luxo-jr/]
- **The ovation:** the standing ovation began before the final frame was on screen. [CHM blog, Garcia 2013]
- **Human beat:** graphics pioneer Jim Blinn came up afterwards and asked Lasseter: "John, was the parent lamp a mother or a father?" Lasseter took that as proof the story worked: nobody asked about the algorithms. [CHM blog, Garcia 2013]
- **Oscars:** nominated for Best Animated Short (lost). Often called the first computer-animated film nominated for an Oscar. **DISPUTED:** Peter Foldès' partly computer-animated *Hunger* was nominated in 1974, so say "the first fully computer-animated film nominated". [CHM blog; Wikipedia "Luxo Jr."]
- **Tin Toy** (made 1988) won the Oscar for Animated Short at the March 1989 ceremony: the first computer-animated film to win an Oscar. [Wikipedia "Tin Toy"]
- **RenderMan:** Pixar's rendering software, used in-house since 1988 and licensed to effects houses (used on *Terminator 2*, *Jurassic Park*, *Casper*). [Pixar 10-K405, 1997]
- **Visual rule:** no clip, still or lamp/ball silhouette. Tell it over the SIGGRAPH date card + a generic desk lamp photo + an audience-silhouette illustration.

## 5. The Disney deal and Toy Story (1991-1995)

- **1991 deal:** Disney agreed to fund and distribute three computer-animated features, about **$26 million**; the first was *Toy Story*. Smith says Catmull closed the deal in **July 1991**. [Wikipedia "Pixar"; IEEE Spectrum, Smith 2021] **VERIFY** month (some sources say May 1991).
- **Bad terms:** Pixar got a small cut. UPI quoted the prospectus: Pixar's compensation "would be very small compared to Disney's". [UPI, "Pixar goes public, soars", 29 Nov 1995 — https://www.upi.com/Archives/1995/11/29/Pixar-goes-public-soars/9307817621200/]
- **"Black Friday" (19 Nov 1993):** after Disney executive notes made Woody nasty, a story reel bombed and Disney halted production; Pixar rewrote it in about two weeks. [Wikipedia "Toy Story"] **VERIFY** exact date before putting it on screen.
- **Budget:** about **$30 million**. [Wikipedia "List of Pixar films" — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Pixar_films]
- **Making it:** 114,240 frames and about 800,000 machine-hours of rendering, on a render farm of about 117 Sun SPARCstations. Our math card: ~7 machine-hours per frame. [Wikipedia "Toy Story"] **VERIFY** the 117 count.
- **Why everything is indoors:** "Everything in the movie was set in rooms inside a house because computer graphics could do boxes." [CHM blog, 5 Dec 2025]
- **Release:** **Wednesday, 22 November 1995** (Thanksgiving week). Opening: about $29 million for the 3-day weekend. [Wikipedia "List of Pixar films"; CHM blog 2025 (gives the $29M weekend, but wrongly dates the opening 29 Nov)]
- **Box office:** No. 1 film of 1995 in the US. First run about **$191.8M domestic / ~$373M worldwide**; with re-releases **$229.9M / $401.2M**. [Wikipedia "List of Pixar films"; Box Office Mojo] Say "about $370 million in 1995".
- **Oscars:** first animated film nominated for Original Screenplay (plus Score and Song); Lasseter received a Special Achievement Academy Award. [Wikipedia "Toy Story"]

## 6. The IPO: Wednesday, 29 November 1995 (one week later)

- **Date and ticker:** Nasdaq, **PIXR**, IPO on **29 November 1995**. [Pixar 10-K405, 1997: "since Pixar's initial public offering on November 29, 1995"]
- **Price:** **$22** a share (the range in an earlier filing had been $12-14). [UPI, 29 Nov 1995]
- **First day:** high **$49.50**, close **$39** (+77%). Raised **$132 million**. [UPI, 29 Nov 1995] The 10-K confirms the Q4 1995 trading range: high $49 1/2, low $22. [Pixar 10-K405, 1997]
- **Who underwrote:** Morgan Stanley and Goldman Sachs passed; Robertson Stephens, Hambrecht & Quist and Cowen took it. [CHM blog, 5 Dec 2025]
- **Jobs becomes a billionaire:** Jobs owned about **80%**. **DISPUTED value:** UPI gives a $1.2 billion market capitalisation for the whole company; CHM and most later accounts say his stake alone was "over $1 billion" / ~$1.2B. Safe line: "On paper, Steve Jobs was now worth more than a billion dollars." [UPI 1995; CHM blog 2025; Fortune — https://fortune.com/article/how-did-apple-cofounder-steve-jobs-become-billionaire-pixar-success]
- **The hangover:** the stock fell to about $12 within weeks (CHM: "about three weeks later"; the 10-K shows a 1996 Q3 low of $12 1/4). [CHM blog 2025; Pixar 10-K405, 1997] **VERIFY** the "three weeks".
- **March 1997:** Jobs still owned **74.7%** of Pixar. [Pixar 10-K405, 1997, "Concentration of stock ownership"]

## 7. The new Disney deal and the streak (1997-2005)

- **24 Feb 1997 co-production agreement:** five films over about ten years; Pixar and Disney **co-finance, co-own and split profits 50/50** (after Disney's distribution fee); Disney controls marketing and distribution; Pixar gets **no theme-park revenue**. Disney also bought 1,000,000 Pixar shares ($15.0M gross proceeds) and got warrants. [Pixar 10-K405, 1997]
- **The streak, worldwide gross:** *A Bug's Life* (1998) $363M; *Toy Story 2* (1999) $511M; *Monsters, Inc.* (2001) $529M; *Finding Nemo* (2003) $871M first run (~$940M with the 2012 3D re-release); *The Incredibles* (2004) $631M. [Wikipedia "List of Pixar films"]
- **Brad Bird:** joined Pixar in 2000 after Warner's *The Iron Giant* (1999) flopped; the first director from outside Pixar to make a Pixar feature (*The Incredibles*, 5 Nov 2004, 2 Oscars). [Wikipedia "Brad Bird"; Wikipedia "The Incredibles"]
- **Directors to name (men only on screen):** Pete Docter (*Monsters, Inc.*, *Up*, *Inside Out*, *Soul*), Andrew Stanton (*Finding Nemo*, *WALL-E*, *Toy Story 5*), Lee Unkrich (*Toy Story 3*, *Coco*), Brad Bird (*Incredibles*, *Ratatouille*). [Wikipedia film pages]
- **Emeryville:** the new Emeryville studio (former Del Monte site, about 20 acres) opened in **November 2000**; its main building is now the Steve Jobs Building. [Wikipedia "Pixar"]

## 8. The breakup with Eisner (2003-2005)

- **Background:** Michael Eisner was Disney CEO from 1984. Roy E. Disney resigned from the board on 30 Nov 2003 and ran a "Save Disney" campaign against him. [Wikipedia "Michael Eisner"; Wikipedia "Roy E. Disney"] **VERIFY** dates.
- **Talks collapse:** Pixar walked away from renewal talks. Jobs' statement, **29 Jan 2004**: "After ten months of trying to strike a deal with Disney, we're moving on." [MacRumors, 30 Jan 2004 — https://www.macrumors.com/pages/2004/01/20040130161625.shtml; CBS News — https://www.cbsnews.com/news/pixar-to-disney-adios-mouse; Disney press release "Disney and Pixar end talks" — https://thewaltdisneycompany.com/press-releases/disney-and-pixar-end-talks/] (Wikipedia lists failed rounds on 16 Jan 2004 and 22 Jul 2004.)
- **What was at stake:** Pixar planned to find another distributor from 2006 and keep full ownership of future films. *The Incredibles* and *Cars* were still owed to Disney under the 1997 deal. [Macworld/MacRumors, Jan 2004]
- **Eisner's reply:** in a statement he wished Jobs and Lasseter's team well. [Disney press release, Jan 2004]
- **Eisner falls:** shareholders withheld about 43% of votes from him on 3 Mar 2004 and he lost the chairman title; on 13 Mar 2005 Disney named Bob Iger as his successor; Iger became CEO on **1 Oct 2005**. [Wikipedia "Michael Eisner"; Wikipedia "Bob Iger"] **VERIFY** the 43% figure.

## 9. Iger makes the call (2005-2006)

- **Hong Kong Disneyland, Sept 2005:** watching the opening parade, Iger realised the only new characters from the past decade were Pixar's. [Bloomberg, 2006, reposted at https://forums.wdwmagic.com/threads/hong-kong-parade-clinched-decision-to-buy-pixar.71383; Iger, *The Ride of a Lifetime*, 2019] Book wording not checked: VERIFY.
- **The reconciliation:** within days of becoming CEO, Iger made peace with Jobs. On **12 Oct 2005** Apple's video iPod launched with ABC shows for sale on iTunes, a Disney deal Iger agreed to quickly. [Apple press release, 12 Oct 2005; summaries of *The Ride of a Lifetime*] **VERIFY** in the Apple newsroom.
- **The deal:** announced **Tuesday, 24 Jan 2006**. All stock, **2.3 Disney shares for each Pixar share**, "transaction value is **$7.4 billion** ($6.3 billion net of Pixar's cash of just over $1 billion)". [Disney/Pixar joint press release, Exhibit 99.1 to Pixar Form 8-K, 24 Jan 2006 — https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1002114/000119312506010886/0001193125-06-010886.txt]
- **Terms for the people:** Catmull, president of both Pixar and Disney Animation; Lasseter, Chief Creative Officer of both and Principal Creative Advisor at Imagineering; **Jobs joins Disney's board**; both studios keep their locations. [same 8-K]
- **Jobs' quote (same release):** "Disney and Pixar can now collaborate without the barriers that come from two different companies with two different sets of shareholders." [8-K, 24 Jan 2006]
- **Closing:** **5 May 2006**. [Wikipedia "Pixar"; Jobs' 13D "Date of Event" = 5 May 2006]
- **Jobs' Disney stake (FIXES the analysis's "287 million"):** his **60,000,002 Pixar shares** became **138,000,004 Disney shares**; with 3 Disney shares he already held, **138,000,007 shares**, which the filing puts at "approximately **6.3%**". Press and Wikipedia say ~7% and about $3.9 billion. He became Disney's **largest individual shareholder**. [Schedule 13D filed by Jobs on Disney, May 2006 — https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1001039/000119312506103741/dsc13d.htm; Wikipedia "Pixar"]
- **Fun detail for the script:** before the deal, Steve Jobs owned exactly **three** shares of Disney. [same 13D]
- **Cars timing (FIX):** *Cars* opened **9 June 2006**, a month after Disney already owned Pixar. Say "Cars was the last film Pixar made as an independent company", not "the last independent Pixar release". [Wikipedia "List of Pixar films"]
- **After Jobs:** his Disney shares (worth about $4.6B) went to his trust after his death in Oct 2011. [CBS News — https://www.cbsnews.com/sanfrancisco/news/steve-jobs-4-6b-in-disney-shares-go-to-trust]

## 10. Golden run, then the slump (2007-2025)

### The golden run (worldwide gross)
*Ratatouille* (2007) $624M; *WALL-E* (2008) $521M; *Up* (2009) $735M; *Toy Story 3* (2010) **$1.067B**, the first animated film past $1 billion. [Wikipedia "List of Pixar films"]

### Lasseter's exit (brief, factual, no images needed)
- **21 Nov 2017:** Lasseter took a six-month leave after acknowledging "missteps" in his conduct with employees. [Wikipedia "Pixar"]
- **8 June 2018:** Disney announced he would leave at the end of 2018. **19 June 2018:** Pete Docter named Pixar's Chief Creative Officer. [Wikipedia "Pixar"]
- One sentence in the script is enough. No adjectives beyond what he himself acknowledged.

### The slump
- **The first real flop:** *The Good Dinosaur* (2015) $332M worldwide on a $175-200M budget. [Wikipedia "List of Pixar films"]
- ***Onward*** (6 Mar 2020): $141.9M worldwide on $200M; cinemas closed days after release (COVID). (FIX: Qany said $109M.) [Wikipedia "List of Pixar films"]
- **Straight to Disney+:** *Soul* (25 Dec 2020), *Luca* (18 Jun 2021), *Turning Red* (11 Mar 2022). All three got a small US cinema run only in 2024. [Wikipedia "List of Pixar films"]
- ***Lightyear*** (17 Jun 2022): **$226.4M** worldwide on a $200M budget. [Wikipedia "List of Pixar films"]
- ***Elemental*** (16 Jun 2023): slow start, a $29.6M US opening, then strong word of mouth to **$496.4M** worldwide. [Wikipedia "List of Pixar films"; Box Office Mojo] **VERIFY** the $29.6M.
- ***Inside Out 2*** (14 Jun 2024): **$1.699B**, still Pixar's biggest film. (FIX: it is no longer "the highest-grossing animated film ever"; China's *Ne Zha 2* passed it in 2025.) [Wikipedia "List of Pixar films"; PIXAR_analysis.md]
- **Layoffs:** about **175 people (14%)** cut on 21 May 2024, as Pixar moved away from Disney+ series. Pixar president Jim Morris wrote that the studio would "return to our focus on feature films". [Fortune, 22 May 2024 — https://fortune.com/2024/05/22/disney-pixar-layoffs/; Washington Times, 21 May 2024]

### Elio (2025) and the post
- **Release:** 20 June 2025. US opening **$20.8M**, the **worst opening in Pixar history**. [The Numbers — https://www.the-numbers.com/movie/Elio-(2025); Axios, 23 Jun 2025 — https://www.axios.com/2025/06/23/elio-pixar-box-office]
- **Final:** **$73.0M domestic / $154.3M worldwide** on a reported $150M+ budget. [The Numbers, checked 27 Nov 2026]
- **The post:** around **27 June 2025**, Pixar's official Instagram (@pixar) posted a short video in the "who said that?" trend. A TikTok creator says: "Stop complaining that Disney doesn't make original stories if you don't show up to movie theaters." Many fans read it as blaming the audience. [Inside the Magic, 27 Jun 2025 — https://insidethemagic.net/2025/06/pixar-calls-out-fans-on-social-media-mg1/; CinemaBlend — https://cinemablend.com/movies/disney-really-went-there-with-new-elio-post-stop-complaining-disney-doesnt-make-original-stories; WDWNT — https://wdwnt.com/2025/06/stop-complaining-pixar-urges-public-to-view-elio-in-theaters-with-popular-tiktok-trend/]
  - It was the **official account**, not "an employee" (fixes Qany's contradiction).
  - The person in the video is a woman: **do not screenshot**. Type the line ourselves on a plain phone-frame graphic.
  - No source I found says it was deleted; don't claim it was.
- **Behind the scenes (optional, neutral):** reports that the original director left and storylines were changed in production; in March 2026 Docter defended the edits in the Wall Street Journal. [Daily Beast; PinkNews, 9 Mar 2026 — https://www.thepinknews.com/2026/03/09/pixar-pete-docter-elios-cut-queer-plot/] Keep this to one neutral line or skip it (brand safety).

## 11. 2026: the comeback year

- ***Hoppers*** (Pixar's 30th feature; dir. Daniel Chong), **6 Mar 2026**: No. 1 opening, **$45.3M** US; final ~**$166.0M US / $389.7M worldwide** on a $150M budget. Reported as Pixar's best opening for an original film since *Coco* (2017). [The Numbers — https://www.the-numbers.com/movie/Hoppers-(2026); Fortune, 9 Mar 2026 — https://fortune.com/2026/03/09/box-office-hoppers-number-1-disney-pixar-the-bride-bombs/; WDWNT — https://wdwnt.com/2026/03/hoppers-opens-at-1-nets-pixars-best-opening-weekend-for-an-original-film-since-2017/]
- ***Toy Story 5*** (dir. Andrew Stanton, co-dir. McKenna Harris), **19 Jun 2026**: **$160M** US opening (franchise record), $312M worldwide opening weekend. [TheWrap — https://www.thewrap.com/creative-content/movies/toy-story-5-312-million-global-opening/; Variety AU]
- **Passed $1B** in July 2026: Pixar's **sixth** billion-dollar film. [Cartoon Brew — https://www.cartoonbrew.com/box-office-report/toy-story-5-billion-dollar-box-office-264930.html; WDWNT, Jul 2026 — https://wdwnt.com/2026/07/toy-story-5-crosses-1-billion-at-global-box-office/]
- **Latest total:** **~$1.15B worldwide** ($480.3M US + $669.4M international) on a $250M budget. [The Numbers, latest international report 25 Nov 2026 — https://www.the-numbers.com/movie/Toy-Story-5-(2026)] Wikipedia shows $1.136B. Say "about $1.1 billion". That puts it **3rd among Pixar films**, behind *Inside Out 2* ($1.70B) and *Incredibles 2* ($1.24B), and ahead of *Toy Story 4* ($1.07B).
- **Closing math for the script:** $10 million in 1986 → $7.4 billion in 2006 → one sequel in 2026 grosses about $1.15 billion in a summer.

## 13. Do-not-say list

1. "Steve Jobs founded Pixar." He bought and funded it. The founders were Ed Catmull and Alvy Ray Smith (Jobs is listed as a co-founder in Pixar's own 1997 filing, so say "bought").
2. "Jobs paid George Lucas $10 million." Lucas got $5M; the other $5M went into the company.
3. "Jobs owned 287 million Disney shares" or "49% of Pixar". See section 12.
4. "Cars was the last independent Pixar film."
5. "Pixar's IPO was the biggest of 1995."
6. "Toy Story was the highest-grossing film in the world in 1995." Say "No. 1 in America".
7. "Luxo Jr. was the first computer-animated film ever nominated for an Oscar." Say "first fully computer-animated".
8. "Jobs tried to sell Pixar to Microsoft." Only secondary summaries say so. Wikipedia names Hallmark, Paul Allen and Larry Ellison (1994). Say "went looking for a buyer".
9. "Jobs lost a million dollars a year." He put in about $50 million.
10. "Elio is Pixar's lowest-grossing film ever." It had the worst *opening*. *Onward* (COVID) grossed less in total ($141.9M vs $154.3M).
11. "An employee blamed fans" or "Pixar deleted the post". It was the official account; no deletion confirmed.
12. "Inside Out 2 is the biggest animated film ever." Not since *Ne Zha 2* (2025).
13. Any claim about Lasseter beyond the dated facts (Nov 2017 leave after acknowledging "missteps"; June 2018 exit announced).
14. Exact quotes from Isaacson's or Iger's books that we have not read (e.g. the Ellison "I made it" call is a rumour).
15. "Katzenberg stole A Bug's Life." Contested; and we don't need it.
16. "We'll show you the 1995 prospectus." It's not on EDGAR.
17. Culture-war framing of *Luca*, *Lightyear* or *Elio* (Qany's asides). Business facts only.

## 14. Best hook facts (verified)

1. Jobs' first $5 million check never stayed at Pixar: Catmull signed it straight over to Lucasfilm. [IEEE Spectrum, Smith 2021]
