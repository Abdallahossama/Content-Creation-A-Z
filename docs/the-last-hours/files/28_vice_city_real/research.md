## 1. Dadeland Mall shooting — Wednesday, 11 July 1979

- **When:** about 2:30 p.m. on 11 July 1979. [NBC 6 Miami, Brian Hamacher, "'Dadeland Mall Massacre': Thursday Marks 40th Anniversary…", 11 Jul 2019 — https://www.nbcmiami.com/news/local/dadeland-mall-massacre-thursday-marks-40th-anniversary-of-cocaine-cowboys-shootout/127956/]
- **Where:** the Crown Liquors shop at Dadeland Mall in Kendall, unincorporated Dade County. [NBC 6, same]
- **Killed:** German Jimenez Panesso, the target (NBC calls him "a major figure in the Colombian drug trade"), and his bodyguard, Juan Carlos Hernandez. [NBC 6, same]
- **Wounded:** NBC 6 says two liquor-store employees. **DISPUTED:** a Miami Herald/AOL list says "four others injured." Safe line: "two dead, at least two wounded." [NBC 6, same; AOL/Miami Herald "15 of Miami's most shocking crimes" — https://www.aol.com/articles/15-miami-most-shocking-crimes-100000660.html]
- **Spray of fire:** dozens of rounds went across the mall parking lot and hit parked cars. [NBC 6, same]
- **Weapons (secondary source, VERIFY):** one account says a silenced .380 Beretta pistol was used on Panesso and a full-auto MAC-10 submachine gun on the bodyguard. This comes from retellings of Gerald Posner's 2009 book, not from a police release. Safer line: "a pistol and a submachine gun." [search summary of NBC/WTVJ and gun-history retellings; Wikipedia "Miami drug war" cites NYT, George Volsky, 22 Jul 1979]
- **The van:** the gunmen fled in a fake delivery van lettered "Happy Time Complete Party Supply" and left it at the far end of the parking lot. [NBC 6, same]
- **"War wagon":** police called the van a "war wagon." Inside were an arsenal of firearms and bulletproof vests. [NBC 6, same]
- **Armour (VERIFY):** WTVJ captions mention bulletproof windows, and the van is described as a white Ford Econoline with crudely stencilled red signs. I found no primary source for armour plating. Say "bullet-resistant", not "armoured tank". [NBC 6/WTVJ, "How WTVJ Covered the 1979 Dadeland Mall Shooting" — https://www.nbcmiami.com/multimedia/how-wtvj-covered-the-1979-dadeland-mall-shooting_miami/127959/]
- **Where "Cocaine Cowboys" came from:** NBC reports that an officer at the scene gave the shooters the nickname "Cocaine Cowboys." [NBC 6, same]
- **Police were outgunned:** ex-Miami homicide detective Nelson Andreu (later West Miami police chief) said police carried six-shot revolvers while traffickers had semi-automatics and submachine guns. [NBC 6, same]
- **Quote:** Andreu called the era "complete chaos." [NBC 6, same]
- **Context:** the first seven months of 1979 were called the bloodiest in South Florida's history up to that time. [NBC 6, same]
- **Why it marks "the start":** historians and press call Dadeland the opening event of the Miami drug war. [Wikipedia "Dadeland Mall", citing History Television "Underworld Histories: Miami", 15 Mar 2011; Wikipedia "Miami drug war"]
- **Prosecution:** no one was ever prosecuted for the Dadeland shooting. [Telemundo 51 retrospective, 2019 — https://www.telemundo51.com/noticias/local/a-40-anos-de-la-masacre-del-dadeland-mall/127810/] (VERIFY with a second source.)
- **Federal follow-up:** NBC's anniversary piece ties Dadeland and the violence that followed to the creation of the federal South Florida Task Force in 1982 (see section 5). There is no FBI/DEA case file specific to Dadeland that I could find. [NBC 6, same]

## 2. The drug-money boom

- **Time magazine, 23 Nov 1981:** Miami's Federal Reserve branch had a currency surplus of $5 billion, "more than the nation's twelve Federal Reserve banks combined" (Time's wording, as summarised). [TIME, James Kelly, "South Florida: Trouble in Paradise", 23 Nov 1981 — https://time.com/archive/6856569/south-florida-trouble-in-paradise]
- **DISPUTED 1979 surplus:** Wikipedia cites a 1979 Federal Reserve cash-flow study that found Florida had a **$5.5 billion** surplus. A former IRS investigator quoted by Miami New Times put it at **$7 billion**. Safe range: "$5 to 7 billion a year." [Wikipedia "Operation Greenback"; Miami New Times, "Awash in a Sea of Money", 6 Oct 2005]
- **Primary-ish source:** Mike McDonald, 27-year IRS Criminal Investigation veteran, said a Treasury analysis found that in 1978 and 1979 "the entire currency surplus of the United States was attributed to south Florida." [PBS Frontline "Drug Wars", interview with Mike McDonald, 2000 — https://www.pbs.org/wgbh/pages/frontline/shows/drugs/interviews/mcdonald.html]
- **Banks ignored the reporting law:** McDonald recalled only "129,000 CTRs" (Currency Transaction Reports) filed by banks nationwide in 1979, against about 12 million a year by 2000. [PBS Frontline, same]
- **Later figures:** the Miami branch surplus was still about $6 billion in 1985 (Miami New Times) and $5.1 billion in 1990 (Miami Herald via UPI). [UPI, "Miami flooded by cocaine cash", 11 Feb 1990 — https://www.upi.com/Archives/1990/02/11/Miami-flooded-by-cocaine-cash/8767634712400/]
- **Drug share:** about 70% of all marijuana and cocaine imported into the US passed through South Florida (Time, 1981). [TIME, 23 Nov 1981]
- **Size of the trade:** drug smuggling was estimated at **$7 to $12 billion a year** in South Florida (Time, 1981). [TIME, same]
- **Reagan, 28 Jan 1982:** South Florida had become "the Nation's major terminal for the smuggling of illegal drugs into the United States." [Reagan Library, Statement Announcing Establishment of a Federal Anti-Crime Task Force for Southern Florida, 28 Jan 1982 — https://www.reaganlibrary.gov/research/speeches/12882b]
- **Real-estate mood quote:** a Miami real-estate agent told Time: "No city in the world has ever had happen to it what has happened to us." (Jane Cousins). [TIME, 23 Nov 1981]
- **Operation Greenback:** a Miami money-laundering task force formed in late 1979 at Treasury's suggestion. Operation Greenback began in early 1980 with Customs, IRS and Justice Department attorneys. [PBS Frontline, McDonald; Wikipedia "Operation Greenback"]
- **Greenback seizure:** in Nov 1980 agents seized $1.6 million in suspected drug profits and two aircraft at Opa-locka. [UPI, "Agents seize $1.6 million in suspected drug profits", 24 Nov 1980 — https://www.upi.com/Archives/1980/11/24/Agents-seize-16-million-in-suspected-drug-profits/6608343890000/]
- **Big Greenback arrest:** Isaac Kattan Kassin was arrested in Feb 1981, held on a $10 million bond and later sentenced to 30 years. [NYT, Jo Thomas, "Suspect Held on $10 Million Bond in a Crackdown on Drugs in Miami", 28 Feb 1981 — https://www.nytimes.com/1981/02/28/us/suspect-held-on-10-million-bond-in-a-crackdown-on-drugs-in-miami.html]
- **A bank pleads guilty:** Great American Bank of Dade County pleaded guilty in 1984 in a $94 million laundering scheme. [UPI, 17 Apr 1984 — https://www.upi.com/Archives/1984/04/17/Bank-pleads-guilty-in-94-million-money-laundering-scheme/8235451026000/]
- **Greenback totals:** 125 arrests by Oct 1982. Gaps exposed by the case fed into the Money Laundering Control Act of 1986. [Wikipedia "Operation Greenback", citing Washington Post 23 Oct 1982 and Grosse 2001] (VERIFY.)

## 3. Mariel boatlift — April to October 1980

- **Trigger:** on 1 Apr 1980, six Cubans drove a bus through the fence of the Peruvian Embassy in Havana. A Cuban guard was killed by a ricochet, and about 10,000 people then crowded into the embassy grounds. [National Archives "The Text Message" blog, Lynn Nashorn, "Castro, Cuba, and a Fleet of Fishing Boats", 3 Aug 2021 — https://text-message.blogs.archives.gov/2021/08/03/the-causes-and-effects-of-the-mariel-boatlift]
- **Opening of Mariel:** Castro opened the port of Mariel to anyone who could get a boat. NARA dates this to 20 Apr 1980, but **DISPUTED:** History.com says 1 May. Use "late April 1980." [NARA blog, same; History.com, Karen Juanita Carrillo, 28 Sep 2020 — https://www.history.com/articles/mariel-boatlift-castro-carter-cold-war]
- **First boat:** reached Key West on 21 Apr 1980 with 48 people. [NARA blog]
- **Fleet:** about 1,700 boats, arranged by exiles in Florida. [NARA blog; History.com]
- **Peak month:** 86,488 people arrived in May 1980, 69% of the total. [NARA blog]
- **Total:** about 125,000 people (History.com gives 125,266). Officially 15 Apr to 31 Oct 1980. [History.com; NARA blog]
- **Carter:** in May 1980 he declared an "open arms" policy. In June 1980 he created the Cuban-Haitian Entrant Program, and in October 1980 his administration negotiated the end of the boatlift. [NARA blog; History.com]
- **Carter quote:** "I have not been elected President of the United States to kill refugees." [quoted in NARA blog]
- **Earlier offer:** Carter had first offered asylum to 3,500 people under the Refugee Act of 1980. [History.com]
- **Criminal records, DISPUTED:**
  - History.com: "up to 20,000" had criminal records, and thousands had been in mental institutions.
  - Other counts say about 23,000 admitted to prior convictions, and many of those were for acts that are not crimes in the US or were political.
  - A widely cited academic figure is that about 4% were hardened criminals.
  - Time (1981) said officials estimated "up to 5,000 hard-core criminals."
  - Safe line: "a few thousand were serious criminals, out of 125,000." [History.com; TIME 23 Nov 1981; NPR transcripts 2023]
- **Time 1981 claim (treat with care):** Marielitos were "believed responsible" for half of Miami's violent crime. This is an official estimate of the time, not a measured fact. [TIME, 23 Nov 1981]
- **Scarface:** the film (Universal, Dec 1983, dir. Brian De Palma, written by Oliver Stone) opens with a title card claiming 25,000 of the refugees had criminal records. That number is the film's, not an official statistic. [search summary of film opening; History.com on Scarface stereotypes]
- **Stereotype:** University of Pennsylvania professor Amalia Dache cites Scarface as an example of the stereotypes used against Marielitos. [History.com]
- **Miami workforce grew** by about 7% because of the boatlift. [NARA blog]

## 4. 1980-1981: the violence

- **Homicides by year, DISPUTED for 1980:**
  - 1980: 569 Dade County homicides (35 per 100,000) in William Wilbanks' academic study, against 573 in Miami New Times.
  - 1981: **621**, the all-time record, per the Dade County Medical Examiner as cited by Miami New Times.
  - Wikipedia gives 349 for 1979, 573 for 1980 and 621 for 1981.
  - [NCJRS abstract, Wilbanks, "Murder in Miami" (1984) — https://ojp.gov/ncjrs/virtual-library/abstracts/murder-miami-analysis-homicide-patterns-and-trends-dade-county; Miami New Times "1981: Miami's Deadliest Summer"; Wikipedia "Miami drug war"]
- **Murder rate:** Miami had the nation's highest rate, 70 per 100,000 in 1980 (city of Miami). [TIME, 23 Nov 1981]
- **Time, 24 Nov 1980:** the city of Miami had 134 murders in all of 1979 and 201 by late Nov 1980. Dade County residents bought over 40,000 handguns in the 12 months to 30 Sept 1980. [TIME, James Kelly, "Nation: 'Absolute War in Our Streets'", 24 Nov 1980 — https://time.com/archive/6698517/nation-absolute-war-in-our-streets/]
- **Quote:** Miami Beach Commissioner Alex Daoud: "An absolute war is being fought in our streets at night." [TIME, 24 Nov 1980]
- **Quote:** an unnamed Miami policeman: "Sometimes I think I'm in Dodge City." [TIME, 24 Nov 1980]
- **Refrigerated truck:** Time (Nov 1981) says Dade County Medical Examiner Joe Davis rented a refrigerated "hamburger van" to hold the overflow of bodies. Miami New Times says the morgue ran out of room in 1981 and the truck stayed in use until 1988, when the new morgue opened. [TIME 23 Nov 1981; Miami New Times "1981: Miami's Deadliest Summer"]
- **Burger King detail, VERIFY:** only Oxygen says the trailer came from Burger King, at $800 a month. Safe line: "a refrigerated trailer borrowed from the fast-food trade." [Oxygen.com — https://www.oxygen.com/blogs/in-1980s-miami-a-morgue-rented-a-burger-king-trailer-to-manage-the-dead-bodies]
- **Time cover, 23 Nov 1981:** "Paradise Lost?" (with a question mark), about South Florida, written by James Kelly. The cover art is a frowning sun over a postcard-style script. [TIME archive; Hyperallergic description]
- **Mayor quote:** Mayor Maurice Ferré: "You can't compare us to Boston or Denver." [TIME, 23 Nov 1981]
- **Optimist quote:** businessman Mitchell Wolfson: "It's a magic place, it always snaps back." [TIME, 23 Nov 1981]
- **Murder capital:** Dade County recorded 425 murders in 1984. [Wikipedia "Miami drug war"] (VERIFY.)
- **McDuffie riots (brief, facts only):** in Dec 1979 Arthur McDuffie, a Black insurance executive and former Marine, died after a beating by Dade County police officers following a motorcycle chase. [Wikipedia "1980 Miami riots"; EBSCO Research Starters]
- **The acquittal and the riots:** on 17 May 1980 an all-white jury in Tampa acquitted the officers. Riots followed from 17 to 20 May 1980 in Liberty City, Overtown and other areas. [UPI 29 Dec 1982 — https://www.upi.com/Archives/1982/12/29/Overtown-a-frightening-reminder/7706409986000/; WLRN, 17 May 2015]
- **Toll:** at least 18 dead, about 350 injured, about 600 arrested and about $100 million in damage. [Wikipedia "1980 Miami riots"; WLRN]
- **Number of officers, DISPUTED:** sources say four or five. Say "the officers."
- **Settlement:** in 1981 Dade County settled the McDuffie family's lawsuit for $1.1 million. [Wikipedia "1980 Miami riots"]

## 5. South Florida Task Force (1982) and the Miami River Cops

- **Founding:** President Reagan established the task force on 28 Jan 1982, "headed by Vice President George Bush." Members included Secretaries Haig, Weinberger, Regan and Lewis, Attorney General Smith and Edwin Meese, with a working group under Adm. Daniel Murphy. [Reagan Library, statement of 28 Jan 1982 — https://www.reaganlibrary.gov/research/speeches/12882b]
- **Reagan quote:** "Massive immigration, rampant crime, and epidemic drug smuggling have created a serious problem." [Reagan Library, 28 Jan 1982]
- **Start of operations:** Wikipedia says the task force began work in March 1982. The extra federal agents and military support it brought in are not itemised in my sources. [Wikipedia "Miami drug war"] (VERIFY.)
- **Results claimed:** Reagan said on 17 Nov 1982 that drug seizures in the area were up about 50% and drug arrests up 27% since the task force began. [Reagan Library, remarks 17 Nov 1982 — https://www.reaganlibrary.gov/111782b]
- **Caveat:** a 1983 GAO report warned that combined seizure statistics can be overstated when several agencies share credit. Smuggling also shifted to other routes. Present the "50%" as the administration's claim. [search summary of GAO 1983]
- **River Cops raid:** in July 1985 Miami police officers raided the boat Mary C on the Miami River and stole roughly 350-400 kg of cocaine. Three crew members drowned trying to escape. [UPI, "River Cops conviction relieves police", 9 Feb 1988 — https://www.upi.com/Archives/1988/02/09/River-Cops-conviction-relieves-police/7977571381200/; Wikipedia "Miami River Cops Scandal"]
- **Dates, DISPUTED:** the raid is dated 28 or 29 July 1985. Miami New Times says two drowned rather than three. Use three (UPI).
- **Trials:** the first trial, with 7 officers, began in late 1986 (Wikipedia says Sept, UPI says Jan 1987) and ended with a deadlocked jury. In the retrial, Mario Carballo and Osvaldo Coello were convicted in Feb 1988. [UPI 9 Feb 1988]
- **Charges and fallout:** by Feb 1988, 19 officers or ex-officers had been charged, 11 had pleaded guilty and 3 had been convicted. 72 officers had been relieved of duty. [UPI 9 Feb 1988]
- **Quote:** Chief Clarence Dickson: "A great deal of relief just came out through the halls of the building." [UPI 9 Feb 1988]
- **Why it happened:** the Miami Police Department hired 714 officers in 1981-82, more than doubling its size, with weak vetting. [Wikipedia "Miami drug war", citing Lersch 2001]

## 6. Miami Vice (NBC)

- **Premiere:** the two-hour pilot "Brother's Keeper" aired on NBC on Sunday, 16 Sep 1984. It was written by Anthony Yerkovich and directed by Thomas Carter. The series ran until 1989. [CBS Miami, "Flashy & Trend-Setting 'Miami Vice' Debuted 30 Years Ago", Sep 2014 — https://www.cbsnews.com/miami/news/flashy-trend-setting-miami-vice-debuted-30-years-ago; Britannica]
- **Creator:** Anthony Yerkovich, then 34, a former Hill Street Blues writer. The pilot's first title was "Gold Coast." [TIME, Richard Zoglin, "Video: Cool Cops, Hot Show", 16 Sep 1985 — https://time.com/archive/6704652/video-cool-cops-hot-show]
- **Michael Mann:** executive producer, credited by Time with the music and visual style. [TIME, 16 Sep 1985]
- **The "MTV cops" memo:** Time (1985) reports that NBC programming chief Brandon Tartikoff wrote himself a two-word note, "MTV cops," then pitched it to Yerkovich. [TIME, 16 Sep 1985]
- **Memo, DISPUTED:** Yerkovich says he had been developing the idea for years before that note, starting from reports about laundered money and police seizing drug dealers' property. CBR's verdict is that the note probably existed but was not the show's origin. Safe line: "Legend says it started with a two-word memo: 'MTV cops.'" [CBR, Brian Cronin, "TV Legends Revealed", 6 Aug 2014 — https://www.cbr.com/tv-legends-revealed-did-miami-vice-really-begin-as-mtv-cops/]
- **Yerkovich quote:** he called the show "a sort of a modern-day American Casablanca." [TIME 1985, via CBR]
- **Budget:** about $1.3 million per episode, against about $1 million for a typical cop show. [TIME, 16 Sep 1985]
- **Effect on Miami:** city officials estimated the show added about $1 million per episode to the local economy. [TIME, 16 Sep 1985]
- **Mayor quote:** Mayor Ferré said it "shows Miami's beauty." [TIME, 16 Sep 1985]
- **Fashion:** Macy's opened a Miami Vice section and Kenneth Cole planned "Crockett" and "Tubbs" shoes. Bloomingdale's Kal Ruttenstein said it "has taken Italian men's fashion and spread it to mass America." [TIME, 16 Sep 1985]
- **Early ratings:** weak at first in its Friday slot against Falcon Crest, then summer reruns made it a hit. [TIME, 16 Sep 1985]

## 7. FBI Miami shootout — Friday, 11 April 1986

**Timeline. All times are a.m., Eastern Standard Time.**

- **Before 8:45:** the FBI Miami bank-robbery squad (C-1) expected the robbers to strike that Friday. They were looking for a black Monte Carlo stolen from Jose Collazo. [Wikipedia "1986 FBI Miami shootout", citing FBI file 89-6047]
- **8:45:** a rolling stakeout led by Supervisory Special Agent Gordon McNeill began from a Home Depot parking lot (exact location not confirmed). 14 agents took part, and 8 of them later fought in 5 unmarked cars. [Wikipedia, same]
- **About 9:30:** agents Ben Grogan and Jerry Dove spotted the Monte Carlo near the Barnett Shopping Center and began following it, joined by Hanlon, Manauzzi and McNeill. [Wikipedia, same]
- **About 9:35-9:40:** a felony car stop on SW 82nd Avenue. Agents rammed the Monte Carlo, which ended nose-first against a tree beside a parked car at **12201 SW 82nd Avenue** (SW 82nd Ave and 122nd St, Suniland area, now Pinecrest). [Wikipedia; CBS/AP, 11 Apr 2016 — https://www.cbsnews.com/news/fbi-marks-30-years-since-infamous-bloody-miami-shootout]
- **Start time:** I found no source for **9:45**. Sources give about 9:30 for the sighting and about 9:35-9:40 for the gunfight. Say "just after half past nine in the morning."
- **The gunfight:** under 5 minutes, about **145 shots**. CBS/AP says "more than 140." [Wikipedia; CBS/AP]
- **The odds:** 8 agents against 2 suspects. AP puts it at 4 to 1. [CBS/AP]
- **Who fired what:** Platt fired at least 42 rounds from a Ruger Mini-14 (.223) plus revolver rounds, so at least 48 in all. Matix fired one shotgun round. Agents fired roughly 70-80. [Wikipedia, citing W. French Anderson, "Forensic Analysis of the April 11, 1986, FBI Firefight", 1996]
- **The decisive early hit:** a 9 mm round fired by Jerry Dove hit Platt in the arm and chest early in the fight and stopped short of his heart. Platt kept fighting for minutes afterwards and killed both agents. **DISPUTED:** Wikipedia says "an inch" short, Mireles (Gun Digest) says "about two inches." [Wikipedia; Gun Digest, Edmundo Mireles, "1986 Miami Shootout: The Aftermath", 7 Aug 2020 — https://gundigest.com/tactical/1986-miami-shootout-the-aftermath]
- **The ending:** agent Edmundo Mireles Jr., badly wounded in the left arm, fired a pump shotgun one-handed and then his revolver, hitting both suspects and ending the fight. He received the FBI Medal of Valor. [Wikipedia]
- **Killed:** Special Agent Benjamin P. Grogan (25-year veteran, one year from retirement, nicknamed "The Doctor") and Special Agent Jerry L. Dove (30, four years in the FBI). **DISPUTED:** Grogan's age is 52 in Wikipedia and 53 per AP. [CBS/AP 11 Apr 2016; Wikipedia]
- **Wounded (5):** Gordon McNeill, Richard Manauzzi, Edmundo Mireles Jr., John Hanlon and Gilbert Orrantia. Ronald Risner was the only one of the eight not hurt. [Wikipedia]
- **FBI's own count:** "two dead, three seriously wounded, and two others injured." [FBI.gov, "Fatal Firefight in Miami", 11 Apr 2011 — https://www.fbi.gov/news/stories/fatal-firefight-in-miami (seen in search snippet)]
- **FBI wording:** it called this "one of the deadliest and most violent shoot-outs in FBI history." [FBI.gov, same]
- **AP wording:** "the bloodiest shootout in FBI history." [CBS/AP 2016]

**The suspects**

- Michael Lee Platt (b. 3 Feb 1954) and William Russell Matix (b. 25 Jun 1951) were both ex-US Army (they met at Fort Campbell, Kentucky) and ran a tree/landscaping business in Miami. [Wikipedia; Washington Post, Mary Thornton, "Miami Killers Led Double Lives", 20 Apr 1986]
- **Earlier crimes (Oct 1985 to Mar 1986, Dade County):**
  - 4 Oct 1985: murder of Emilio Briel at an Everglades target-shooting area, and theft of his car.
  - Oct 1985: several armoured-car courier robberies or attempts, including one where courier Jose Sanchez was wounded on 16 Oct.
  - 8 Nov 1985: two bank robberies on South Dixie Highway ($10,000 and $41,469).
  - 10 Jan 1986: a Brink's courier was shot and $54,000 taken.
  - 12 Mar 1986: Jose Collazo was shot and his Monte Carlo stolen. He survived.
  - 19 Mar 1986: Barnett Bank robbery ($8,338).
  - [Wikipedia, citing FBI file 89-6047] (VERIFY dollar amounts against the FBI report.)
- **Unproven earlier deaths:** Matix's first wife and a co-worker were stabbed to death in Columbus, Ohio, on 30 Dec 1983. He was a suspect but was never charged. Platt's wife's death in Dec 1984 was ruled a suicide. **Do not present either as murders.** [Wikipedia]
- **Weapons:** Platt had a Ruger Mini-14 plus .357 revolvers. Matix had a 12-gauge S&W 3000 shotgun. Agents had mostly S&W 9 mm Model 459 pistols and .357/.38 revolvers. Only two cars had shotguns, no agent had a rifle, and only two wore vests, rated for handgun rounds. [Wikipedia; CBS/AP]

**What changed afterwards**

- **The Silvertip:** the FBI's 115-grain 9 mm Winchester Silvertip load came under scrutiny for failing to penetrate deeply enough to reach vital organs. That is the round that stopped short of Platt's heart. [Gun Digest/ShootingIllustrated summaries; Wikipedia "10mm Auto"] (VERIFY the exact load against the FBI report.)
- **New pistols:** the FBI concluded that revolvers' limited stopping power and slow reloading contributed to the deaths, and moved to magazine-fed semi-automatic pistols. [Wikipedia]
- **Wound ballistics workshop:** held by the FBI. **DISPUTED date:** Mireles says "later in 1988," other sources say Sept 1987. [Gun Digest, Mireles 2020]
- **10 mm:** the cartridge was selected for service in 1989. The FBI signed a Jan 1990 contract for 9,500 Smith & Wesson Model 1076 pistols in 10 mm. Agents used a reduced-velocity "10 mm Lite" load, not full-power 10 mm. [Gun Digest, Bill Vanderpool, "10mm Handguns and the FBI", 25 Jul 2018 — https://gundigest.com/article/10mm-handguns-and-the-fbi; Wikipedia]
- **.40 S&W:** Smith & Wesson and Winchester developed it in 1990 as a shorter cartridge with ballistics like the 10 mm Lite. FBI agents were widely issued Glock .40s from about 1997. [Wikipedia ".40 S&W"; search summary]
- **Back to 9 mm:** the FBI returned to 9 mm in 2014-2015, citing improved bullet technology. [Wikipedia ".40 S&W"]
- **Training changes:** moving-and-shooting drills, one-handed and weak-hand shooting and reloading, shooting from cars and at moving targets, and more car-stop training. The FBI also bought more shotguns and MP5 submachine guns and authorized rifles. [Gun Digest, Mireles 2020]
- **Memorials:** in 2001 Pinecrest co-named part of SW 82nd Ave "Agent Benjamin Grogan Avenue" and "Agent Jerry Dove Avenue." The FBI Miami field office building in Miramar is named after both agents. [CBS/AP 2016]
- **TV movie:** "In the Line of Duty: The F.B.I. Murders" (NBC, 1988). [Wikipedia]

## 9. Griselda Blanco (facts only, no images)

- **Role:** a Colombian trafficker who ran a Miami cocaine operation from the late 1970s. She was nicknamed the "Black Widow" and "Godmother." [NBC 6 2019; Wikipedia "Griselda Blanco"]
- **Dadeland link:** **attributed, never proven.** Former Miami homicide detective Nelson Andreu and others "believe" she masterminded the hit, and she was never charged for it. Script wording: "Police came to believe Griselda Blanco's people were behind it. She was never charged." [NBC 6, 11 Jul 2019]
- **Arrest:** on 17 Feb 1985 DEA agents arrested her at her home in Irvine, California. The DEA agent widely credited is Robert "Bob" Palombo, who survived and was not killed. [Biography.com; National Geographic; Wikipedia]
- **Sentences:**
  - Federal: convicted in New York on cocaine charges and sentenced to 15 years (some reports add a $25,000 fine on 8 Nov 1985). **DISPUTED:** Semana says 25 years.
  - Florida: in 1998 she pleaded guilty to three counts of second-degree murder, with 20 years running concurrently. The state case had collapsed earlier over a phone-sex scandal involving hitman Jorge "Rivi" Ayala and secretaries in the state attorney's office.
  - [Wikipedia "Griselda Blanco", citing AP 24 Feb 1998]
- **Deportation:** released in 2004 and deported to Colombia. [Wikipedia]
- **Death:** shot dead by a gunman on a motorcycle outside a butcher shop in Medellín on 3 Sep 2012, aged 69. [The Guardian, Richard Luscombe, 4 Sep 2012; Miami Herald, 5 Sep 2012]
- **Body count:** **unprovable.** "Suspected in at least 40, possibly up to 200 murders" is a common claim with no court record behind it. Do not state a number as fact. [Wikipedia "Miami drug war"]

## Do-not-say list (myths or unprovable)

1. "The van was an armoured tank." Police called it a "war wagon" with weapons and vests inside. Bulletproof glass is mentioned, but armour plating is not confirmed.
2. "Griselda Blanco ordered the Dadeland hit" stated as fact. It is a police belief, and she was never charged.
3. "Griselda killed 200 people." No court record supports this. She pleaded guilty to three second-degree murders.
4. "Castro emptied his prisons and sent 25,000 criminals." The 25,000 figure comes from Scarface's title card. Official estimates of serious criminals were "a few thousand" out of about 125,000.
5. "Marielitos caused half of Miami's crime" as fact. This was a 1981 official estimate reported by Time.
6. "The morgue rented a Burger King truck." Only one source says this. Time says a refrigerated "hamburger van."
7. "Miami Vice was born from a napkin that said MTV cops." Present this as legend. The creator disputes it.
8. "The shootout began at 9:45." Sources say about 9:30 (sighting) and 9:35-9:40 (gunfire).
9. "The FBI issued full-power 10 mm." Agents got the reduced "10 mm Lite" load.
10. "Matix murdered his wife" and "Platt murdered his wife." Never charged. Platt's wife's death was ruled a suicide.
11. "A single bullet changed everything." Simplified. The FBI report cites several factors: revolvers, few shotguns, no rifles, vests and car-stop tactics.
12. Exact 1980 homicide total as a single number. 569 or 573 depending on source.
13. "Vice City came out on 29 October 2002." Most sources say 27 October.
14. "The Fed's Miami branch had more cash than all other branches combined" stated loosely. Quote Time 1981's exact claim with attribution.

## Best hook facts (verified)

1. Two men were gunned down at 2:30 on a weekday afternoon in a suburban shopping mall liquor store. Police found the getaway "party supply" van full of guns and vests and called it a "war wagon." [NBC 6, 2019]
2. In 1981 Dade County ran out of morgue space and its medical examiner rented a refrigerated food van to hold bodies. It stayed in use until 1988. [TIME 1981; Miami New Times]
3. 621 homicides in Dade County in 1981, the all-time record. [Miami New Times, citing the Medical Examiner]
4. An IRS veteran said that in 1978-79 "the entire currency surplus of the United States was attributed to south Florida." [PBS Frontline]
5. Miami's Fed branch sat on a cash surplus of about $5 billion, and Time said 70% of the cocaine and marijuana entering the US came through South Florida. [TIME, 23 Nov 1981]
6. In about six months, about 1,700 boats carried about 125,000 Cubans to Florida, 86,488 of them in May 1980 alone. [NARA]
7. A Miami Beach commissioner in 1980: "An absolute war is being fought in our streets at night." [TIME, 24 Nov 1980]
8. The President of the United States put his Vice President in charge of fighting crime in one region: South Florida, 28 Jan 1982. [Reagan Library]
9. Eight FBI agents against two robbers. Under five minutes, about 145 shots, 2 agents dead and 5 wounded: one of the deadliest days in FBI history. [FBI.gov; Wikipedia/FBI report]
10. One 9 mm bullet stopped short of the gunman's heart. He fought on, and the FBI rebuilt its guns and training: 10 mm, then the .40 S&W, which police used for decades. [Gun Digest, Mireles; Wikipedia]
11. Bonus: Rockstar's founder said Miami Vice, more than Scarface, was "the strongest influence of all" on Vice City. GTA VI went back to "Leonida," and its first trailer drew about 90 million views in 24 hours. [Edge via GamenGuide; Guinness]
