## 1. Katzenberg at Disney, 1984-1994

- Katzenberg was chairman of Walt Disney Studios from 1984 to 1994, under chairman/CEO Michael Eisner and president/COO Frank Wells. [Wikipedia "Jeffrey Katzenberg"]
- Renaissance films under his watch: *The Little Mermaid* (Nov 1989), *Beauty and the Beast* (Nov 1991), *Aladdin* (Nov 1992), *The Lion King* (limited 15 Jun, wide 24 Jun 1994). [Wikipedia film pages]
- 1993: he discussed with Eisner becoming president (Wells to vice chairman). Per Katzenberg, Eisner assured him he would get the job if Wells left. [WP citing The Hollywood Reporter, Kim Masters, 9 Apr 2014]
- Eisner on the promise, 1994: Vanity Fair reported that Katzenberg says Eisner promised him Wells's job; Eisner: "Maybe he did." [Vanity Fair, Nov 1994, "A Mouse Divided" — https://vanityfair.azurewebsites.net/article/1994/11/a-mouse-divided] (mirror URL; check vanityfair.com archive before quoting on screen)

## 2. Frank Wells — Easter Sunday, 3 April 1994

- Wells, Disney's president and COO, was killed in a helicopter crash on 3 April 1994 returning from a heli-skiing trip in Nevada's Ruby Mountains, near Lamoille, Elko County. [Wikipedia "Frank Wells"; D23 "Frank G. Wells dies in helicopter accident" — https://d23.com/this-day/frank-g-wells-dies-in-helicopter-accident-2/]
- 3 April 1994 was Easter Sunday (computus: Easter 1994 = 3 Apr).
- Also killed: passenger Beverly Johnson, 46 (a woman: do NOT show any image of her) and pilot Dave Walton; two survivors were rescued. [Deseret News, 5 Apr 1994 — https://www.deseret.com/1994/4/5/19101188/pilot-praised-for-daring-rescue-of-helicopter-crash-survivors/]
- Cause: NTSB found snow in the engine caused a flameout. [Deseret News, 2 Jul 1995 — https://www.deseret.com/1995/7/2/19180516/snow-in-engine-blamed-for-crash/]
- Clint Eastwood was on the trip in a separate helicopter. [Deseret/WP] (VERIFY before saying)
- Distance Ruby Mountains → Burbank: ~480-490 miles straight line (coordinates Lamoille 40.7N 115.5W; Burbank 34.2N 118.3W). Say "nearly five hundred miles".

## 3. Eisner says no — April to August 1994

- After Wells died, Eisner took on Wells's duties himself instead of promoting Katzenberg. [WP citing THR 9 Apr 2014]
- Eisner's later explanation: Roy E. Disney disliked Katzenberg and threatened a proxy fight if he were promoted. [WP citing THR, "Michael Eisner on Former Disney Colleagues…", 27 Jul 2016]
- Board member Stanley Gold said Katzenberg had been brought low by "his ego and almost pathological need to be important." [WP citing THR 9 Apr 2014]
- July 1994: Eisner had emergency quadruple-bypass surgery at Cedars-Sinai; press said it put pressure on him to fill the No. 2 job. [Deseret News, 18 Jul 1994 — https://www.deseret.com/1994/7/18/19120439/bypass-raises-concern-about-eisner-disney/]
- **Wednesday 24 August 1994:** Katzenberg, 43, resigned. His line to AP: "the only plan I've made is to take my family to Disney World next week." Eisner said Katzenberg would have stayed if he'd got Wells's job, "but it was not a job that suited his talents." David Geffen: Katzenberg "wants to move on and run his own company." [AP, Burbank, 24 Aug 1994, via Arizona Daily Wildcat 25 Aug 1994 — https://wc.arizona.edu/papers/old-wildcats/fall94/August/August25,1994/15_1_m.html]
- Joe Roth replaced him as head of the film studio. [same AP]

## 4. DreamWorks SKG — 12 October 1994

- Founded 12 Oct 1994 by Spielberg, Katzenberg and Geffen. [Wikipedia "DreamWorks Pictures"; TheWrap, Jeff Sneider, 27 Apr 2016 — https://www.thewrap.com/dreamworks-animation-history-from-antz-to-shrek-to-comcast]
- Money: $33M from each founder; Paul Allen $500M; CJ Group's Miky Lee $300M (11% stake); founders together held 72%. MCA (Universal) bought 2% for $54M in 1995. [WP "DreamWorks Pictures", citing its refs 7-10, 27] (Allen's % not given; don't say a number)
- DreamWorks Interactive (with Microsoft) made Medal of Honor (1999); EA bought the studio 24 Feb 2000. [WP "DreamWorks Pictures"]
- Paul Allen later sat on the DreamWorks Animation board (director nominee, IPO prospectus 2004). [424B4, 28 Oct 2004]

## 5. Katzenberg v. Disney — the bonus lawsuit

- Filed spring 1996 over his contractual incentive bonus. [Salon, Nikki Finke, "When Moguls Attack!", 19 May 1999 — https://www.salon.com/1999/05/19/eisner/]
- Nov 1997 partial settlement; Disney paid ~$117M, rest to trial. [Orlando Weekly / Daily Journal summaries via search; VERIFY]
- May 1999 trial (damages phase). Under questioning by Katzenberg's lawyer Bert Fields, **Eisner conceded he had said of Katzenberg, "I think I hate the little midget."** Eisner: if he said it, "it was probably out of humor"; he said he did not hate Katzenberg. Height: Katzenberg 5'4", Eisner 6'3". [Salon, 19 May 1999]
- July 1999: settled, terms confidential. LA Times (8 Jul 1999) analysts estimated his total take at $250-275M. [Sound & Vision citing LA Times — https://www.soundandvision.com/content/disney-settles-katzenberg-dispute-will-co-finance-films-mgm; E! Online — https://www.eonline.com/news/38408/katzenberg-disney-settle]
- USA Today's 2005 review of *DisneyWar* gives $295M. **DISPUTED**: say "an estimated quarter of a billion dollars".

## 6. PDI and Antz vs A Bug's Life (1995-1998)

- 1995: DreamWorks took 40% of Pacific Data Images (PDI, LLC); 90% in 2000 (PDI/DreamWorks). [WP "DreamWorks Animation"]
- Toy Story released 22 Nov 1995. [WP]
- Lasseter's account: in Oct 1995 he and Andrew Stanton described *A Bug's Life* to Katzenberg; Lasseter later: "Jeffrey kept asking questions about when it would be released." When Antz surfaced he felt betrayed. Jobs called a Katzenberg offer about dates "a blatant extortion attempt". Katzenberg's side: Antz came from a 1991 pitch by Tim Johnson; he denied offering a date swap. [WP "Antz", sourced to David A. Price, *The Pixar Touch* (2008) and LA Times 1998]
- Disney had dated A Bug's Life against DreamWorks' planned Nov 1998 Prince of Egypt; Antz was moved up from March 1999 to October 1998. [WP "Antz"]
- Antz: 2 Oct 1998; $17.2M opening; $90.8M domestic; $171.8M worldwide. A Bug's Life: 25 Nov 1998; ~$363M worldwide. [WP; Box Office Mojo]
- Jobs: "The bad guys rarely win." DreamWorks' Terry Press: "Steve Jobs should take a pill." [WP "Antz", citing LA Times 1998] (Terry Press is a woman: quote only, no image.)

## 7. Prince of Egypt, El Dorado, Shrek (1998-2002)

- *The Prince of Egypt*: 18 Dec 1998, ~$218.6M; Oscar for Best Original Song "When You Believe" (March 1999). [WP; TheWrap timeline]
- *The Road to El Dorado*: 31 Mar 2000, ~$76.4M on ~$95M. [WP]
- *Shrek*: from William Steig's *Shrek!* (1990); released 18 May 2001; ~$484M worldwide. [TheWrap; WP]
- Chris Farley originally voiced Shrek and recorded most of his lines; died 18 Dec 1997, aged 33. Mike Myers took over, then asked to re-record in a Scottish accent; cost widely reported as about $4M (VERIFY; say "millions"). [WP "Shrek"]
- First Best Animated Feature Oscar: 74th Academy Awards, **24 Mar 2002**; Shrek beat *Monsters, Inc.* and *Jimmy Neutron: Boy Genius*. [WP "74th Academy Awards"]
- Farquaad/Eisner: Wikipedia ("Lord Farquaad") calls it **speculation**; no DreamWorks or Katzenberg statement confirms or denies it. Use only "Hollywood whispered… never confirmed". [WP "Lord Farquaad"]

## 9. Shrek 2, the IPO (2004)

- Shrek 2 opened Wed 19 May 2004 (prospectus says 18 May incl. previews). Fri-Sun $108.0M = record animated opening (Finding Nemo $70.2M); 5-day ~$129.0M; 4,163 theatres; single-day record $44.8M. [DWA 424B4 28 Oct 2004 — https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1297401/000095012304012634/y98977b4e424b4.htm ; The Numbers; Irish Examiner May 2004]
- Prospectus: Shrek 2 "achieved the highest domestic box office gross of any animated film" (~$436.7M domestic as of Oct 2004). [424B4]
- Worldwide ~$919.8M original, ~$933M with re-releases; highest-grossing animated film until *Toy Story 3* (2010). [WP "Shrek 2"]
- *The Incredibles*: Nov 2004, ~$631M. [WP]
- IPO: DreamWorks Animation SKG priced 27 Oct 2004, NYSE "DWA", 29,000,000 shares at **$28.00**, $812M offering ($659.75M to the company). [424B4]

## 10. Paramount, the 8% fee, Flushed Away (2005-2006)

- Viacom/Paramount agreed to buy DreamWorks (live action) Dec 2005 (accepted 9 Dec, announced 11 Dec), ~$1.6B incl. ~$400M debt; closed 1 Feb 2006. [WP "DreamWorks Pictures"]
- DWA–Paramount distribution deal effective **31 Jan 2006**: Paramount keeps "a fee of 8.0% of revenue" and recoups all distribution and marketing costs before DWA records revenue; same 8% on home video. Term: 13 films or 31 Dec 2012. [DWA 10-K for 2005 — https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1297401/000119312506051169/d10k.htm ; WP]
- *Madagascar*: 27 May 2005, ~$532M. *The Penguins of Madagascar* TV series on Nickelodeon from 2008. [TheWrap; WP]
- *Flushed Away*: 3 Nov 2006, ~$178M on ~$149M. 10-K: "a write-down of film costs associated with … Flushed Away, of $108.6 million". Aardman split announced after. (2005: $29.0M write-down on Wallace & Gromit too.) [DWA 10-K for 2006 — https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1297401/000119312507041272/d10k.htm]
