## 1. Ted Turner and the MGM library (1963-1986)

- **1963:** Ted Turner, 24, took over Turner Advertising, his father's billboard company, after his father, Robert E. Turner II, died by suicide in March 1963. [Wikipedia "Ted Turner"; analysis file row 1]
- **1 Jun 1980:** CNN launched, the first 24-hour all-news TV network. [AP via ABC7, 6 May 2026]
- **25 Mar 1986:** Turner Broadcasting System bought MGM from Kirk Kerkorian for **$1.5 billion**. [Wikipedia "Turner Entertainment Co." — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turner_Entertainment_Co.]
- **26 Aug 1986:** Turner sold the MGM name, all of United Artists and the Culver City lot back to Kerkorian for about **$300 million**. The reason was Wall Street's concern over the "debt-load of his companies." [same]
- **How long Turner owned MGM:** 25 Mar to 26 Aug 1986, which Wikipedia (citing the Orlando Sentinel, 27 Aug 1986) counts as **"75 days"**. **DISPUTED:** 25 Mar to 26 Aug is about five months on the calendar. "75 days" probably counts from a later step in the deal. Script line: **"He kept the studio for about five months."** Never say "two days". [Wikipedia "History of Cartoon Network" — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Cartoon_Network; Turner Entertainment Co.]
- **What he kept (the real prize):**
  - MGM's film, TV and cartoon library, including Tom and Jerry.
  - Most of the **pre-1950 Warner Bros. library**, which includes Looney Tunes/Merrie Melodies released before Aug 1948.
  - The Fleischer/Famous Studios Popeye cartoons.
  - US/Canadian distribution rights to the RKO library.
  - [Wikipedia "Turner Entertainment Co."]
- **Library size:** often quoted as "about 3,650 films." **VERIFY:** not found in the sources checked. Script line: **"thousands of films."**

## 2. Hanna-Barbera and the launch plan (1991-1992)

- **Sep 1991:** Great American Communications announced it would sell Hanna-Barbera Productions to Turner Broadcasting. [UPI, 3 Sep 1991 — https://www.upi.com/Archives/1991/09/03/Great-American-to-sell-Hanna-Barbera/7715683870400/]
- **29 Oct 1991:** Turner bought Hanna-Barbera, plus most of the pre-1991 Ruby-Spears library, for **$320 million**. It outbid MCA and Hallmark Cards. [Wikipedia "Turner Entertainment Co." and "History of Cartoon Network", citing LA Times 29 Oct 1991 and NYT 30 Oct 1991; MeTV "When Ted Turner bought Hanna-Barbera" — https://metv.com/stories/when-ted-turner-bought-hanna-barbera]
  - **VERIFY:** one source says the Apollo investment group helped finance it. Don't say it.
- **Feb 1992:** UPI reported that, after the $320M deal, Turner closed the two Hanna-Barbera retail stores in Southern California. This is a small money detail showing the purchase was about the library, not the merchandise shops. [UPI, 26 Feb 1992 — https://www.upi.com/Archives/1992/02/26/Turner-closes-Hanna-Barbera-stores/8216699080400/]
- **18 Feb 1992:** Turner announced the channel. [NYT, Bill Carter, 19 Feb 1992, cited by Wikipedia "Cartoon Network" — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cartoon_Network]
- **12 Mar 1992:** the company was founded. [same]
- **The founders (men, for on-screen use):** William Hanna (1910-2001) and Joseph Barbera (1911-2006) founded Hanna-Barbera in 1957. Their studio was the core of the new channel's library. [Wikipedia "Hanna-Barbera"]

## 3. Launch: Thursday 1 Oct 1992

- **What it was:** the first 24-hour single-genre cable channel built around animation. [Wikipedia "Cartoon Network"]
- **Opening:** the launch played the finale of Tchaikovsky's *1812 Overture*, then a special, *Droopy's Guide to the Cartoon Network*. The first cartoon shown was *Rhapsody Rabbit*. [Wikipedia "History of Cartoon Network"; fan wiki for the overture]
- **Launch reach:** carried by **233 cable systems, about 2 million subscribers**. [Wikipedia "History of Cartoon Network", citing Variety, 9 Feb 1993]
- **Launch time:** **DISPUTED:** one fan wiki says noon ET, and no primary source was found. Don't say a clock time. Say "Thursday, October 1, 1992."
- **Library at launch:** "an 8,500-hour cartoon library" comes from a fan wiki only (**VERIFY**). Safe line: **"thousands of hours of old cartoons."**
- **Growth:** by 1994 it was the 5th most widely distributed US cable network. It went from 38 million to 54 million households between 1997 and 1998. [Wikipedia "History of Cartoon Network", citing Advertising Age, 30 Nov 1998]
- **HQ:** the Turner campus at 1050 Techwood Drive NW, Atlanta. [Wikipedia "Cartoon Network" infobox]
- **First president:** Betty Cohen, 1992 to 18 Jun 2001. She left after creative disagreements with Jamie Kellner. **Name only, no photo** (channel rule). [Wikipedia, citing WSJ 18 Jun 2001]

## 6. The Boston bomb scare: Wednesday 31 Jan 2007

All facts in this chapter are from [Wikipedia "2007 Boston Mooninite panic" — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2007_Boston_Mooninite_panic] unless another source is given.

**The campaign**
- **What the devices were:** battery-powered LED light boards showing a cartoon character (a "Mooninite") from Adult Swim's *Aqua Teen Hunger Force*. They were guerrilla marketing for the show's movie, run by the agency **Interference, Inc.** for Turner.
- **Ten cities:** Boston, New York, Los Angeles, Chicago, Atlanta, Seattle, Portland, Austin, San Francisco and Philadelphia. In some of them the devices had been up for about two weeks.
- **In Boston:** 40 devices were shipped. 20 were placed in mid-January and 18 more on 29 Jan. That gives **38 placed**, an inference from the two counts (**VERIFY**; many press reports say "38").

**The day, hour by hour**
- **8:05 a.m.:** a civilian reports a device on a stanchion under I-93, above Sullivan Square station, to an MBTA officer.
- **9 a.m.:** the MBTA asks the Boston Police bomb squad for help.
- **Just after 10 a.m.:** the bomb squad destroys it with a water charge. Northbound I-93 reopens about 10:05.
- **12:54 p.m.:** a second device is reported at Stuart and Charles Streets.
- **1:11 p.m.:** State Police call in the squad for devices under the **Longfellow** and **Boston University** bridges. Both bridges are closed, and the Coast Guard stops boat traffic on the Charles River.
- **2-3 p.m.:** a police analyst recognises the cartoon character, and police conclude it is a publicity stunt.
- **About 4:30 p.m.:** Turner issues a statement saying it "regretted the devices being mistaken for danger."
- **Evening:** Peter Berdovsky and Sean Stevens, the two men paid **$300 each** to put the signs up, are arrested.

**Fallout**
- **Mayor Thomas Menino:** "It is outrageous, in a post 9/11 world, that a company would use this type of marketing scheme." [Fox News/AP, cited by Wikipedia]
- **5 Feb 2007:** Turner and Interference agreed to pay **$2 million**: $1M to the Boston Police Department and $1M to the Department of Homeland Security.
- **Fri 9 Feb 2007:** **Jim Samples resigned.** In a staff memo he said he stepped down "in recognition of the gravity of the situation that occurred under my watch." [Wikipedia; CNNMoney 9 Feb 2007 — https://money.cnn.com/2007/02/09/news/newsmakers/cartoon_network/; Broadcasting & Cable — https://www.nexttv.com/news/cartoon-network-chief-resigns-over-marketing-blunder-boston-81911]
- **11 May 2007:** charges against Berdovsky and Stevens were dropped in exchange for community service (80 and 60 hours) and a public apology.
- ***Aqua Teen Hunger Force* was NOT cancelled.** Only an episode titled "Boston", made in response, was pulled.
- **2 May 2007:** Stuart Snyder was named president. He was removed on 6 Mar 2014. [Wikipedia, citing Broadcasting & Cable and Variety]

## 8. The 2010s revival: get the origins right

**Adventure Time** (Pendleton Ward) [Wikipedia "Adventure Time" — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adventure_Time]
- **Pilot:** a 7-minute short made by **Frederator Studios**, finished early 2006.
- **11 Jan 2007:** the pilot aired on **Nicktoons Network**. It went viral online, especially after a *Random! Cartoons* rebroadcast on 7 Dec 2008.
- **Nicktoons passed** on a series. Frederator took it elsewhere.
- **CN's condition:** it would develop the series if Ward proved the pilot "wasn't a one-hit wonder." CN executive Rob Sorcher was key.
- **Aug 2008:** CN approved season 1.
- **Mon 5 Apr 2010:** premiere.
- **3 Sep 2018:** finale after 10 seasons and 283 episodes. It won 8 Primetime Emmys and a Peabody.
- **CORRECTION:** Adventure Time did **not** come out of the Cartoonstitute. It began at Nickelodeon's door.

**Regular Show** (J.G. Quintel) [Wikipedia "Regular Show" — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regular_Show]
- **Roots:** CalArts student films, *The Naïve Man from Lolliland* (2005) and *2 in the AM PM* (2006).
- **The Cartoonstitute:** Quintel pitched through CN's **Cartoonstitute** project, announced Apr 2008.
- **Mon 6 Sep 2010:** premiere.
- **16 Jan 2017:** finale after 8 seasons and 244 episodes.
- **So** Regular Show is the one that came out of the Cartoonstitute.

**Story beat (both true):** CN's 2010 comeback was built partly on a pilot Nickelodeon passed on.

## DO-NOT-SAY list

1. "Turner owned MGM for two days" / "74 days" as fact. Say "about five months."
2. "Ted Turner invented Adult Swim" or "Jim Samples created Adult Swim." It was Mike Lazzo's Williams Street team.
3. "Family Guy is an Adult Swim show." It is a Fox show.
4. "The Boston scare was in 2006" or "Aqua Teen Hunger Force got cancelled." It was 31 Jan 2007, and the show ran on.
5. "They were bombs" or "fake bombs." They were LED advertising signs that police treated as possible bombs. Don't call it a "hoax"; charges were dropped.
6. "Turner paid $2 million to Boston." Turner **and Interference** paid $1M to Boston PD and $1M to DHS.
7. "Jim Samples was fired." He resigned (some reports say under pressure).
8. "Adventure Time came out of the Cartoonstitute." See ch. 8.
9. "CN Real was a ratings disaster from day one." Early reports showed gains. Say "it didn't last a year."
10. "Cartoon Network shut down" (2024) or "Cartoon Network is dead." The channel is still on air (Nov 2026); the studio was merged into WB Animation.
11. "Zaslav deleted shows for tax write-offs" as fact. Say "reported" / "widely seen as."
12. "Cartoon Network merged with Nickelodeon." They share an owner and an animation unit. The channels are not merged or renamed.
13. Launch clock time ("7 p.m.", "noon"). Unverified.
14. "8,500 cartoons" or "3,650 films" in the library. Unverified counts. Say "thousands."
15. "Turner lost $7 billion." Say "about $8 billion."
16. "First 24-hour cartoon channel in the world" stated loosely. Say "the first 24-hour channel built only on cartoons" (Wikipedia: first 24-hour single-genre animation channel).
17. "CN was No. 1, far ahead of Nickelodeon" (2001 or 2011). Overstated; no ratings source checked.
18. The Oct 2026 "gigaleak", and the 21 Sep 2026 Nick announcement date. Wikipedia only.
19. Any detail about Ted Turner's illness beyond "Lewy body dementia, disclosed in 2018."

## Best hook facts (verified)

1. In 1986 Ted Turner paid $1.5 billion for MGM, sold most of it back within months, and kept the films and cartoons. [Wikipedia / Turner Entertainment]
2. $320 million in 1991 for Hanna-Barbera, outbidding MCA and Hallmark. [LA Times/NYT via Wikipedia]
