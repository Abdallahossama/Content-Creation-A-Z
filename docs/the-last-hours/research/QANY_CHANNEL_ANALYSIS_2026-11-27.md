# Omar Qany (@QANY) deep dive, and how to translate his formula for US viewers (27 Nov 2026)

Research agent, 27 Nov 2026. Sources: yt-dlp metadata only (flat channel lists, per-video metadata, Arabic auto-captions for 8 videos, 8 thumbnail jpgs; no video files), plus 21 English YouTube searches for market evidence. Numbers are as of 27 Nov 2026. "Views/sub" uses today's 200K subs, so older videos (posted when the channel was smaller) look relatively better than they were at the time.

## 0. The one-paragraph answer

Qany is not a gaming or true-crime channel. He is a **nostalgia "franchise biography" channel**: "N Years of [thing your whole generation grew up with] | The Full Story". He pivoted on 4 Dec 2025 from music remixes/YTP (5-55K views) to these documentaries and went to ~200K subs in under a year: **31 long videos, 14.6M views, median 455K per video, floor 160K, best 1.44M (Detective Conan)**. The engine is (1) a famous name everybody already loves, (2) a big "N Years of" number + "The Full Story" promise, (3) a chronological journey era-by-era "from the top to the bottom (and back)", (4) told by a friend with opinions, not a narrator, (5) one consistent thumbnail template: his own face in a straw hat in the centre, the franchise's characters around him, the franchise logo, the words "The Full Story". For The Last Hours, copy the **structure and packaging**, not the topic family: take famous American legends/cases/institutions that US viewers "grew up with" (from movies, school, TV) and give them the "N Years of X: The Full File" treatment.

### 2.1 "30 Years of Detective Conan | From Spacetoon to the truth about the Black Organization... The Full Story" (pYC40FcJkCo, 29 Aug 2026, 44:49, 1,436,539 views, 7.2x subs)
- **Topic**: manga/anime history + full lore recap of a show every Arab kid saw dubbed on Spacetoon.
- **Hook (0:00-0:45)**: superlative + numbers ("one of the most famous anime in history, especially here in the Arab world, 1,210 episodes, 27 films, more episodes than One Piece") -> shared memory ("we all watched it as kids and innocently waited for the ending, when Conan returns to normal") -> friend jab ("Buddy, it's 2026 and that still hasn't happened") -> the viewer's question ("so what's been going on all these years?") -> promise ("the whole history, how it started as a simple manga, and the main timeline of the Black Organization, especially since a huge secret was just revealed") -> CTA "like, share, subscribe, let's go" at ~0:40.
- **Structure**: 1992 origin (an editor jealous of a rival mystery manga orders one; the creator resists, then invents the "shrunk detective" twist in two weeks; trivia: glasses from Clark Kent, name from Conan Doyle) -> episode-1 retelling as a story (minute 9-12) -> the Black Organization arc told like a thriller (minute 24-26: Akai's "death", told shot by shot) -> latest reveal -> outro.
- **Tone devices**: correcting himself on name pronunciation as a gag ("didn't you say you'd say Sinichi? - my choice"), "something that amazed me", notes on scenes Spacetoon censored.
- **Ending**: thanks to channel members, bye. Short.
- **Why it won**: biggest shared-childhood IP in the region + an unresolved 30-year mystery (when will it end?) + recent news peg. A "mystery that never ends" is exactly our genre.

### 2.9 Patterns across the 8 (the "Qany script engine")
1. **Hook in 30-45 s, always the same 5 beats**: superlative about a famous thing -> shared memory ("we all...") -> twist/question (why it ended / why it fell / it never ended) -> scope promise ("from the first ... to today", "every version") -> "before we start, as usual, like, share, subscribe - yalla beena".
2. **Origin story first, told as a human drama** (jealous editor, revenge-driven executive, poor writer denied credit, founder tragedy). Then strict chronology, one chapter per era/version/season.
3. **"From the top to the bottom (and back)"**: the arc is a rise-and-fall; the fall (reboots, flops, sale) becomes the emotional climax; the end returns to the hook's question.
4. **Friend voice**: "honestly" / "for me" / jokes every 1-2 minutes, light insults to bad entries, rival jabs, myth-busting rumours, reacting to fresh news ("a trailer dropped Monday").
5. **CTA placement**: one at 0:20-0:45 (after the promise, before the story), one at the very end (+ "watch the previous episodes and wait for the next ones" + thanks to channel members). No mid-video asks seen in the captions.
6. **Ending**: a moral or verdict + a comment question ("who's your favourite...?") + thanks for watching to the end.
7. **Numbers everywhere**: years in titles, episode counts, sales, budgets, dates.
8. **The avatar**: it is not an illustration. It is **his own real face** (a stylised/composited photo) wearing **a Luffy-style straw hat** (One Piece reference = instant anime-fan signal). He is the centre of 7 of 8 top thumbnails, changing expression/prop/costume per topic: thinking pose (Conan, Adventure Time), revolver + Hawaiian shirt (GTA), eye-black war paint (Batman), grin + glowing watch (Ben 10), frown (Pixar). The channel logo bug "OMAR QANY" with a tiny straw hat sits top-centre on every thumbnail.
9. **Thumbnail template**: host centre, 3-6 iconic characters/people around him at the edges, saturated background in the franchise's palette, the franchise's real logo bottom-centre, "القصة الكاملة" (The Full Story) split left and right of the logo. No question text, no arrows, no red circles. Every thumbnail is the same layout = instant series recognition in the feed.

**Packaging formula**
- Title: `N Years of [FAMOUS NAME] | [one hook clause: superlative, rise-and-fall, or secret]... The Full Story`. Famous name in the first 3 words, the big number first, the arc in the subtitle.
- Thumbnail: same template every time (see 2.9). Recognisability > novelty.
- Cadence: one long video every 7-14 days (mean 11.5). No Shorts strategy.
- Length sweet spot: 25-50 min; no penalty for 50+.
- News pegs boost: GTA (launch week) 37K/day, Gumball (new season/news) 16K/day, Conan (recent reveal) 16K/day vs 2-4K/day for evergreen ones.

## 5. Risks and what not to copy

**Do not copy**
- His topics (cartoons, anime, game franchises) or his scripts. Translating his narration = plagiarism, and the facts are not ours to vouch for.
- His visual method: his videos run on franchise footage, dubbed theme songs and official character art. On an English channel this means Content ID claims/strikes and no monetisation; it also breaks our copyright-safe rule. Game footage (GTA) and film clips are out; character art and logos of franchises are trademark/copyright risks in thumbnails.
- His personal jabs at named real people (e.g. a writer insulted in the Adventure Time outro). For true crime, opinions only about facts and institutions; never about victims or families.
- The host-as-real-face part: his face in a straw hat is the brand. Noah is illustrated, so parasocial pull is weaker; that is fine (the CN video without his face is his 2nd best), but keep Noah identical and centred every time so the template does the recognition work.

**What differs for US audiences**
- **Competition**: his Arabic niche has almost no long-form competition; ours has heavyweights on the exact topics: LEMMiNO (~5.9M subs; Kennedy 15.6M, Bermuda 10.3M), fern (~5.3M subs; Zodiac 5.4M, Secret Service 9.2M/22.1M), Internet Historian (~4M subs, internet-culture histories), NatGeo/HISTORY/PBS full episodes (3-10M each), BuzzFeed Unsolved (11-15M), Biographics, plus "fall asleep to the entire story" multi-hour channels. Company/franchise-history channels such as Company Man exist too (not verified in this research). Our edge must be the recurring host + hour-by-hour "last hours" spine + the "N years, full file" promise, not "first to cover it".
- **The nostalgia lever is weaker for true crime**: Qany's audience loves the subject; US viewers are curious about ours. So the hook must carry more: open on the ending (the last hours), then promise the full N-year timeline.
- **RPM**: US/Canada history/true crime pays far more than Egyptian-Arabic views (rough industry ranges, not measured: Egypt well under $1 RPM vs US documentary-style often several dollars), so even 10% of his views would out-earn him. But US viewers compare against high production values.
- **Searchability**: his titles are browse/nostalgia-driven; ours can also win search ("Zodiac Killer full story", "Alcatraz history"), so put the searched name first.
- **AI voice + mass production**: YouTube's "inauthentic/mass-produced content" rules target repetitive templated uploads. Keep real research, a consistent human-led persona and an own-voice clone, varied scripts; don't publish faster than research allows (he does 1 per 7-14 days at 25-55 min).
- **Legal**: living people and open cases (Lockerbie trial, JFK, Hoffa) need "alleged" language; no naming of unproven suspects as perpetrators.
