## 0. How he makes it (the format at a glance)

- **Spine:** a chronological, film-by-film walk through all 29+ Pixar features. Each film gets the same mini-template: **premise question -> theme he likes -> one technical breakthrough -> box-office number + Oscar -> his verdict**. There are about 25 of these blocks, each 1-3 min long.
- **Frame story:** he opens on the 2025 *Elio* Instagram controversy ("what brought Pixar this low?") and closes the loop at 47:30 ("the problem is the new staff, not the audience").
- **Three acts:** (1) Rise, 1974-1995: underdog tech lab, Jobs, Toy Story. (2) Golden age, 1995-2010: 11 hits in a row, the Disney deal. (3) Decline, 2011-2026: sequels, Lasseter's exit, Disney+, Elio, Toy Story 5.
- **On-screen language** (seen on every sheet):
  - Dark navy grid background, his "QANY" logo top-left, a small "PIXAR" bug top-right.
  - Films shown inside a framed box, or full-bleed.
  - Big white Arabic kinetic-type cards (one phrase each) roughly every 30-60 s.
  - A big year number as a chapter card (1974, 1979, 1995, March 2020).
  - **His avatar:** a cut-out head of a curly-haired young man in a straw hat, floating on black. It appears ~20 times as a "reaction" beat for jokes and opinions.
  - The box-office beat is always the same: **poster + green Arabic number + flying $100 bills + a blue stock-chart line.**
  - Real-people photos are mostly press shots and studio stills, some black and white.
- **Pacing:** a visual change every ~5-10 s, constant film footage, and very little "empty" talking. He cuts in the film's actual dialogue in the Egyptian dub as stingers (6:30, 8:00, 9:00, 16:00, 19:30, 26:00, 31:00).
- **Ending:** a talking-head-style outro card (avatar holding the Luxo-style lamp) plus a subscribe pop-up. Almost no end teaser.

## 2. Hook, ending, arc, turning points, asides

### Hook (0:00-0:60)
- **Present-day scandal first.** He opens on "Pixar blamed its fans on Instagram (2025)", which he calls "a first in Pixar history". Then the big question: "what brought Pixar this low?" Then a quick reaction clip.
- **Promise:** "the whole history, film by film, plus the tech it gave cinema". Then the subscribe ask, then a cold jump to "1974".
- **Why it works:** a *mystery about a fall* (decline story) wrapped around a *nostalgia catalogue*. The viewer stays for both "what went wrong" and "my childhood films".
- **Weak points to beat:**
  - The subscribe ask comes at 0:30, before any payoff.
  - The hook claim is shaky: it was Pixar's official account (see Section 3), but he later says "one of Pixar's employees". He is inconsistent.

### Ending (52:30-54:30)
- A moral, not a twist: "tech without soul is worthless".
- Then an open question ("how do they fix it? I don't know").
- Then a nostalgic sign-off and a subscribe ask. **Our channel rule:** after the payoff, do a polite spoken like/subscribe plus "whose last hours next?" at the very end. His ending is soft and has no next-video tease. We should end on a stronger closing image and an end-screen tease.

### Story arc
1. **Underdogs, 1974-1991:** scientists with no product, Lasseter fired by Disney, Lucas selling, Jobs bleeding cash.
2. **Breakthrough, 1991-1995:** a near-cancellation (Black Friday), *Toy Story*, the IPO.
3. **Dominance, 1995-2010:** betrayal (*Antz*), near-disaster (the TS2 deletion), 11 straight hits, Disney forced to buy Pixar for $7.4B.
4. **Drift, 2011-2018:** sequels, Lasseter's attention split, first flop, #MeToo exit.
5. **Crisis, 2018-2025:** Spider-Verse makes the look dated, COVID, Disney+ dumping, *Lightyear*, *Elio*.
6. **Uneasy present, 2026:** *Toy Story 5* makes a billion but is called "creatively bankrupt".

### Turning points he leans on
- Lasseter fired from Disney (1983).
- Jobs' purchase (1986).
- The 1991 Disney deal.
- The *Toy Story* "Black Friday" rewrite (1993).
- The IPO and the 50/50 renegotiation (1995-97).
- *Antz* betrayal (1998).
- The TS2 file deletion (1998).
- The Eisner exit and Iger's phone call (2005).
- The $7.4B acquisition (2006).
- The Lasseter exit (2017-18).
- *Spider-Verse* (2018).
- COVID and Disney+ (2020-22).
- *Elio* backlash (2025).

### His opinions and asides (summarised, many will not suit a US audience)
- Lasseter: "a hell of a comeback".
- "Entertainment always pushes science forward."
- Toy Story's message is the meaning of life: "stay with me to the end".
- "I cried as a kid at Buzz falling" and at *Up* and *Coco*.
- Katzenberg: the rivalry was healthy.
- Pixar at the Oscars = "Messi at the Ballon d'Or".
- *Cars 2* is "really bad". He dislikes *Brave*, *Incredibles 2*, *Turning Red*, *Elemental* ("ugly"), *Luca* ("doesn't befit Pixar").
- He cooked ratatouille himself.
- "Finding Nemo came out in my birth year" (2003).
- *Soul* "conspiracy": Black leads turned into something else.
- **Culture-war asides (do NOT carry over):**
  - "Gay agenda" remarks on *Luca*, *Lightyear* and *Elio*.
  - Calls a same-sex moment "disgusting".
  - "Tired of female leads".
  - "Creativity only comes from suffering" (glorifying crunch).
  - "New employees have no soul".
- **Running jokes:**
  - "Now I understand why it flopped" (Good Dinosaur).
  - "I'm so tired" (the final line).
  - "Something that would give me a heart attack" (the TS2 deletion).

### Origins
- **1974, Catmull "starts it all".** OK with context. In 1974 Catmull finished his Utah PhD (texture mapping, z-buffer) and joined NYIT's Computer Graphics Lab. Pixar itself traces to Lucasfilm in 1979. *Source: Wikipedia "Edwin Catmull".*
- **1979, Lucas hires Catmull to head the Lucasfilm computer division.** OK. The unit was later called the "Graphics Group". "47 years" in his title = 1979 to 2026. *Wikipedia "Pixar".*
- **Lasseter joins in 1984 after Disney fired him; met Catmull at a 1983 conference.** OK with nuance. He was fired from Disney in 1983 over a CG project, and the "faster or cheaper" exchange is his own anecdote. He joined Lucasfilm late 1983/early 1984. *Wikipedia "John Lasseter".* His dialogue is a dramatised paraphrase.
- ***The Adventures of André & Wally B.* (1984).** OK. It pioneered motion blur and squash-and-stretch in CG. "First CG film with character animation" is an overstatement, so say "a landmark".
- ***Young Sherlock Holmes* stained-glass knight (1985), Lasseter + Bill Reeves.** OK. Made with ILM; the first fully CG character in a feature.
- **Pixar Image Computer sold to medical and government users; too expensive.** OK. It cost about $135,000 per unit.
- **Feb 1986: Jobs pays $5M to Lucas plus $5M capital (total $10M).** OK. The deal closed 3 Feb 1986. *Wikipedia "Pixar"; 247wallst 25 Oct 2026 ("he bet $10 million on a graphics unit he bought from George Lucas").*
- **Named after its computer; the logo looked like the machine.** OK on the name. The "logo copies the computer" detail is weak: the early cube logo resembled the machine, but VERIFY before saying it.
- ***Luxo Jr.* (1986) = first CG film nominated for an Oscar; lost.** OK.
- ***Tin Toy* "1989" wins the Oscar.** FIX: made in 1988, won the Oscar at the March 1989 ceremony. It was the first CG film to win an Oscar.
- **RenderMan used on *Jurassic Park*.** OK (ILM, 1993). It was also used on *Terminator 2* and many others.
- **Jobs losing "$1M a year".** ? Commonly cited: Jobs put in about $50M total before Toy Story and nearly sold the company. Prefer "tens of millions of his own money".
- **CG ads for Tropicana and Trident; Docter and Stanton hired.** OK (Pixar also made Listerine and Life Savers spots). Stanton joined in 1990, Docter in 1990.
- **Disney tried three times to rehire Lasseter.** ? Lasseter's own anecdote (he turned down Katzenberg). Keep it as "he says".
- **1991: 3-picture deal, Disney funds/distributes/markets, Pixar creative.** OK. May 1991, roughly $26M.

### Toy Story (1995)
- **Katzenberg's "edgy" notes made Woody a jerk; Disney halted production; two-week rewrite.** OK. "Black Friday", 19 Nov 1993. *Wikipedia "Toy Story".*
- **114,240 frames; 800,000 machine-hours.** OK. *Wikipedia "Toy Story".*
- **Ralph Eggleston kept humans simple.** ? He was the art director; the human limits are well documented, but the attribution to him is soft.
- **$30M budget.** OK.
- **"$362M+ worldwide".** OK-ish. The original run was ~$373M; ~$394M with re-releases.
- **"Highest-grossing film of its year".** FIX/nuance: #1 of 1995 in the US ($191.8M). Worldwide it is close with *Die Hard with a Vengeance* ($366M); it only clearly leads with re-releases. Say "No. 1 movie of 1995 in America".
- **First animated film nominated for Original Screenplay; plus Score and Song.** OK. Lasseter also got a Special Achievement Oscar, which he omits.
- **IPO at $22/share, biggest IPO of the year.** OK. 29 Nov 1995. Shares closed ~$39 on day one; Jobs' stake was worth ~$1.2B. "Biggest IPO of 1995" is widely reported. Our US angle: the Pixar S-1/prospectus on SEC EDGAR.
- **After the IPO, Pixar renegotiated to 50/50.** OK. Feb 1997, a 5-picture deal with costs and profits split 50/50. Disney kept the character rights; he implies that too.

### 1998-2004
- ***A Bug's Life*: Aesop + *Seven Samurai*; $363M; "more than double *Antz*".** OK ($363.3M vs *Antz* $171.8M).
- **Katzenberg "stole" the ant idea.** ? CONTESTED: Lasseter and Jobs' account, denied by Katzenberg. Present it as "Pixar believed..." In reality *Antz* came out on 2 Oct 1998 and *A Bug's Life* on 25 Nov 1998. *Antz* also has a DreamWorks/PDI origin claim.
- **Crowd animation; outtakes in the credits.** OK.
- **TS2 planned as direct-to-video; upgraded; Disney wanted it in-house.** OK on direct-to-video and the upgrade. The "in-house" part is partly true: Disney's Circle 7 was later formed for sequels in 2004-05. He blurs two eras. VERIFY.
- **1998 accidental deletion (`rm -rf`), ~90% of the files.** OK. 90% is the usual figure, as told by Oren Jacob and Galyn Susman.
- **Backup at the technical director's home (new-baby work-from-home).** OK. The person is Galyn Susman, a woman: tell the story, don't show her.
- **Rebuilt in 9 months; Nov 1999 date fixed; 100-hour weeks.** OK. It caused repetitive-strain injuries; mention that honestly.
- **TS2: $511M; Golden Globe Musical/Comedy.** OK. $497M original run, ~$511M with re-release.
- ***Monsters, Inc.*: Nov 2001, $528M, $115M budget; lost the first Animated Feature Oscar to *Shrek*; won Song for "If I Didn't Have You".** OK. $577M with the 2012 3D re-release. The Oscar category was introduced for the 2001 films.
- **Sulley's fur, the "Fizt" simulator.** OK. About 2.3 million hairs.
- ***Finding Nemo*: May 2003, $941M, then highest-grossing animated film, first Animated Feature Oscar, 40M+ DVDs.** OK with nuance. $871M original run; ~$940M after the 2012 3D re-release. It was the record holder at the time on its original run. Best-selling DVD at the time.
- **Stanton voiced Crush (lying on a couch) and the seagulls.** OK.
- **Water "too real", toned down.** OK (often-told anecdote).
- ***The Incredibles*: Brad Bird first outside director; "10 years and $500M" warning; $92M budget; $631M; 2 Oscars.** OK. The "10 years / $500M" line comes from Bird's own retelling. Bird is the first director from outside Pixar to direct a Pixar feature.

### Cars and the Disney deal
- **Eisner CEO 21 years from 1984; Katzenberg left; Roy E. Disney revolt; March 2005 announcement; Iger takes over.** OK. Eisner was CEO 1984 to 30 Sep 2005; Iger took over 1 Oct 2005.
- **Hong Kong Disneyland: Iger noticed the only new characters were Pixar's.** OK. Iger tells this in *The Ride of a Lifetime* (2019). The parade was at the opening, Sept 2005.
- ***Cars* = "last independent Pixar film".** FIX. The deal was announced 24 Jan 2006 and closed 5 May 2006. *Cars* was released 9 June 2006, after Pixar was already Disney-owned. Better: "the last film made by Pixar as an independent company".
- ***Cars*: $461M; merch $10B+.** OK with nuance: $462M box office; the ~$10B merchandise is cumulative retail sales within ~5 years (by 2011). It wasn't immediate.
- **$7.4B all-stock acquisition.** OK.
- **Jobs' "49%" became "287 million" shares, ~7%, largest individual shareholder.** FIX. Jobs owned ~50.6% of Pixar. At 2.3 Disney shares per Pixar share he received **~138 million Disney shares**, about 7% (SEC 13D says 6.3% on its basis) and the largest single shareholder. *Sources: SEC Schedule 13D, sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001001039/000119312506103741/dsc13d.htm; CBS News "Steve Jobs' $4.6B in Disney shares go to trust".*
- **"Then he went back to Apple and made the iPhone".** FIX/timeline. Jobs had already returned to Apple in 1997 (interim CEO 1997, CEO 2000). The iPhone was 2007.
- **Catmull president of both studios; Lasseter CCO of both + Imagineering adviser; Pixar keeps its culture.** OK.

### 2007-2019
- ***Ratatouille* $620M + Oscar.** OK ($623.7M).
- ***WALL-E* $521M + Oscar; ~30 min with no dialogue; "IMDb top 100".** OK on money and Oscar. "~30-40 min with almost no dialogue" is fine. IMDb: it is in the Top 250, not a fixed "top 100", so say "IMDb Top 250".
