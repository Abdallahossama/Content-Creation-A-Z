## 0. The video at a glance

- **Real shape:** the title promises a revenge story, but after about 3 minutes of revenge setup the video becomes a **film-by-film, year-by-year review** of DreamWorks Animation (1998 to 2024). Each year is one beat: film, its plot in two lines, his opinion, its box office, and who "won the year" (Disney/Pixar or DreamWorks). The revenge frame comes back only at the turning points (Shrek as a revenge film, Shrek 2 beating The Incredibles, 2013 "beginning of the end", 2015 sale offers, 2016 Comcast sale, the "Disney wins" ending).
- **Arc:** revenge setup (1994) -> shaky start (Antz, El Dorado flop) -> knockout punch (Shrek, first Oscar) -> peak (Shrek 2, Madagascar, Kung Fu Panda, Dragon) -> money bleed (Paramount 8% fee, Flushed Away, Rise of the Guardians, Turbo) -> collapse (2014-15 layoffs, PDI closed, studio shopped around, "Disney said no") -> sold to Comcast for $3.8B (2016) -> quieter "safe" era, ending on The Wild Robot.
- **Voice:** a fan who grew up on these films. About 40% of the runtime is plot recaps and "watch this one / I hate this one". Business facts are thin and a few are wrong (section 3).
- **Look (from the storyboard), same template as his Cartoon Network video:**
  1. **Kinetic text cards**: big white Arabic words with glow on a dark charcoal background with blue blur ("let's go", "under his supervision", "not for kids", "for the first time", "not that easy", "one of the biggest flops", "the film's bad", "because of this year", "very bad"). Year numbers as huge white numerals ("1998", "2003", "2007", "2022"; "2013" in red).
  2. **Money cards**: a poster in the middle with a green number on one side and a red number on the other (El Dorado "95 million" red vs "76 million" green; Antz "171.8 million dollars" in green). Shrek 2 poster with flying $100 bills and "928 million dollars". Red-hearts or green-arrow animated backgrounds for flop vs win.
  3. **His avatar**: a cut-out boy's head in a Luffy straw hat, centred on dark. About 8 times, for every aside ("I know you never heard of it", thinking pose with hand on chin).
  4. **Film clips and posters**: about 65-70% of the runtime is DreamWorks/Disney/Pixar/Illumination/Blue Sky footage, trailers, posters and logo idents.
  5. **Real photos**: Katzenberg (young, B&W at a desk; older, red background with Disney logo; B&W 2010s portrait), a newspaper page "Chairman of Disney Studios Resigns" with "1994", the three founders standing together (blurred first, then revealed under "the founders"), Paul Allen at a desk with "500 million dollars", a B&W storyboard-room photo, Michael Eisner photo next to Lord Farquaad, Messi and Ronaldo side by side, Jerry Seinfeld 1990s portrait, Masayoshi Son on a SoftBank stage, the Comcast logo on a building at night, an Oscars presenter, a Medal of Honor PlayStation box.
- **About 70% of his visuals are copyrighted film footage, posters and logos we cannot use.** The real-people photos and the money/number cards are the parts worth learning from.

## 2. Hook, ending, arc, turning points, asides

### Hook (0:00-0:30)
1. **Recognition list** (Shrek, Kung Fu Panda, Madagascar, Dragon, Spirit) to make every viewer think "I know these".
2. **A misconception to correct:** "you think they're Disney films, but no, and there's a reason".
3. **The full arc in one sentence**, rise to equal Disney, then rock bottom.
4. **Ending spoiler as bait:** "it was offered to Disney, and Disney said no." Strong, because the viewer waits 20 minutes for it.
5. Subscribe ask at 0:20, before any story. (Our rule: no ask in the hook.)

Weakness: the title promises **revenge**, but there is no human scene. Katzenberg's motive is told in 30 seconds and never staged. Wells' death, the Eisner refusal and the "worst nightmare" line are the best material and he rushes them.

### Ending (22:45-23:15)
Moral plus summary: "either you join the giants or they crush you" ties back to the Disney sale. Then "the real winner is the audience". No final image, no last line from Katzenberg. Plain outro with subscribe/like/share.

### Story arc
Setup (1994 feud) -> Act 1 rough start (1998-2000) -> Act 2 triumph (2001 Shrek, 2004 Shrek 2, 2008 KFP, 2010 Dragon) -> Act 3 money bleed (2006 Paramount fee + Flushed Away, 2012 Guardians, 2013 Turbo) -> crash (2014-15 layoffs, PDI closed, sale offers) -> resolution (2016 Comcast, $3.8B) -> epilogue (safe era, Last Wish, Wild Robot).

### Turning points he marks
1. Wells' death + Eisner's refusal (1994): the "why".
2. Antz released 2 months before A Bug's Life (1998): first shot fired.
3. Shrek wins the first animated Oscar over Monsters, Inc. (2002 ceremony): revenge lands.
4. Shrek 2 at $928M beats The Incredibles (2004): the peak.
5. DreamWorks splits from its parent; Paramount takes 8% (2005-06): the hidden bleed.
6. "For the first time Disney copies DreamWorks" (The Wild, 2006).
7. Despicable Me (2010): a third rival appears.
8. 2013 "beginning of the end": Frozen + Despicable Me 2 vs Turbo.
9. 2015 layoffs, PDI closed, studio up for sale, "Disney said no".
10. 2016 sold to Comcast; Katzenberg out.

### Opinions, asides and jokes (summarised)
- "Not for kids at all" is said about El Dorado and Shrek.
- Skips The Prince of Egypt on religious grounds (depiction of a prophet). Also a religious aside when islanders think the con men are gods.
- Chel is "the most ... animated character" (a flirty joke he cuts off himself, "don't look at me like that").
- Shrek = Katzenberg's grudge film; Farquaad = Eisner.
- Lack of an Egyptian dub is why Shrek is less loved in Egypt; Lebanese dub of Bee Movie was bad.
- Shark Tale = "bad copy of Nemo"; he thought they were the same film as a kid.
- "Everyone steals the same concept" aside, defended as shared tech and a small industry.
- "DreamWorks characters are always a bit ugly."
- Mock rant on talent vs hard work (Ronaldo vs Messi), then a fake apology.
- Watched Megamind with his older brother; loved Turbo as a kid even though "it's genuinely bad".
- Trolls is "the worst thing my eyes have seen"; "why do kids today like this garbage".
- Thrilled The Bad Guys 2 shows the real Qasr El Nil bridge in Cairo.
- Wild Robot "the best cartoon in years".
