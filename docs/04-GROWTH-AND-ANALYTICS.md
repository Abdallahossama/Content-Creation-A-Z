## Lessons
1. **CTR is the bottleneck, not retention.** 1.7-2.0% vs a healthy ~4%+. Alcatraz holds 51% of a 14-minute video.
2. **The famous name in the title** decides whether YouTube can match the video to demand (Alcatraz, Roanoke, Cooper vs "129 men vanished").
   Fix low-impression videos with a title A/B test that adds the name.
3. **YouTube barely tests a video when the channel's recent CTR is low and there are no returning viewers** (Franklin 88, Cooper 54 impressions).
4. **Shorts:** ~40% stay to watch (60% swipe in the first second) → frame 1 must hit (shock line: "It rolled off."); Shorts die after
   ~2 days; 97-99.6% from the Shorts feed; Short → long conversion ~1.7%. Money/number titles win for Shorts.
5. **Retention drops** land on: the spoken channel ident, backstory dumps, full-black text cards, a spoken mid-video ask before the
   action. Evidence/document sections hold flat.
6. **TV viewers rely on autoplay**; end-screen clicks were 0%. Give the end screen a "Best for viewer" slot.
7. **Breakout formula (Oct 2026):** "They Asked [famous name] [question]. He [answer]." on WWII records (851K, 399K views on small
   channels), B&W face + 1-3 words on a red block, ~10 minutes. Small channels break out on famous names + primary records.
8. **Patience:** judge a long video after 5-7 days; Shorts after 1 day (retitle flat Shorts with a money/number hook).
