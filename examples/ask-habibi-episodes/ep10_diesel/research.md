# Ep 10 research: "Why Diesel Costs $2 More Than Gas Right Now" (long version, ~8 min)

Researched 22 Oct 2026. On 22 Oct, AAA has regular gas at **$4.27** and diesel at **$6.31**, a gap of **$2.04**. A year ago the gap was **56 cents**. Diesel set an all-time record of **$6.53 on 22 Sep 2026**. Gasoline has not beaten its June 2022 record.

The story has two layers:
1. **Structural (true every year).** Since Sept 2004 diesel has nearly always cost more than gas. The higher tax explains only about 8 cents of that. Each barrel of crude gives fewer gallons of diesel than gasoline. Diesel shares its supply with heating oil. And diesel trades on a global market, where the US is a big net exporter.
2. **The 2026 shock.** It began with the Iran war on 28 Feb 2026, which choked Hormuz traffic. Gulf diesel exports collapsed. Ukraine then hit almost every major Russian refinery (Ryazan most recently, on 15 Oct). Russia banned diesel exports from 8 July. According to the IEA, net diesel/gasoil exports from Russia and the Middle East combined fell **more than 60%** year on year in September.

The US response is a Treasury licence (GL 135, issued 9 Oct, valid until 7 Apr 2027) that lets Russian diesel in. Twelve senators from both parties want it reversed. Analysts say even the full promised volume covers only about 9-10 days of US demand. The first shipment is roughly a day's worth.

For the grocery angle, be honest. Diesel is up 74% year on year and trucking fuel surcharges hit a record. But BLS shows grocery prices up only **1.8%** year on year (Sept CPI). The pressure shows up first in freight, farm and heating costs. Food reacts later, and only partly.

For winter, EIA sees heating oil averaging $5.26 (+34%) and diesel averaging about $4.49 in 2027. The IEA says the market stays tight into 2027. Both are forecasts, not our predictions.

## Corrections to the Short

1. **"Gas ~$4.30, diesel ~$6.32 (AAA)."** Close enough. Latest AAA (22 Oct) is **$4.27 / $6.31**; EIA weekly (19 Oct) is $4.28 / $6.28. Use "about $4.27 for gas and $6.31 for diesel, a $2.04 gap", or round to "$4.30 vs $6.30". Re-check on recording day.
2. **"Diesel was $3.64 a year ago."** Slightly off. AAA's year-ago figure is **$3.63** (22 Oct 2025); EIA's is **$3.62** (20 Oct 2025). Say **"$3.63"**.
3. **"About a fifth of the world's oil normally passes through Hormuz."** Correct (EIA: 20 mb/d, about 20% of global liquids consumption, 2024). Keep "normally". Do not say it is fully closed now. IEA: September flows were 10.9 mb/d, about half of normal.
4. **"12 ships attacked in one week, most since the war began."** Correct per BBC Verify (UKMTO reports, 5-11 Oct). Better: "**at least 12 ships reported attacks in one week**, the most since the war began". Counts differ by source (IMO counted 11 tankers in the week to 4 Oct).
5. **"Ukrainian drone knocked out a key unit at a big refinery in Ryazan (last week)."** Correct: 15 Oct, Reuters says CDU-6 was halted. In an 8-minute video, date it ("on October 15th") instead of "last week". It was one of four crude units, so not the whole refinery.
6. **"Russia used to be the world's top seaborne diesel exporter."** Supported (Reuters 2023: "just ahead of the United States"). Keep "seaborne" and "used to be".
7. **"Its diesel exports are now the lowest since at least 2016."** Needs softening. That was true for **1-10 October** (Vortexa via Bloomberg). The ban was eased on 9-11 Oct and loadings restarted, though they are still very low. Say **"in early October, Russia's diesel exports hit their lowest in data going back to 2016"**.
8. **"Diesel exports from the Middle East and Russia together fell by more than 60%."** Correct, but say whose number and when: **"In September, combined net diesel exports from Russia and the Middle East were down more than 60% from a year earlier, according to the IEA."**
9. **"Trucks carry about 73% of US freight (ATA)."** Correct as **72.7% of domestic freight tonnage** (2024 data). Add "by weight".
10. **"The US is letting Russian diesel in until April (a waiver)."** Correct: OFAC General License 135, issued 9 Oct 2026, expires 7 Apr 2027. As of 22 Oct **none has arrived yet**; the first tanker was only due to leave Russia on 22 Oct. Say "allowing" rather than "letting in", or add "starting this month".
11. **"Senators from both parties want it reversed."** Correct: 12 senators, 6 R and 6 D, letter of 13 Oct.
12. **"Analysts say it's only a few days' worth of US diesel."** Half right; make it precise. The **first shipment** (300,000 t) is about **one day's worth** (about 1.5 days of US production per GasBuddy; about ¾ of a day's consumption per The Guardian). The **full promised package** (up to 4.8M t) is about **9-10 days** (S&P Global, CNBC). Suggested line: **"The first shipment is about one day of American diesel. Even the whole deal is under two weeks."**

## Do NOT say

- **"Hormuz is closed."** It is disrupted and dangerous but not fully shut. IEA: 10.9 mb/d in September against ~20 normally. CENTCOM says traffic flows; Iran claims control. Say "choked" or "disrupted".
- **"Ukraine destroyed half of Russia's refineries."** The 51% figure is a Ukrainian government claim that AP says is unverified. Other estimates use different definitions. Say "by some estimates, around half of Russia's refining capacity has been knocked offline at times this year".
- **"The Ryazan refinery was destroyed / shut down."** One crude unit was halted; three kept running.
- **"Gas prices hit a record."** Only diesel did. Regular's record is still $5.016 (June 2022).
- **"Diesel always costs more than gas."** It was often cheaper before September 2004.
- **"Grocery prices are soaring because of diesel."** Food at home was +1.8% year on year in September (BLS). Talk about freight costs, surcharges, farm costs and heating oil, and say grocery effects come later and in part.
- **"A semi burns 20,000+ gallons a year"** (as an average). The typical figure is about 10,000-13,000 gallons. 20,000+ applies only to very high-mileage long-haul trucks (e.g., 120,000+ miles a year). Say "**over 13,000 gallons a year at typical mileage**" (CALC: 85,991 mi ÷ ~6.5 mpg) or "more than 10,000".
- **"Russian diesel will bring prices down."** Estimates are 10-15c/gal at most (De Haan) or $1-2/bbl wholesale (Goldman). No volume has arrived yet.
- **"The US banned diesel exports."** A ban was discussed and denied. Wright: "We will not cease exports of U.S. diesel."
- **Exact oil price "today".** Brent was quoted anywhere from ~$98 to ~$112 this week depending on the contract and day. If needed, say "around $100 a barrel or more" (AAA headline on 22 Oct: "Crude Oil Nears $100/Barrel").
- **"The Iran war is over" or "there's a ceasefire".** The 17 Jun memorandum collapsed by 8 Jul, and attacks are ongoing in October.
- **A total count of ships attacked since February.** No authoritative running tally exists. Wikipedia's list of ~103 incidents is not a source.
- **"Diesel up 77% this year"** (NFU) or "68% since the war" (secondary). Use EIA: **+65% since the week before the war; +73-74% on a year ago**.
- **Naming every senator.** CBS and Roll Call list "Alan Armstrong (R-Okla.)", whom I could not independently confirm. Name only Collins, Murkowski, McConnell and Shaheen, or say "12 senators, half Republican, half Democrat".
- **Any forecast of our own** (e.g., "$7 diesel this winter"). Only quote EIA, IEA or named analysts, with attribution.
