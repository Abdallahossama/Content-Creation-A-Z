DESCRIPTION
-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Who are the Muslim Brotherhood? Their story begins in 1928 with six workmen and a schoolteacher in a canal town, and its founder's last night: Cairo, Saturday, February 12, 1949, about 8:30 p.m.

Hassan al-Banna built the Brotherhood from a coffee-house circle in Ismailia into a movement of hundreds of thousands, with a secret armed wing. In 1948 a judge and then Egypt's prime minister were shot dead by members. Seven weeks later, Banna was shot as he got into a taxi in Cairo. A boy shouted the getaway car's number, 9979, and vanished; five years later a court found the car had been rented by the Interior Ministry for its chief of criminal investigation. Then: Nasser, Qutb, Sadat, the ballot box, Morsi, and the US designations of January 2026.

Why you're hearing the name now: in Michigan's Senate race, Republicans have tied Democratic candidate Abdul El-Sayed to the Brotherhood. El-Sayed says he has never been a member and never will be; PolitiFact rated the President's claim "Pants on Fire", and the AP found no evidence of any affiliation.

An educational documentary told from the record: the Egyptian courts' 1954-55 rulings, contemporary wire reports, historians on every side, and US government documents. No images of the shooting or of the dead are shown.
