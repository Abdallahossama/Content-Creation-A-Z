At three twelve a.m., the doors locked behind me.
Outside, something was eating the parking lot.
How long would you last in here?
I lasted thirty days. But Day seventeen almost killed me.
##
Day one.
I'm a night-shift stock clerk at a supermarket on the edge of town.
At two a.m., the radio in the back room started screaming about riots.
At three, a man walked in through the front doors with a bite on his neck.
He grabbed the first person he saw. That was Walt from the bakery.
My manager, Rick, hit the emergency button, and the steel shutters came down.
Then it was just five of us inside.
Rick, Walt, a security guard named Marcus, a delivery driver named Dex, and me.
And through the glass, we watched the town fall apart.
##
Day two.
Walt had a bandage on his arm. He said it was a scratch.
Nobody wanted to argue with the guy who knew how to bake bread.
Marcus counted what we had.
Four thousand cans. Six hundred bottles of water. Enough food for months.
Rick said the army would come in three days. Tops.
I wrote "Day two" on the break room wall with a marker. It felt silly.
##
Day three.
Walt didn't come out of the bathroom.
When Marcus opened the door, Walt wasn't Walt anymore.
I won't tell you what happened next. I'll just say Marcus was very fast.
That night, nobody slept. We finally understood the rules.
A bite or a scratch, and you have about a day.
##
Day five.
No army. No helicopters. The radio was just static now.
So we got to work.
We pushed every shopping cart against the glass doors.
We stacked dog food bags behind them, because dog food bags are heavy and nobody was buying dog food anymore.
Dex found a ladder to the roof.
From up there, we could see the whole town. Nothing was moving. Except them.
##
Day eight.
The power went out.
The freezers started dying, so we had a feast.
Steak, ice cream, frozen pizza cooked on a camping grill from aisle twelve.
For one night, it felt like a party at the end of the world.
Then the smell of cooking meat brought a crowd to the back door.
Forty of them. Maybe more.
We stopped cooking after that.
##
Day eleven.
Rick made the rules.
One can of food per person per day. Two bottles of water.
Nobody goes near the doors alone.
Dex didn't like the rules. Dex didn't like Rick.
I started noticing cans missing from the storeroom.
I didn't say anything. That was my mistake.
##
Day fourteen.
We heard a voice on the radio for the first time in a week.
A man said there was a safe zone at the river bridge, twelve miles north.
Boats were leaving every morning until the end of the month.
Rick wanted to stay. Dex wanted to go now.
Marcus said we should wait and plan.
I said nothing again. I just looked at the map.
##
Day seventeen.
This is the day I told you about.
I woke up at four a.m. because it was cold. Too cold.
The back door was open.
Dex was gone. So was half our food. And all of the water.
He left the door open so nobody would follow him.
They were already inside.
I remember running through the dark, past the cereal aisle, with something right behind me.
Marcus pulled me up the ladder by my shirt.
Rick didn't make it to the ladder.
##
Day eighteen.
It was just me and Marcus on the roof.
Two people. Three bottles of water. Twelve cans of beans.
And a store full of the dead below us.
Marcus said, we're not dying on a roof that smells like fish.
So we made a plan.
##
Day twenty.
We rationed everything.
Half a can a day. A capful of water every two hours.
We caught rainwater in a plastic kiddie pool from the seasonal aisle.
I lost so much weight my uniform looked like a tent.
At night, we listened to them below us. Walking. Always walking.
##
Day twenty-three.
Marcus figured it out.
They follow sound.
So we threw a car alarm key fob from the roof into the parking lot.
Click. The alarm went off on a minivan across the street.
And every single one of them walked out of the store toward it.
We had ten minutes.
We ran down, grabbed water, grabbed cans, grabbed two backpacks, and ran back up.
It was the best ten minutes of my life.
##
Day twenty-six.
We heard the radio again.
The boats at the bridge were leaving for the last time on Day thirty.
Twelve miles. On foot. Through a town full of them.
Marcus looked at me and said, it's that or the roof forever.
##
Day twenty-nine.
We trained.
Walking on the roof in silence. Hand signals. Car alarm keys in every pocket.
I wrote one last line on the wall in marker.
Day twenty-nine. If you find this, we went north.
##
Day thirty.
We left at four a.m. The same time Dex left.
Every time we saw a crowd, we set off a car alarm somewhere else.
Click. They turned. We walked.
Eleven miles in, at sunrise, we saw the bridge.
And a boat. Still there.
And standing on the dock, with our food in a shopping cart, was Dex.
He saw us. He smiled. And then he raised his hand and pointed at the boat.
It was full.
