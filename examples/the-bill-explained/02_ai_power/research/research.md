## 3. The bill, line by line

**Where the money goes (typical bill):** supply/generation (energy + capacity), transmission, distribution, then riders, taxes and fees.
- REPORTED (PolitiFact, citing experts): the wholesale "supply" part is **about 30% to 50% of a household bill**. The rest is transmission, distribution and taxes.
- CLAIM: "generation 56%, distribution 31%, transmission 13%" is widely attributed to EIA, but we could not find it on an EIA page. A Statista/EIA 2022 chart puts generation at about 62%. **Don't put a percentage split on screen. Say "the biggest piece is usually generation".** RECORD (EIA): "The cost of generating electricity is the largest component of the price of electricity." https://www.eia.gov/energyexplained/electricity/prices-and-factors-affecting-prices.php

### Line (a): Energy / generation charge, the wholesale price
- REPORTED (Bloomberg News analysis, 29 Sep 2025): wholesale prices at some grid nodes near data centers rose **up to 267% from April 2020 to April 2025**. Of the nodes where prices rose, **more than 70% were within 50 miles** of significant data center activity. The analysis covered about 25,000 nodes. https://www.bloomberg.com/graphics/2025-ai-data-centers-electricity-prices/
- **Verdict on "267%":** it is **wholesale prices at specific nodes, not household bills.** PolitiFact rated Sen. Warren's version ("residents' bills rose as much as 267%") **Mostly False** (12 Jun 2026).
- RECORD (Dallas Fed Working Paper 2606, Kay, Reaser & Taylor):
  - **Original, 20 Mar 2026:** existing data centers have raised average US wholesale prices **2% to 6%**, with larger effects in data center corridors.
  - **Revised Oct 2026 (newest):** data centers raised wholesale prices **6.9% nationally by 2025 and 15.0% in northern Virginia**. Under the preferred scenario it reaches **12.3% nationally by 2028**. https://www.dallasfed.org/research/papers/2026/wp2606
  - The paper also notes the effect spreads through trade to regions with few data centers. **Use the revised numbers. The "2–6%" figure is out of date.**
- RECORD (Dallas Fed, 5 Mar 2026): data centers add about **0.05 percentage points to headline PCE inflation in 2026** and 0.13 pp in 2030 through retail electricity prices. https://www.dallasfed.org/research/economics/2026/0305-kay-datacenters
- REPORTED (Dallas Fed via Stacker/KRDO, 9 Nov 2026): wholesale power is **roughly half** of the retail price, so a wholesale rise hits retail bills at about half its size, and with a delay. https://krdo.com/stacker-business-economy/2026/11/09/how-data-center-demand-is-reshaping-electricity-costs-across-the-us/

### Line (b): Capacity charge (hidden inside "supply"/"generation" in PJM states)
PJM runs the grid for **13 states + DC** (about 67 million people). Every year it holds an auction that pays power plants to be available 3 years ahead, and households pay for that through their supply rate.

| Delivery year (auction held) | Clearing price ($/MW-day) | Total cost | Data center share (Independent Market Monitor) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2024/25 (Jul 2023) | $28.92 | about $2.2B | — |
| **2025/26** (Jul 2024) | **$269.92** | **$14.7B** | **$9.3B, 63% of the increase**. The monitor said demand "raised capacity costs by $9.3 billion, or 174%" against a no-data-center case. (Bloomberg's June 2025 report: $9.4B.) |
| **2026/27** (Jul 2025) | **$329.17** (cap) | **$16.1B** | Data center load alone = **+82.1%** in capacity revenues (2025 State of the Market, 12 Mar 2026) |
| **2027/28** (Dec 2025) | **$333.44** (cap) | **$16.4B** | **$6.5B = 40%** (Utility Dive, 7 Jan 2026). First time the whole region fell **short of the reliability target** (about 6,500 MW). |
| **2028/29** (results 14 Jul 2026) | **$325** (cap) | **about $16.4B** | **$6.3B = 38%** (btw.media, 22 Jul 2026, citing the monitor). **6,831 MW short** of the reliability requirement. |

## 5. Counterpoints (keep these in the video)
1. **Prices rose even where there are few data centers.** Maine, New Hampshire and Hawaii have big increases. Gas was **+60% in 2025**. Storms, wildfires, grid aging and inflation all raise distribution costs (LBNL: distribution pushed up prices in 22 states, 2024 to 2025).
2. **Over the long run, prices haven't exploded in real terms.** RECORD-grade (LBNL/Brattle 2026, via EEI):
   - Real average retail prices in 2025 were only **3% above 2019** and **6% below 2010**.
   - Electricity was **1.25% of household spending** in 2025, near an all-time low.
   - Coverage says 29 states saw real prices fall from 2019 to 2025 (REPORTED, secondary).
3. **More demand can lower rates if big users pay their share.** RECORD-grade (LBNL/Brattle via EEI):
   - Load growth spreads fixed grid costs over more kWh. **Outside PJM**, customers "have largely been shielded" from data center costs.
   - REPORTED (Columbia CGEP; Stacker): **North Dakota** demand grew almost **40%** while real prices fell about 3¢/kWh. Nebraska and New Mexico are similar.
   - The Dallas Fed paper derives conditions under which "load growth lowers retail rates".
4. **Wholesale is not your bill.** The 267% figure is node-level wholesale; wholesale is about half of retail, and with a lag.
   - COMPUTED illustration (rough): the Dallas Fed revised **+6.9% wholesale** × about ½ pass-through ≈ **+3.5% retail** ≈ **about $5.40/month (about $65/yr)** on a $157 bill.
   - The original 2–6% gives about **$1.60 to $4.70/month**. In PJM's capacity-hit zones (above) the data center slice is **larger**: $10 to $20+/month. **Present this as an order-of-magnitude estimate, never as a fact.**
5. **Forecasts may be inflated.** Grid Strategies: utilities may overstate data center demand by up to 40%. OpenAI/Oracle dropped the Abilene expansion (Mar 2026). If the buildings don't come, auction costs built on forecast load were paid anyway: the monitor's $6.2B of the 2027/28 cost was for data centers **not yet built**.

## 6. Outlook
- RECORD (STEO, 10 Nov 2026): residential price **18.2¢ (2026) to 18.7¢ (2027)**, +2.7%. Henry Hub falls to $2.88 in 2027, which is a downward pull on prices.
- **PJM:**
  - The **2029/30 base auction** is scheduled for **early-to-mid December 2026** under the extended cap (about $325). Sources give 9 Dec or a close of 15 Dec, so check.
  - The **backstop auction** for about **6.8 GW** of new plants (15-year fixed price, offer cap **$555/MW-day**) was **delayed** after FERC only partly approved it (**29 Sep 2026**). There is no new date yet. NRDC says it could cost up to **$20B** (CLAIM). https://utilitydive.com/news/pjm-delays-backstop-procurement-ferc-data-center/831751
  - PJM also asked to **curtail new large loads** without their own supply in emergencies. A secondary report says FERC rejected the curtailment piece on 9 Oct 2026 but accepted a 50 MW large-load registry (CLAIM).
- **FERC large loads:**
  - DOE directed FERC (Oct 2025) to set national rules for connecting loads over 20 MW (deadline 30 Apr 2026).
  - On **18 Jun 2026** FERC issued **show-cause orders** to all six grid operators to justify or reform large-load (over 50 MW) interconnection rules (REPORTED, Bracewell / Willkie / Linklaters). https://www.bracewell.com/resources/ferc-show-cause-orders-data-center-interconnection/
  - We found **no final national rule**, so don't say one exists.
- **States:** the 2026 sessions were dominated by affordability. Expect more large-load classes and "data centers pay their own way" laws. No state moratorium had passed as of mid-June (REPORTED).

## DO-NOT-SAY
- "Bills went up 267% because of data centers." That figure is wholesale prices at some grid nodes (PolitiFact: Mostly False when used for bills).
- "Data centers are the main reason bills are rising nationwide." It isn't supported. Say instead: "a big driver in some regions, especially the Mid-Atlantic".
- "AI adds $X to everyone's bill" as a fact. Only give our COMPUTED range, labeled as an estimate.
- "Dallas Fed: 2–6%" as the current number. It was revised in Oct 2026 to 6.9% (2025) and 12.3% (2028).
- "Electricity is up X% in the last 12 months (October)." BLS published no 12-month October figure; use September's +4.4%.
- "Utilities asked for a record $23 billion" (unverified). Use "$18.6 billion in the first half of 2026, gas and electric".
- "Generation is 56% of your bill." The source isn't confirmed.
- "FERC passed a national rule" (no final rule found). "The backstop auction happened" (it was delayed).
- Exact xAI turbine counts or MW. Also "illegal turbines" (it's an allegation in a lawsuit).
- That any company, utility or politician is "price-gouging" or "making you pay for their AI". Use mechanisms, not blame.
- Blaming a president or party. Shapiro's cap and the White House pledge are fine to state as facts.
- "Stargate is 1.2 GW running." Say "planned to reach about 1.2 GW; about 618 MW delivered by September 2026".
- Mixing EIA monthly (Aug 18.16¢) with annual (2025 17.23¢) in one "up X%" claim without saying which.
