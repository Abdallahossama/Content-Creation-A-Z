## 2. Structural reasons diesel costs more

1. **A barrel gives more gasoline than diesel.** A 42-gallon barrel of crude yields about 19–20 gal of gasoline and 11–13 gal of distillate (diesel or heating oil). Refiners can change the mix only within limits. RECORD (EIA Energy Explained, Refining crude oil): https://www.eia.gov/energyexplained/oil-and-petroleum-products/refining-crude-oil.php
2. **Crack spreads (the refiner's margin).** In 2026 diesel's crack went far above gasoline's.
   - REPORTED: on 18 Aug 2026 the diesel crack was $104.62/bbl and the gasoline crack $54.68/bbl (thetrading.tools reading, https://www.thetrading.tools/crack-spread). This is a CLAIM-grade source.
   - REPORTED: the US Gulf Coast ULSD crack averaged **$110.97/bbl** in the week to 25 Sep 2026, against $28.62 in January (Crux Investor, https://www.cruxinvestor.com/posts/crude-exports-recovered-to-98-of-pre-war-levels-diesel-did-not).
   - REPORTED: "2026 will be remembered as the year that diesel cracks topped the century mark" (RBN Energy, https://rbnenergy.com/daily-posts/blog/100bbl-diesel-crack-or-how-2026-exposed-fragility-global-refining).
   - RECORD: EIA says crack spreads are high "because of damage to refineries in the Middle East and Russia" and expects them to stay elevated "through most of 2027" (STEO, Nov 2026, https://www.eia.gov/outlooks/steo/archives/nov26.pdf).
   - COMPUTED: $100/bbl ÷ 42 = about $2.38 per gallon of refining margin.
3. **Cleaner fuel costs more to make.** The EPA 15 ppm ultra-low sulfur diesel (ULSD) standard started at refineries on 1 Jun 2006, reached retail on 1 Sep 2006, and was phased in fully by 2010. RECORD (EPA, https://19january2025snapshot.epa.gov/diesel-fuel-standards/diesel-fuel-standards-and-rulemakings). The ULSD transition "affected diesel fuel production and distribution costs". RECORD (EIA, https://www.eia.gov/energyexplained/diesel-fuel/prices-and-outlook.php). We found no official cents-per-gallon figure for this cost, so don't give one.
4. **Taxes (small part of the gap).**
   - Federal tax is 24.4¢/gal on diesel and 18.4¢/gal on gas. RECORD.
   - Average state taxes and fees (Jan 2026) are 35.5¢ on diesel and 33.27¢ on gas. California has the highest (87.3¢ diesel, 70.9¢ gas) and Alaska the lowest (9¢). RECORD (EIA, https://www.eia.gov/todayinenergy/detail.php?id=67165). Another EIA page gives 35.86¢ for diesel, so say "about 36 cents".
   - COMPUTED: tax difference ≈ 6.0¢ + 2.2¢ = **about 8¢ of the $2.13 gap (under 4%)**.
5. **Winter heating oil.** Diesel and heating oil are the same distillate stream. In winters with high heating-oil demand, diesel rose above gasoline. RECORD (EIA FAQ). EIA forecasts heating oil at $5.96/gal in Q4 2026, against $3.68 in Q4 2025. RECORD (STEO Nov 2026, Table 2). EIA expects a slightly milder winter (1% fewer heating degree days than last winter). RECORD (STEO).
6. **Diesel is the world's workhorse fuel, and Europe is short of it.**
   - RECORD (EIA FAQ): distillate demand is high in Europe, China, India and the US.
   - REPORTED: Europe usually imports close to 1 million b/d of diesel. Before the war, about 27% of its imports came from the Middle East (OGJ/IEA, https://ogj.com/general-interest/economics-markets/news/55316604/2026-brings-new-challenges-for-european-diesel-supplies).
   - CLAIM: a September 2026 report cites Vortexa saying Europe's diesel imports hit a record low for the month. The month is unclear in what we saw, so don't use it until it's checked.
   - REPORTED: Bloomberg called diesel "the world's workhorse fuel" (EnergyConnects, Aug 2026, https://www.energyconnects.com/news/oil/2026/august/war-brings-winter-of-discontent-for-the-world-s-workhorse-fuel/).
   - When Europe pays more, US diesel gets pulled overseas as exports. That is an inference, so voice it as "Europe is bidding for the same barrels".
7. **US refinery closures.**
   - RECORD (EIA Refinery Capacity Report 2026, https://www.eia.gov/todayinenergy/detail.php?id=67807):
     - US capacity on 1 Jan 2026 was 18.2 million b/d across 130 refineries, down about 1%.
     - LyondellBasell Houston closed in Mar 2025 (263,776 b/d).
     - Phillips 66 Los Angeles closed in Oct 2025 (138,700 b/d), cutting West Coast capacity by 5%.
     - Valero Benicia, CA (145,000 b/d) has since stopped refining.
   - RECORD (STEO Nov 2026): refinery runs are slightly below Dec 2025 "because of reduced overall refinery capacity", citing Benicia.
   - COMPUTED: 263,776 + 138,700 + 145,000 = **about 547,000 b/d closed since early 2025**, about 3% of US capacity.
   - RECORD (STEO): refineries ran 16.6 million b/d in October 2026, "very high rates".
8. **Inventories are at a record low for the season.**
   - RECORD (EIA weekly distillate stocks, https://www.eia.gov/dnav/pet/hist/LeafHandler.ashx?n=PET&s=WDISTUS1&f=W): **103.3 million barrels** in the week ending 6 Nov 2026. The week before (30 Oct) was 99.3 million.
   - COMPUTED: 103.3 million is about **8% below the 2021–2025 average** for the same week (112.8M) and the **lowest for that week of the year in the series** (back to 1982). The next lowest was 106.3M in 2022.
   - REPORTED: stocks are the "lowest level for this time of year in EIA records" (Energy News Beat, 5 Nov 2026, https://energynewsbeat.co/?p=230651).
   - Earlier RECORD (EIA WPSR): 13% below the 5-yr average in the week ending 4 Sep 2026.

## 4. Who pays

- **Trucks** carried **72.7% of US domestic freight tonnage** (about 11.27 billion tons in 2024). REPORTED, ATA figure (via trade press, https://www.truckinginfo.com/news/march-truck-tonnage-posts-strongest-annual-gain-since-2022). Say "about 73% of freight by weight".
- **ATRI Operational Costs of Trucking (2026 update, 2025 data):** RECORD/REPORTED, https://truckingresearch.org/2026/07/new-atri-report-details-accelerating-costs-and-low-profitability-despite-cuts/
  - Total cost was **$2.336 per mile** in 2025, a record.
  - Fuel was **about $0.48 per mile**.
  - Non-fuel costs were $1.854.
  - COMPUTED: fuel was 20.5% of cost in 2025. ATRI's 48¢ at EIA's 2025 average diesel price of $3.66 implies about 7.6 mpg. At $6.127 the same truck pays **about 80¢ per mile** (0.48 × 6.127/3.66), and fuel becomes **about 30%** of per-mile cost. This assumes no change in mpg or fuel surcharges.
- **Fuel surcharges:** most carrier contracts index surcharges to the weekly EIA diesel price. That is industry practice (CLAIM, general knowledge), so say "surcharges are usually pegged to the government's weekly diesel price" and keep it unquantified.
- **Food and groceries:**
  - REPORTED: transportation, storage and energy are more than 7% of total food costs (USDA ERS, via Local10, 14 Sep 2026, https://www.local10.com/news/local/2026/09/14/attention-shoppers-rising-diesel-costs-will-hit-your-grocery-bill-experts-warn/).
  - RECORD (BLS CPI, 10 Nov 2026): food at home rose +0.4% in October. BLS could not publish 12-month food-at-home changes because of the 2025 data gap. https://www.bls.gov/news.release/archives/cpi_11102026.htm
  - We have no official diesel-to-grocery pass-through number for 2026, so don't invent one.
- **Farmers** (RECORD-grade, AFBF Market Intel, 1 Oct 2026, https://www.fb.org/intel/markets/diesel-costs-increase-for-harvest):
  - Farm diesel rose from $3.01 to $5.61 (Sep 2025 to Sep 2026, USDA AMS Illinois).
  - Harvest diesel costs rose by **$7.98/acre for corn**, $3.56 for soybeans, $14 for rice, and $57.59 for apples.
  - AFBF asked for a suspension of the federal diesel tax (Feedstuffs; REPORTED).
  - USDA's farm fuel expense of about $22B (+29%) in 2026 is a CLAIM: it is secondary and we have not checked it.
- **Trains and ships:** freight railroads and most ships burn diesel or other distillate fuels. Railroads also charge fuel surcharges. We found no 2026-specific figure, so **mention qualitatively only**.

### COMPUTED example: one semi truck, one year
Assumptions: 100,000 miles a year at 6.5 mpg = **15,385 gallons**.
- Extra paid at today's spread (diesel against what the same gallons of gas would cost): 15,385 × $2.132 = **$32,800/yr**.
- At the "normal" 2010–2021 spread: 15,385 × $0.334 = $5,140/yr. So the 2026 gap costs **about $27,700 more than normal**.
- Against diesel a year ago: 15,385 × $2.290 = **$35,230 more in fuel this year**.
- Range at 6 to 7 mpg: $30,460 to $35,530 extra at today's spread.
- Viewer version: a diesel pickup doing 12,000 mi/yr at 20 mpg uses 600 gal × $2.132 = **about $1,280/yr more** than a gas truck.

## YouTube ad-safety notes
- Treat war as **economic context only**. Use neutral verbs ("conflict", "attacks on shipping", "drone strikes on refineries"). Avoid "bombing", "killed" and casualty numbers. Don't show combat footage, explosions, injured people, weapons close-ups, or flags burning.
- Visuals: tankers, maps (Hormuz and the Russia–Europe routes as **graphics**), refineries, trucks, pumps, price boards. Stock footage only (Pexels, per channel rules). **No women in visuals** (channel hard rule).
- Keep politics two-sided and sourced: name both parties for the senators and give Treasury's position in the same breath.
- Put the title and thumbnail on the **price** ("$2 more"), not the war. Words like "war", "attack" or "strike" in the title and thumbnail raise the risk of limited ads.
- Show the date on every price card ("EIA, week of Nov 9, 2026") because prices move every week.
- Add sources to the description (EIA gasdiesel, STEO Nov 2026, EIA refinery capacity, ATRI, AFBF, OFAC GL 135).
