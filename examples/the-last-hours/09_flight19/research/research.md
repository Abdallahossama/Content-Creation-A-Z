# FLIGHT 19: verified research (five TBM Avengers from NAS Fort Lauderdale, and PBM-5 "Training 49" from NAS Banana River, Wednesday 5 December 1945)

Channel: The Last Hours (@TheLastHoursDocs; US / Canada / UK audience). Format: hour-by-hour countdown told only from the record. Folder 09_flight19, release date: owner to set.
Spine: from the 11:00 a.m. weather forecast at NAS Fort Lauderdale through the 2:10 p.m. takeoff, the first "lost" call at about 3:40-3:45, the compass messages, the turns (030, 090, 270, then east again at 6:04), sunset at 5:29, the 5:50 radio fix that was never sent to the planes, the last call at 7:04 p.m., the Mariner that took off at 7:27 and the flames seen at 7:50, the five-day search, the Board of Investigation (record dated 10 December 1945), the "mental aberration" endorsement (15 February 1946) and the 1947 correction after Lt. Taylor's mother appealed.
Compiled 2026-10-23.

**Clock note (read first):** All Navy times in the Board record carry the suffix **"R" (Romeo = UTC-5 = Eastern Standard Time)**. Wartime daylight time ("Eastern War Time") had ended on 30 Sep 1945, so 5 Dec 1945 local time in Florida was EST. **Coast Guard radio logs (Form 2614A) and the tanker's message are in GCT (UTC):** the Pensacola HF/DF log starts a "new radio day" at 0000 GCT = 7:00 p.m. EST. Subtract 5 hours. The Pensacola log's "NET ALERTED ON 4805 KCS ON FT28" at 2147 GCT matches Board Finding 27 (nets alerted at 1647R): proof the conversion is right [COMPUTED]. The winds-aloft form has "EWT" pre-printed on it; treat it as local time.

**Transcript note:** No audio of Flight 19 exists. Every quote below was typed or written by hand by radio operators at different stations (Port Everglades, Fort Lauderdale, Pensacola) or recalled under oath by Lt. Robert F. Cox. The same transmission is worded differently at different stations and logged a few minutes apart. Quote the log you name; never present any audio or actor voice as the real men.

## How to read this file

Every fact carries a status tag and one or more source IDs (section 0).

- **[CONFIRMED]**: primary/official record: the Board of Investigation record and exhibits on NARA microfilm M1657 (read on the original frames), the NHHC transcriptions of the accident cards, the Navy endorsements, NARA catalog documents.
- **[REPORTED]**: credible secondary: NHHC H-Gram and *Naval Aviation News* summaries of the Board record, contemporary AP/UP reporting, NARA blog, Smithsonian, Kusche, Quasar, museum biographies, 1991 press. Wikipedia used only as a lead-finder and tagged WIKI.
- **[DISPUTED]**: sources disagree (both sides given).
- **[COMPUTED]**: our arithmetic from confirmed inputs (time zones, weekdays, sun/moon, intervals, distances, unit conversions). Method shown.
- **[LEGEND]**: rumour, myth, invented dialogue, or popular error. Do not narrate as fact.

Rules kept: no invented numbers, quotes or sources. Long quotes only from U.S. government text (public domain). Modern writers paraphrased; any modern quote under 15 words.

**Content flags for the edit (hard rules):**
- **27 men are presumed dead** (14 + 13). Factual, respectful tone. Nobody was ever found: no bodies, so nothing to describe. No speculation presented as fact.
- **Women and girls: no images, ever.** People in this story who are women: Lt. Taylor's mother **Katherine Taylor** and aunt **Mary Carroll** (they fought the Navy's verdict), his sister **Georgia Lowe**, Capt. Powers's wife, Lt. Cox's daughter (interviewed on TV in 2021), any WAVES who may appear in 1945 base or radio-room photos, the women of the 1986 Challenger crew (section 11). **Narration only.** List in section 18.
- **Lt. Charles C. Taylor's dignity:** the Navy first blamed him, then cleared him (1947). Rumours that he was drunk or "didn't want to fly because of a premonition" are excluded as fact (section 17).
- **No Bermuda Triangle framing as cause.** The record describes navigation error, weather, radio interference, darkness and fuel. Say what the record shows; name the myths as myths (section 6).
