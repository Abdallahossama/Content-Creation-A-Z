Another Pilot Offered Help. He Said "Don't Come After Me" #flight19

December 5, 1945, around 4 p.m. The leader of five lost Navy bombers says both his compasses are out and he's sure he's over the Florida Keys. He was over the Bahamas. Another instructor offers to fly out and meet him. The answer, as that instructor told the Navy under oath: "Don't come after me."

14 Men, 5 Planes, and an Order About the Last 10 Gallons #flight19

5:24 p.m., December 5, 1945. A Coast Guard radio station hears the order to Flight 19: when one plane drops to ten gallons of gas, all planes will land together. On the sea. Five minutes later the sun set at Fort Lauderdale. It was not their last message.

The Navy Knew Roughly Where Flight 19 Was. Nobody Told Them. #flight19

5:50 p.m., December 5, 1945. Radio direction-finders along the coast put the five lost Avengers within a hundred miles of one point, about 230 miles north of home, over open sea. The position went to a tower by phone and out on the teletype. No station ever broadcast it to the planes.
