# RASPUTIN'S LAST NIGHT: verified research (Petrograd, Friday night 29 December 1916 into Saturday 30 December 1916, New Style = 16-17 December 1916, Old Style; the body found Monday 1 January 1917 NS = 19 December OS; the body burned in the night of 10-11 March 1917 OS = 23-24 March 1917 NS)

Channel: The Last Hours (@TheLastHoursDocs; US / Canada / UK audience, 35+, mostly TV). Format: hour-by-hour countdown with the clock times the record has, told only from the record. Folder 23_rasputin_last_night. Narrator: the File Keeper. Length about 10-12 min. Release **Mon 28 Dec 2026** (two days before the 110th anniversary, **Wed 30 Dec 2026**) [COMPUTED: CAL].

Spine: **Jan 1869 (OS)** born in Pokrovskoye, Tobolsk province, Siberia → **1892** religious calling, years of wandering → **1905** meets the imperial family → the heir Alexei's haemophilia; Rasputin "seemed able to stop the bleeding" → **27 Sep 1912 OS** a secret order for an inquiry into his life and teaching → **Aug 1914** war → **Sat 19 Nov 1916 OS (2 Dec NS)** Purishkevich's Duma speech against the "dark forces" → **20-21 Nov OS** Yusupov comes to Purishkevich → **22 Nov OS** first meeting: Yusupov, Dmitri, "Lieutenant S." (Sukhotin), Purishkevich; Dr Lazovert recruited → **Fri 16 Dec OS (29 Dec NS)** 9:30 p.m. Purishkevich leaves his hospital train; **11:15 p.m.** walks out of the City Duma; **11:50 p.m.** his car arrives, Lazovert driving → **Sat 17 Dec OS (30 Dec NS)**: just after midnight at the Moika palace; **12:35 a.m.** Yusupov and Lazovert drive off to fetch Rasputin; **12:45 a.m.** cyanide poured into two glasses; **about 1 a.m.** the car is back, "Yankee Doodle" on the gramophone; **about 2:30 a.m.** Yusupov's shot in the basement; **after 3 a.m.** three men drive off to burn the clothes; **about 3-4 a.m.** the courtyard: Purishkevich's four shots; the policeman; **before 5 a.m.** the body is dropped through the ice at the Petrovsky Bridge; **after 5 a.m.** Purishkevich back at his train → **17 Dec OS, afternoon** blood on the bridge railing, a boot in the ice → **Sun 31 Dec NS, 9:10 p.m.** the British ambassador cables London → **Mon 1 Jan 1917 NS** body found under the ice; the Tsar back at Tsarskoye Selo; autopsy (Kosorotov) → **Wed 3 Jan 1917 NS (21 Dec OS)** burial at Tsarskoye Selo → **Fri 5 Jan NS (23 Dec OS)** Dmitri sent to Persia; Yusupov exiled to his estate → **2 Mar 1917 OS** the Tsar abdicates → **8-10 Mar OS** coffin dug up → **between 3 and 4 a.m., 11 Mar OS (24 Mar NS)** body burned in the forest near Lesnoye; the act signed.

Compiled 6 Nov 2026. 30 Dec 1916 → 28 Dec 2026 = **40,175 days** [COMPUTED: CAL].

**Clock and calendar note (read first):**
- **Two calendars.** Russia used the Julian calendar (Old Style, OS) until February 1918; in 1916-17 it ran **13 days behind** the Western (Gregorian, New Style, NS) calendar. Russian sources (Purishkevich, the police, Kosorotov, the 1917 act) give OS dates; British and American papers (Buchanan's telegrams, the New York Times) give NS dates. **Weekdays are the same in both** (16 Dec OS and 29 Dec NS are both Friday) [COMPUTED: CAL].
  - Narration rule: give the Western date the audience knows, with the Russian date once on screen: "Friday night, December 29, 1916. In Russia's own calendar, December 16."
- **Petrograd clock in 1916 = local mean time of the capital, about UTC+2:01** (Russia had no standard time zones until 1919; summer time was first used from 1 July 1917, so it does not apply to December 1916 or March 1917). Offset computed from the longitude of the Moika palace, 30.316°E → +2 h 01 min [COMPUTED: LONG]. **Do not use the IANA zone Europe/Moscow for 1916: it gives Moscow's own mean time (+2:30), not Petrograd's.**
- **New York / Toronto (EST, UTC-5) = Petrograd minus 7 h 01 min; London (GMT) = Petrograd minus 2 h 01 min** [COMPUTED: TZ]:
  - Purishkevich leaves his train, **9:30 p.m. Fri** Petrograd = **2:29 p.m. EST Friday**.
  - Yusupov and Lazovert drive off for Rasputin, **12:35 a.m. Sat** = **5:34 p.m. EST Friday**.
  - Rasputin arrives, **about 1:00 a.m.** = about **6 p.m. EST Friday**.
  - Yusupov's shot, **about 2:30 a.m. Sat** = **7:29 p.m. EST Friday** (= 12:29 a.m. Saturday in London).
  - Courtyard shots, **3-4 a.m.** = **8-9 p.m. EST Friday**.
  - Body through the ice, **before 5 a.m.** = before **10 p.m. EST Friday**.
  - Buchanan's first telegram, **9:10 p.m. Sun 31 Dec** = **2:09 p.m. EST Sunday**; his second, **9 p.m. Mon 1 Jan** = **1:59 p.m. EST Monday**.
  - The burning, **3-4 a.m. Sat 24 Mar 1917** Petrograd = **8-9 p.m. EST Friday 23 Mar**.
- **Story beat [COMPUTED]: in New York it was still Friday evening, dinner time, while Rasputin was being killed. The New York Times printed the story on its front page on Wednesday, January 3, 1917.**
- **Sun, Petrograd, Sat 30 Dec 1916 (local mean time):** sunrise **9:03 a.m.**, sunset **3:03 p.m.**; civil dusk ended 4:00 p.m.; full astronomical night from 5:47 p.m. Friday to 6:19 a.m. Saturday. **Six hours of daylight.** Sunrise Mon 1 Jan 1917: 9:03 a.m., sunset 3:05 p.m. (body found in daylight; time of day unknown) [COMPUTED: SUN, NOAA-type formula, Moika 59.937°N 30.316°E].
- **Moon:** a waxing crescent, about **33% lit**; it **set at about 10:20 p.m. on Friday 29 Dec** and did not rise again until about 10:45 a.m. Saturday. **The murder, the courtyard and the bridge all happened under a moonless sky** [COMPUTED: MOON, low-precision lunar theory, ± about 15 min]. Weather (cloud, snowfall, temperature) is **UNKNOWN**; Purishkevich mentions snow in the courtyard ("rykhlyy sneg", loose snow) and frost ("moroz") [PUR18]. Say "a moonless winter night", not "a full moon over the Neva".
- **All clock times below are Petrograd time unless marked.**

**Record note:** Nobody who was not part of the plot saw the killing. The night is told by (1) **Purishkevich's diary**, first printed in Kiev in 1918, **read in full** (Russian Wikisource text of the Paris 1923 edition; translations ours); it gives most clock times; (2) **Yusupov's 1927 memoir** in the English translation by **Oswald Rayner** (yes, the same Rayner), **read in the archive.org OCR text** (chapters XI-XIV; page numbers approximate); (3) **two British Foreign Office telegrams and a minute in FO 371/2994**, read as images on The National Archives blog; (4) **the New York Times front page of 3 Jan 1917**, read as a page image; (5) **the official act on the burning of the body, March 1917**, read in the German translation printed under its facsimile; (6) the police side (constable Vlasyuk's statement) and the autopsy, known to us **only as quoted or summarised** by later writers (Radzinsky via AP; Mlechin; Fontanka; ru.wikipedia citing Platonov's document collection). **Douglas Smith (2016), Radzinsky (2000) and Cook (2005) were not readable here**; their positions are given only as reported by others, tagged, with page numbers marked "not verified".

## How to read this file

Every fact carries a status tag and one or more source IDs (section 0).

- **[RECORD]**: primary documents: the Foreign Office telegrams, the 1912 Consistory order, the 1917 burning act, contemporary newspapers as printed (NYT 1917).
- **[ACCOUNT]**: a participant's own written account: Purishkevich 1918, Yusupov 1927 / 1953. These are the main sources for the night, and both men had reasons to shape the story. Always attributed in narration ("Purishkevich wrote...", "Yusupov later claimed...").
- **[REPORTED]**: historians, forensic reviewers, later press (TNA blog, Dundee 2006, AP 2000, Fontanka, MK, Mental Floss).
- **[CLAIM]**: an assertion one side makes and no record confirms (the British-agent theory; "drowned alive"; the daughter's account).
- **[DISPUTED]**: sources disagree (each version given).
- **[COMPUTED]**: our arithmetic (calendars, weekdays, time zones, sun, moon, distances). Method shown.
- **[UNKNOWN]** / **[UNVERIFIED]**: we could not read the source that would settle it; named.
- **(fetch)** = read through a page-summary tool; **(snippet)** = search-result snippet only; **(img)** = read by eye from the document image; **(RU)/(DE)** = translated by us.

**Content flags for the edit (hard rules):**
- **No corpse, ever.** Police photographs of the body (on the ice, on the slab, the face) exist on Commons and elsewhere: **never use them, not even blurred, not even in a thumbnail** (assets.md AVOID list). No autopsy imagery, no re-enactment of shooting a man, no wax figures (the Yusupov Palace basement has a wax-figure tableau: excluded, see assets.md).
- **Wounds: one neutral line.** "The autopsy found three gunshot wounds; the one to the head was fatal." Nothing more about what the bullets did. **Do not narrate** Purishkevich's kick to the head or the post-mortem injuries in Kosorotov's statement (editor's note only).
- **Women and girls: narration only, never an image.** The Empress Alexandra, her daughters, Anna Vyrubova, Rasputin's daughters Maria and Varvara, his wife, Princess Irina Yusupova (the "bait"), Khioniya Guseva (who stabbed him in 1914), Purishkevich's wife (a nurse on his train). Many Rasputin photos include women or children: all such files are in the assets.md AVOID list. The Empress's letters and telegrams appear as **on-screen text**, not as her photo.
- **No judgement words** in the narrator's voice ("mad monk", "evil", "lecher", "patriots", "murderers' heroic deed"). Quote such words only inside an attributed quote. "Mad monk" was a nickname; Rasputin was never a monk [REPORTED: he was a peasant pilgrim and lay "starets"; NYT 1917 calls him "monk", which is the period press usage].
- **"Drowned alive" and "poisoned" are claims, not facts** (section 9).
- **The British angle is a theory**: say "some historians believe"; never "MI6 killed Rasputin" (section 10).
- **Religious respect**: no mockery of the Orthodox icons, prayer, or Rasputin's faith; no "demonic" music stingers over religious images.

## 4. Backstory: why five men decided to kill him (about 2 min of script)

1. **Who he was.** Born 1869 in Pokrovskoye, a Siberian village on the Tura River; in 1892 a religious calling sent him wandering across Russia; by 1905 he was received by the imperial family [REPORTED: TNABLOG; NYT05 snippet]. The heir, Alexei, had haemophilia; Rasputin "seemed to be able to stop the bleeding... How he did this is still not fully understood" [REPORTED: TNABLOG]. Visual: Prokudin-Gorsky's 1912 colour photo of Pokrovskoye (assets C4).
2. **The palace kept watch on him early.** On 27 Sep 1912 (OS) an order "on His Imperial Majesty's decree" told the Pokrovskoye priest to send, in secret, accurate information on Rasputin's "life, activity and teaching" [RECORD: DEC1912, read on the image].
3. **War and influence.** With the Tsar at army headquarters from 1915 and the Empress in charge at home, Rasputin's enemies blamed him for ministers rising and falling; British files record his part in the dismissal of Foreign Minister Sazonov and of Samarin [REPORTED: TNABLOG citing FO 800/75].
4. **The speech.** Sat 19 Nov 1916 (OS) / 2 Dec (NS): Purishkevich, a monarchist, told the Duma that "dark forces" surrounded the throne and that ministers had become puppets whose strings Rasputin held [ACCOUNT: PUR18, entry of 19 Nov]. The next day Yusupov asked to see him; on 21 Nov at 9 a.m. he came [PUR18].
5. **The group.** On 22 Nov, at Yusupov's house: Purishkevich (arrived 8 p.m.), "Lieutenant S." (Sukhotin) half an hour later, then Grand Duke Dmitri. They needed a fifth man to drive; Purishkevich proposed his own train doctor, Lazovert [ACCOUNT: PUR18].
6. **The plan, as written on 24 Nov** [ACCOUNT: PUR18]: everyone at Yusupov's at midnight; at 12:30 Yusupov drives to fetch Rasputin; the car pulls right up to the courtyard door so no one sees who gets out; the poison in the Madeira should work "in ten to fifteen minutes"; the body to be taken out of the city and dropped into the river. On 26 Nov Purishkevich inspected the basement with Yusupov [PUR18].
7. **The bait.** Rasputin was invited to meet Yusupov's wife Irina (Purishkevich: "the countess"); she was in fact away [ACCOUNT: PUR18; YUS27]. Buchanan heard it differently: the lure was "the prospect of meeting one or two well known Demimondaines" [RECORD: FO1976]. Narration only.
8. **Why the date.** Purishkevich wanted it done before he left for the front: he invited Duma members to inspect his train at the Warsaw Station at 9 a.m. on 17 Dec, the morning after [ACCOUNT: PUR18, 10 Dec entry].
9. **The British knew something.** "I was told about a week ago by a friend who is in close touch with some of the younger Grand Dukes that a number of young officers had sworn to kill him before the end of the year" [RECORD: FO1976, Buchanan, 31 Dec 1916].

## 9. Poison, drowning, and the autopsy (what the record shows / doesn't)

- **No poison found.** The autopsy found "no traces of poison" [REPORTED: TNABLOG; MF; FONT16]. Possible explanations, all unproven: the cyanide was neutralised (a popular theory), it was never cyanide (Lazovert's later claim of a harmless substance [REPORTED: TNABLOG]), or the cakes story is invented (Radzinsky: Rasputin ate no sweets; "he decided to create a thriller" [REPORTED: AP2000]).
- **The wounds.** Three gunshot wounds; Kosorotov's statement as quoted: a shot "almost point-blank" through the stomach and liver; a shot in the back damaging the right kidney; and a shot "point-blank, in the forehead, probably to someone already dying or dead" [REPORTED: KOS17 via RUWIKI; FONT16 says Kosorotov described two close-range shots with soot and one from a distance]. **Narrate in one line only.**
- **"Drowned alive" is DISPUTED.**
  - Against: Kosorotov, as quoted: lungs "not distended", "no water and no frothy fluid" in the airways; "thrown into the water already dead" [REPORTED: RUWIKI citing Platonov 2001]. TNA blog: "no water in his lungs" [REPORTED]. Mental Floss reports that the account Douglas Smith cites says the same [REPORTED: MF]. ## 10. The British angle (about 45 s; THEORY, labelled as such)

- **What is on paper.** Buchanan heard of the plot "about a week ago" (FO1976); the Tsar later told Buchanan he had heard British agents were involved, and Buchanan strongly denied it [REPORTED: TNABLOG]. Many in Russia, Sweden and Germany believed it at the time; the supposed motive: Rasputin was against the war and Britain feared a separate peace [REPORTED: TNABLOG].
- **Oswald Rayner**, a British intelligence officer in Petrograd and Yusupov's friend from Oxford, is named by Andrew Cook (*To Kill Rasputin*, 2005) as possibly present, "possibly even firing the fatal shot" [REPORTED: TNABLOG; COOK05 not read]. Rayner later translated Yusupov's 1927 memoir [RECORD: YUS27 title page].
- **The forensic argument.** For Cook, Prof. Derrick Pounder (Dundee) concluded in 2006 that the fatal forehead shot came from a different weapon, "the largest calibre gun known to be present", and suggested the bullet was taken away [REPORTED: DUNDEE06]. Popular accounts say a .455 Webley (a British service revolver); Mental Floss calls that "far from certain" and says the autopsy could not identify the gun [REPORTED: MF; RUWIKI].
- **The letter.** A letter from Capt. Stephen Alley to Capt. John Scale dated 7 Jan 1917 is quoted (Cook) as saying the objective was achieved and "Rayner is attending to loose ends" [CLAIM: RUWIKI, MF; MF calls it "unauthenticated"].
- **Against.** No document in the MI6 archives links Rayner, Hoare or any British agent or diplomat to the murder [REPORTED: en.wikipedia "Oswald Rayner" (snippet), citing the official MI6 history]; Hoare called the charge absurd [REPORTED: snippet]; Smith notes the "lack of any evidence for British agents at the murder scene" [REPORTED: BBCHM16 snippet]; the TNA blog's own conclusion: Buchanan had prior knowledge, "but there is no evidence to suggest the British were involved" [REPORTED: TNABLOG].
- **Narration line:** "Some historians believe a British officer was in that courtyard. The British files show the ambassador knew a plot was coming. They do not show anyone from Britain pulling a trigger."

## 13. Sensitivity and accuracy notes

- **Never show** the police photographs of the body, the morgue, the autopsy, or any "dead Rasputin" image (assets.md AVOID). No reconstructions of a man being shot. The shots are sound only, over a dark frame or a document.
- **The wounds:** one neutral line (section 9). Do not narrate the kick to the temple, the post-mortem injuries, or the frozen state of the body.
- **Women:** no image of any woman or girl, including the Empress, her daughters, Vyrubova, Irina, Rasputin's daughters, or "admirers" photos. Many classic Rasputin photographs are group shots with women: use only the men-only files listed in assets.md, and check any crop at full size.
- **Language:** "killed", not "executed"; "the conspirators", not "the patriots"; "Rasputin", not "the mad monk" (he was not a monk). Purishkevich's diary is openly hostile to the Empress (calls her a German "evil genius"): if quoted, attribute and keep it short.
- **Children:** Rasputin's children appear in several photos (AVOID). The Tsarevich Alexei is mentioned (illness) but not shown (most Alexei photos are with his sisters or mother).
- **Calendars:** always say which calendar a date is in on screen once; the TNA blog, Buchanan and the NYT use Western dates; Purishkevich, the police and the act use Russian dates.
- **Do not present as fact:** the cyanide, the "superhuman" revival, "drowned alive", British agents, "eleven men present", the "prophetic last letter" attributed to Rasputin (its authenticity is doubted; not checked here: leave out).
