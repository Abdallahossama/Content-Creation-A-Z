# 129 Men Vanished. This Note Is All They Left: verified research

Channel: The Last Hours (US / UK / Canada audience). Format: countdown of dated moments, ~15 min, told from the original records.
Spine: the Victory Point note (28 May 1847 "All well" + 25 April 1848 margin note).
Compiled 2026-10-20. Dates are as written in the records (ship's dates, no time zone). Times are given only where a record states one. Nothing in 1845–1848 has a recorded clock time; the first clock times come from the 1859 search records.

## How to read this file

Every fact carries a status tag and one or more source IDs (section 0).

- **[CONFIRMED]**: primary record or official / academic source: the note itself (via RMG and the Canadian Museum of History), Hobson's 1859 report, McClintock's 1859 narrative, Rae's 1854 report, the London Gazette, Admiralty poster, Parks Canada, Government of Canada / UK Government, peer-reviewed papers (Arctic, Polar Record, Journal of Archaeological Science (Reports), PLOS ONE, International Journal of Osteoarchaeology), university releases about those papers, Dictionary of Canadian Biography.
- **[REPORTED]**: reputable press or secondary history (CBC, National Geographic, CNN, Dickens Journals Online summaries, Wikipedia used only as a lead).
- **[DISPUTED]**: sources disagree, or the claim is a contested interpretation. Both sides are given.
- **[INUIT-TESTIMONY]**: Inuit oral accounts as recorded by outsiders (Rae 1854, McClintock 1859, Hall 1869, Gilder/Schwatka 1879–81) or by Inuit historians (Louie Kamookak). Narrate as testimony ("Inuit told Rae that..."). These passed through interpreters, and their dates and numbers are often approximate.
- **[LEGEND]**: myth, fiction or popular error. Do not narrate as fact.
- **[COMPUTED]**: our own arithmetic from confirmed inputs (unit conversions, day counts, weekdays). Safe if worded "about".

Rules kept: no invented numbers, dates, quotes or sources. Quotes below are only from public-domain records (1846–1881). Modern sources are paraphrased.

Content flags for the edit:
- Human remains: do NOT show the Beechey Island bodies, skulls, bones, or the 3D scan of Fitzjames's jaw. Describe findings in words only.
- Women: no images of Lady Jane Franklin or any woman (including the Inuit interpreter Taqulittuq and the elderly woman McClintock met). They appear only as narration context.
- Cannibalism: keep wording factual (see section 15 for safe lines).

## 2. The headcount: 129 vs 128 vs 134 (which number to use)

| Figure | Where it comes from | Status | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| **134** | Everyone who left England on 19 May 1845 ("one hundred and thirty-four individuals left the United Kingdom", McClintock, citing the Admiralty) | CONFIRMED | MCC, PC-EXP |
| **5 sent home** | From HMS *Terror*: John Brown (able seaman), Robert Carr (armourer), James Elliot (sailmaker), William Aitken (marine). From HMS *Erebus*: Thomas Birt (armourer). One went home in HMS *Rattler*, four in the transport *Barretto Junior* | CONFIRMED | MCC |
| **129** | The men who sailed on from Greenland into the Arctic, and all died. Matches the 1848 note exactly: 105 survivors + 24 dead = 129 | CONFIRMED | MCC, VP, STE21 ("129 officers and crew who sailed and died"), CBC17 |
| **128** | Appears in some sources (e.g. Swanston et al. 2018 abstract: "128 officers and men"); likely counts the men *under* Franklin, excluding Franklin himself | DISPUTED (minor) | SWA18 |
| **138** | McClintock's first (wrong) assumption in 1859, which made "9 individuals unaccounted for"; he corrected it to 134/129 in his Conclusion | CONFIRMED (as his error) | MCC |
| Canada Gazette says the ships "set out with a total complement of 129" from Greenhithe | Loose wording (129 is the post-Greenland number) | DISPUTED (minor) | CG15 |
| 24 officers + 110 men = 134 | Common breakdown; not checked against a muster roll this session | REPORTED | WIKI and secondary |

**Use 129** for "the men who vanished." Use 134 only for "left England." Avoid 128 and 138.
Optional observation (only if the 24-officer figure is checked against a muster first): the 5 invalids were all ratings, so the 129 would be 24 officers + 105 men; by April 1848, 9 of 24 officers had died versus 15 of 105 men. [COMPUTED from REPORTED; verify before use]

### 5.1 The object

### 5.6 How it was found (Hobson's own words, 1859, public domain)
- Morning of 6 May 1859: a small cairn with a broken pickaxe and an empty canister. Then, "After marching from three and a half to four miles in a southerly direction from this place, a large cairn was seen close to which we pitched our tent at 11:15 AM." [CONFIRMED: HOB]
- "A small cylinder was soon discovered among some loose stones that had evidently fallen from the top of the cairn." [CONFIRMED: HOB]
- "I of course brought away the original and left a copy of the document." [CONFIRMED: HOB]
- Around the cairn: "a vast quantity of clothing, 4 sets of heavy boats' coppers, pickaxes, shovels, fragments of oars, boat hook, stoves, &c."; Hobson's reading: on landing, everything thought superfluous was thrown away. Also a sextant engraved "Frederick Hornby R. N." and a gun-case plate "C. F. Osmer, R. N." [CONFIRMED: HOB]
- He stayed until 11 a.m. on 7 May. [CONFIRMED: HOB]
- McClintock learned of it from a note Hobson left in a cairn about 12 miles past Cape Herschel: Hobson "had found a record—the record so ardently sought for". [CONFIRMED: MCC]

### 5.7 Title check
The note is not literally "all they left": there is a second 1847 copy (Back Bay), the Peglar papers, graves, relics and the two wrecks. Park (UW26) describes the Peglar papers as almost the only written documents from the expedition ever found. Safe framing: "the only message that tells us what happened to them" or "their last official word." [CONFIRMED basis: HOB, MCC, UW26]

## 9. Causes of death: known vs debated

| Factor | Evidence | Status | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Starvation | Inuit accounts of starving men; boat held no meat or biscuit; reviewers see starvation among multiple causes | CONFIRMED (as a factor) | RAE54, MCC, BYA21 |
| Cold / exposure | Part of the multi-factor view; no single cause can be proved for most individuals | CONFIRMED (as a review's conclusion) | BYA21 |
| Disease (TB, pneumonia) | Beechey burials: TB signs, Torrington probably pneumonia; Hartnell TB | CONFIRMED (for those men) | RMG-M, CHR17 |
| Scurvy | Long suspected; Hobson's own search party got scurvy in 1859 (shows the risk); skeletal evidence debated: Keenleyside 1997 supports Beattie's earlier conclusions; Mays, Maat & de Boer (2015) "reconsider" it | DISPUTED | HOB, KEEN97, MMB15 (title only) |
| Lead: the 1980s–90s theory | High lead in Beechey and King William Island remains; isotopes matched tin-can solder; Keenleyside: lead "contributed" | CONFIRMED (that lead was elevated) | KOW89, KOW91, KEEN97 |
| Lead: newer studies | **2014/15**: lead alone may not explain the disaster; 19th-century Britain was lead-heavy; some men may have been more vulnerable combined with poor diet (Millar et al.). **2016**: Hartnell's nails show normal lead until his last weeks (Christensen et al.). **2018**: bone microstructure shows no unusually high exposure versus a Royal Navy cemetery in Antigua; does not support lead as pivotal (Swanston et al.) | DISPUTED | MIL15, CHR17, SWA18 |
| Lead source | Tin-can solder (Kowal 1991) vs. the ships' water/distilling system (Battersby 2008, not read this session) | DISPUTED | KOW91; Battersby via secondary |
| Zinc deficiency | Proposed from Hartnell's nails; criticized (nails as a marker; navy rations probably adequate) | DISPUTED | CHR17; critique REPORTED |
| Botulism | One 2003 hypothesis paper | DISPUTED (speculative) | HOR03 |
| Survival cannibalism | Cut marks on about a quarter of NgLj-2 remains (1997); first cut-marked bone found by Beattie in 1981; possible "end-stage" marrow extraction (broken, pot-polished bone ends, interpreted tentatively); Fitzjames's jaw has multiple cut marks (2024); the four men identified in 2026 show none | CONFIRMED (for those remains) | KEEN97, BS83, MB16, STE24, UW26 |
| Whether anyone was killed for food | Cannot be determined from the bones | CONFIRMED (review) | BYA21 |

Narration line: "Most researchers now see no single killer: hunger, cold, disease and scurvy together. Lead was once the favorite answer; newer studies doubt it played a big role."

## 14. Hook facts (strongest documented details)

1. The same sheet of paper says "All well" (May 1847) and, in the margins 11 months later, "deserted" and "105 souls". [CONFIRMED: VP]
2. Franklin died 14 days after the "All well" date. [COMPUTED from VP]
3. "Monday 24th May 1847" is a real Monday: the men writing it were still keeping a careful calendar. [COMPUTED]
4. They even got their own winter wrong ("1846-7" for 1845–46). [CONFIRMED: MCC, HOB]
5. Gore signed in 1847 as a lieutenant; a year later he is "the late Commander Gore". [CONFIRMED: VP, MCC]
6. The last line, added by Crozier: "And start on tomorrow 26th for Backs Fish River." After that, silence. [CONFIRMED: VP, MCC]
7. 6 May 1859, 11:15 a.m.: Hobson pitches his tent by the cairn; the tin lies among fallen stones. [CONFIRMED: HOB]
8. The boat: 1,400 lb on a sledge, with silver forks, a novel, soap, slippers, 40 lb of chocolate, and no meat. [CONFIRMED: MCC]
9. Two guns standing upright, one barrel still loaded. [CONFIRMED: MCC, HOB]
10. A skeleton found at midnight in a steward's clothes, carrying another man's papers: 167 years later, DNA says the papers were his own. [CONFIRMED: MCC, STE26b]
11. An elderly Inuk woman, 1859: "they fell down and died as they walked along." [INUIT-TESTIMONY: MCC]
12. Erebus was found in just 36 ft of water, 166 years after the crews walked away, in an area Inuit testimony had pointed searchers toward. (When it sank is unknown.) [CONFIRMED: PC-SUM, PC-SRCH; COMPUTED years]
13. Terror was found because one Inuk hunter remembered hugging a mast sticking out of the sea ice. [REPORTED: CBC16]
14. On Terror, one door on the lower deck is still closed: the captain's sleeping cabin. [CONFIRMED: PC-19]
15. Six of 129 men have their names back. 123 have not. [CONFIRMED / COMPUTED: UW26]

## 15. Narration safety lines

- Cannibalism (factual, no gore): "Inuit told John Rae that some of the last men had been driven to eat the dead to survive. In 1997, cut marks on bones at Erebus Bay confirmed it. One of those men was Captain James Fitzjames."
- Rae's quote can be shown as a historic document with attribution (Rae, 1854), but do not dwell on it.
- Inuit testimony: always attribute ("Inuit told McClintock...", "According to testimony Hall recorded...") and say when dating is uncertain.
- Lead: "For decades, lead from tinned food was blamed. Newer studies suggest it wasn't the main killer."
- Numbers: "129 men went into the ice. None came out."
- Franklin's grave: "No one has ever found it."
- Beechey: show the headboards and landscape, never the bodies.
- Lady Franklin: narrate her role (bought the *Fox*, pushed the search); no image.
