# GLENN MILLER'S LAST HOURS: verified research (Friday 15 December 1944: about 1:45 p.m. British time, a single-engine UC-64A Norseman, serial 44-70285, lands at RAF Twinwood Farm near Bedford to pick up Lt Col Norman F. Baessell and Major Glenn Miller; **1:55 p.m.** it takes off for France with Flight Officer John R. S. Morgan at the controls; it never arrives and is never found; Mon 18 Dec: the band lands in Paris and Miller is not there; Wed 20 Dec: the Army's first written "missing" message; Fri 22 Dec: Eisenhower's cable to Washington; Sat 23 Dec: the Missing Air Crew Report No. 10770 is signed and his wife is told; **Sun 24 Dec, 6 p.m.**: the world is told)

Channel: The Last Hours (@TheLastHoursDocs; US / Canada / UK audience, 35+, mostly TV). Format: hour-by-hour countdown, told only from the record, narrated by the File Keeper. Folder 19_glenn_miller. Release Mon 14 Dec 2026 (the eve of the 82nd anniversary). Length about 10-12 min.

Spine: **Wed 13 Dec 1944** the VIP flight Bovingdon → Paris (Orly) is cancelled for weather → **Thu 14 Dec** cancelled again; at lunch at Milton Ernest Hall Lt Col Baessell offers Miller a seat on his own flight; that evening they eat together and play poker → **Fri 15 Dec, early morning** dense fog and freezing drizzle at Twinwood (250-ft ceiling) → **8:00 a.m.** pilot Morgan, at Abbots Ripton/Alconbury, orders Norseman 44-70285 serviced; asks for clearance to Villacoublay and does not get it → **noon** Twinwood's cloud lifts to 2,000-3,000 ft → **1:45 p.m.** the Norseman lands at Twinwood; engine left running → **1:55 p.m.** takeoff, runway 23 → (**2:33 p.m.**: a C-64-type aircraft reported over Beachy Head, identity disputed) → **4:50 p.m.** sunset; it never reaches France → **Sat 16 Dec** the Battle of the Bulge begins → **Mon 18 Dec** band lands at Orly; Miller missing → **Wed 20 Dec** teletype: "missing and unreported" → **Fri 22 Dec** Eisenhower's cable → **Sat 23 Dec** MACR 10770 dated; telegram to Mrs Miller → **Sun 24 Dec, 6 p.m.** SHAEF announcement (1 p.m. in New York) → **Mon 25 Dec** the Christmas broadcast goes ahead without him → **Sat 20 Jan 1945** Eighth Air Force Board of Inquiry, Bedford → 1945: declared dead after a year and a day → 1984/2001 the Lancaster theory → 2014-2017 the icing explanation (Spragg) → 2019 TIGHAR looks at a fisherman's wreck.

Compiled 6 Nov 2026. Release day Mon 14 Dec 2026 is **29,949 days (81 years, 11 months, 29 days)** after 15 Dec 1944 [COMPUTED: CAL]. The 85th anniversary is Sat 15 Dec 2029.

**Clock note (read first):**
- **Britain was on British Summer Time (UTC+1) all through the winter of 1944-45** (wartime: BST in winter, "double summer time" in summer). Checked with the IANA tz database (Europe/London, via Node.js Intl, tzdata in Node 24) [COMPUTED: TZ]. Spragg: "1:55 p.m. British Summer Time" (SPRAGG-PORTRAIT p. 43). **This matters:** the Lancaster theory's timing argument turns on whether RAF logs used GMT or local time (section 6).
- **Paris was also UTC+1** in December 1944 [COMPUTED: TZ]; the Eisenhower cable's "1800A hours" = 6 p.m. UTC+1 (military zone A) = 6 p.m. in London and Paris.
- **New York / Washington / Toronto were on Eastern War Time (UTC-4) in 1944**, not EST → **British time minus 5 hours**. Chicago (Central War Time) minus 6; Los Angeles (Pacific War Time) minus 8 [COMPUTED: TZ].
  - Norseman lands at Twinwood **1:45 p.m.** = **8:45 a.m.** in New York.
  - Takeoff **1:55 p.m.** = **8:55 a.m. Friday in New York** (5:55 a.m. in Los Angeles).
  - Beachy Head sighting (disputed) **2:33 p.m.** = 9:33 a.m. New York.
  - SHAEF announcement **6:00 p.m. Sun 24 Dec** = **1:00 p.m. Christmas Eve in New York** (10 a.m. in Los Angeles).
- **In America, Glenn Miller took off before most people had finished breakfast on Friday; the country learned he was missing on Christmas Eve, at lunchtime.** [COMPUTED]
- **Sun (COMPUTED, NOAA algorithm):** Twinwood (52.19°N 0.47°W), Fri 15 Dec 1944: sunrise 9:05 a.m., **sunset 4:50 p.m.**, end of civil dusk 5:31 p.m. (BST). Beachy Head: sunset 4:54 p.m. Villacoublay (48.77°N 2.20°E): sunset 4:55 p.m. local. **The flight had under three hours of daylight left when it took off.**
- All clock times in this file are **British (BST, UTC+1)** unless marked.

## How to read this file

Every fact carries a status tag and one or more source IDs (section 0).

- **[RECORD]**: US government records read in the original scan (MACR 10770, the OMPF pages, the teletype), and contemporary wire copy as printed (UP, 24 Dec 1944).
- **[REPORTED]**: historians and investigators (Spragg, TIGHAR, Correll), later press (BBC, Guardian, Forces News).
- **[CLAIM]**: one person's account: Fred Shaw, the fisherman, Hunton Downs, Clarence Wolfe, Udo Ulfkotte; also quotes that reach us only through Don Haynes's recollection. Never narrate as fact.
- **[DISPUTED]**: sources disagree (each version given).
- **[COMPUTED]**: our arithmetic (weekdays, time zones, sun, distances, speeds). Method shown.
- **[UNKNOWN]**: the record we read is silent; what would settle it is named.
- **(fetch)** = read through a page-summary tool. **(OCR)** = scanned newspaper text. **WIKI** = Wikipedia, lead-finder only.

Rules kept: no invented numbers, quotes or sources. US government text is public domain (long quotes allowed). Books and press: short quotes (under 15 words) or paraphrase, attributed.

**Content flags for the edit (hard rules):**
- **No Glenn Miller music, ever.** No recordings 1935-44, no AAF band broadcasts, no V-Discs, no "In the Mood"/"Moonlight Serenade" even hummed or as a cover. archive.org carries Miller records wrongly marked "public domain" (items glenn-miller-a-string-of-pearls_202312 etc.): **do not use**. Content ID will claim them. Our own score only.
- **No stills from *The Glenn Miller Story* (Universal, 1954)** or any film or TV programme. Mention the film by name only.
- **Women and girls: no images, ever.** His wife Helen, his mother, the band's guest singers, WACs, nurses: narration only. The casualty telegram is addressed to "Mrs Helen D Miller": it is a text document, allowed, but crop to the message lines if possible (assets.md).
- **No bodies, no wreck re-creations that imply we know how they died.** The crash is sound + grey water + a document.
- **Living families:** Morgan's and Baessell's relatives, and the Miller family (the estate works with the CU Boulder archive). Present every cause as a theory or as the inquiry's finding, attributed. **Never say "the pilot's fault" as fact.** See section 12.

### 4.5 Record problems (pick one version; say "about" or "accounts differ")

1. **Fog.** Early-morning dense fog is reported (TIGHAR); at takeoff the cloud base was about 2,000 ft (TIGHAR; Spragg). The 1954 film's fogbound runway is wrong per Spragg. **Script: "The morning fog had lifted into a low grey ceiling."**
2. **Who took Miller to the airfield.** Cable: a band officer who watched the takeoff. Spragg: a driver, separately. **Script: "An officer of his band watched him go."**
3. **Destination.** MACR: Bordeaux via A-42. Spragg/TIGHAR: Villacoublay (Paris). **Script: "bound for Paris"**; the MACR's "Bordeaux" can be a document beat.
4. **Authorised or not.** See 4.3. Avoid the word "illegal" or "unauthorised" as fact.
5. **The 2:33 Beachy Head sighting.** Official statement; identity doubted. Say "a single-engine plane was reported".
6. **Morgan's age.** Only CU2014 ("20"). Say "a young flight officer".
7. **When the MACR was filed.** 16 Dec (Spragg) vs 23 Dec (the document). Show the document: "23 December".

## 5. The mechanism: how a famous man can vanish for nine days

- **He flew outside the system.** His orders were for an ATC flight from Bovingdon; this was a "casual" service-command flight (Spragg: "casual"). SHAEF did not know he was on it [REPORTED: SPRAGG-PORTRAIT; NARA-BLOG24].
- **The destination did not expect them.** Villacoublay did not report the Norseman overdue (TIGHAR) [REPORTED].
- **The pilot's own unit only knew it had a missing aeroplane** and pilot; not who was aboard (Spragg) [REPORTED].
- **The next morning, the war changed.** The Ardennes offensive began on 16 Dec [REPORTED].
- **The band was the alarm.** Only when the band landed at Orly on 18 Dec did anyone ask [REPORTED].
- **Then the paperwork:** 20 Dec teletype; 22 Dec cable; 23 Dec MACR and telegram; 24 Dec 6 p.m. release [RECORD].

## 6. The theories (each one attributed; none proven)

### 6.1 The official finding: weather, disorientation, mechanical failure (icing)
- **Board of Inquiry, 20 Jan 1945** (as summarised by Spragg): disorientation into the water, or an unrecoverable mechanical failure at or below 1,500 ft, possibly carburettor icing; weather contributing [REPORTED: SPRAGG-PORTRAIT].
- **Spragg (CU Boulder):** the plane "went down in mere seconds", "likely because fuel lines from wing tanks froze" (CU2014); "The guy flew right into freezing conditions" (CU2014); the model had been "recalled due to defective carburetor heaters" (CU2014); probable causes "icing conditions in the engine, fuel line and on the wings, including carburettor heater failure or possibly a hydraulic fluid leak" (BBC2024); "This was a non-survivable accident" (CU2014); "an avoidable tragedy" (BBC2024) [REPORTED].
- **Correll:** C-64s "were prone to wing icing" (caption); archive curator Cass favours the 1945 finding of "pilot disorientation" with carburettor or wing icing as a possible factor [REPORTED: AFMAG13].
- **TIGHAR:** "a classic weather-related aviation accident driven by human factors" [REPORTED].
- **PBS *History Detectives*, "The Disappearance of Glenn Miller"** (aired 8 Jul 2014) compared later Norseman crashes; Bedford guide David Fowler (BBC2024): a single carburettor "in cold weather was subject to freezing up" [REPORTED].
- **What is NOT recorded:** the MACR itself lists weather and cause as "Unknown". The icing explanation is a reconstruction, not a recovered wreck [RECORD].

### 6.2 The Lancaster jettison theory (Fred Shaw)
- **The facts under it:** 138 Lancasters of No. 3 Group were recalled from Siegen on 15 Dec 1944 (BCWD) and dumped bombs in a designated area of the Channel south of Beachy Head on the way home (GUARD01; AFMAG13) [RECORD / REPORTED].
- **The claim:** Fred Shaw, a navigator on one of those Lancasters, said he watched the bombs burst below and saw a small high-wing monoplane, a Norseman, go into the sea. From a filmed interview quoted by the Guardian: **"There's a kite gone in"** (Shaw to his rear gunner); the gunner: **"Yes, I saw it"** [CLAIM: GUARD01].
- **When he spoke:** Guardian 2001: he made the connection after seeing *The Glenn Miller Story* in **1956**; TIGHAR, Correll: went public in **1984**; CU2014 (Spragg): first told "in a bar in South Africa in 1984" [DISPUTED].
- **His logbook** (sold at auction, Sotheby's Sussex, reportedly £19,000, 1999) records the cancelled Siegen operation and a jettison, **not** the Norseman (secondary sources only; logbook not seen) [REPORTED, unverified].
- **Support:** historian Roy Nesbit (Channel 4, *Glenn Miller's Last Flight*, 31 Dec 2001) said the jettison zone lay within a couple of miles of the shuttle route; the "one hour" gap was because "The Americans used local time, but we used Greenwich mean time" (Nesbit to the Guardian). The MoD told him, he said: "the proof is at the bottom of the Channel" [REPORTED: GUARD01]. Correll: Shaw's account "was confirmed by the pilot and the flight engineer" [REPORTED: AFMAG13].
- **Against:** (1) **Time zones:** TIGHAR says the RAF was on British Summer Time, not GMT, so Nesbit's correction runs the wrong way and "Shaw was back on the ground before the Norseman reached the Channel"; Spragg: "time would have had to shift by an hour and the small plane would have had to be 20 degrees off course" (CU2014). Our check: the UK was on BST (UTC+1) that winter [COMPUTED: TZ]. (2) **Cloud:** bombs dropped from about 5,000 ft through 8/10 cloud (TIGHAR). (3) **No report at the time** by any of the seven-man crew (TIGHAR). (4) **Jettisoned bombs were dropped unfused** (TIGHAR). (5) The Anderton sighting near Reading implies a dog-leg that puts the Norseman too late for the jettison (Spragg via FORCES17). (6) The Stinson L-1s, bombed a little after 1 p.m., may be what Shaw saw (TIGHAR) [REPORTED].
- **The Guardian/Channel 4 jettison time:** "at 1.40pm"; which clock that is in is not stated [DISPUTED].
- **Script verdict:** present as "the best-known theory", then the timing problem; never as fact. Spragg (BBC2024): Lancasters "did not accidentally cause the C-64 to crash" (his view).

- **Shot down by British anti-aircraft fire near Folkestone, 9 Sept** (Clarence Wolfe, self-published 2006): dismissed; Miller was seen in public for three months after (AFMAG13) [CLAIM].
- **Secret mission, captured and killed by Nazi agents** (Hunton Downs, *The Glenn Miller Conspiracy*, 2009) [CLAIM: AFMAG13]. ## 8. What the record shows / doesn't (3 paragraphs for the script)

**What the record shows.** On Friday, 15 December 1944, a single-engine UC-64A Norseman, serial 44-70285, of the 35th Air Depot Group left Twinwood at 1355 hours with three men aboard: Flight Officer John R. S. Morgan, Lieutenant Colonel Norman F. Baessell and Major Alton Glenn Miller. That is in the Missing Air Crew Report and in a teletype of 20 December. The plane never arrived. Five days later the Army wrote that it was "missing and unreported"; on 22 December Eisenhower's headquarters cabled Washington that "no trace of this airplane can be found"; on 23 December his wife was told; at 6 p.m. on Christmas Eve the world was told.

**What the record doesn't show.** It does not say where the plane went down, or why. The Missing Air Crew Report answers "Unknown" to the weather, to the cause and to who last saw it, and "None" to eyewitness statements and to the search. No wreck, no body and no radio call have ever been found. The 2:33 p.m. sighting over Beachy Head is in a statement written five weeks later, and the original observer's log has not been found. The Board of Inquiry of 20 January 1945 has been summarised by Dennis Spragg; we have not read it.

**What people believe, and why it stays open.** The inquiry, and Spragg's reading of it, point to low cloud, freezing air, a young pilot without a full instrument rating, and a Norseman that may have iced up and gone into the water within seconds. The best-known alternative, Fred Shaw's account of jettisoned Lancaster bombs, rests on one man's memory, told decades later, and on a clock that may be an hour out. Until something is lifted from the Channel floor, as the Ministry of Defence reportedly told Roy Nesbit, "the proof is at the bottom of the Channel."

## 12. Sensitivity notes

- **Three families.** Morgan and Baessell have living relatives; the Miller family works with the CU Boulder archive (Spragg was asked by Glenn's son Steve Miller in 2009 to answer the conspiracy books: CU2014). Treat all three men equally: name them, give each a line, list all three as lost.
- **Blame:** the Board of Inquiry named pilot disorientation as one possibility. Always attribute: "the inquiry found..." / "Spragg concludes...". Never in the File Keeper's own voice.
- **Theories are theories.** Shaw's account: a theory from one witness, told decades later. Icing: the most-supported explanation, not proven (no wreck).
- **No sensational rumours** (brothel, Nazi torture): one neutral line at most, or none.
- **Helen Miller:** narration only; the telegram as a document; no photograph.
- **Tone:** respectful; he died on active service; ABMC lists all three on the Tablets of the Missing.

## 16. Best facts for the script (15)

1. The Army's report answers "Unknown" to weather, cause, and who last saw the plane (MACR).
2. Takeoff 1355 hours: 8:55 a.m. in New York (MACR; TZ).
3. The engine never stopped at Twinwood: ten minutes on the ground (TIGHAR; AFMAG13).
4. "Where the hell are the parachutes?" (reportedly, per Haynes).
5. Under three hours of daylight left at takeoff (SUN).
6. The morning fog: 250-ft ceiling and freezing drizzle at Twinwood (TIGHAR).
7. Nobody was waiting at the other end; the airfield never reported it overdue (TIGHAR).
8. The next morning, the Battle of the Bulge began (SPRAGG-PORTRAIT).
9. Three days later his band landed in Paris and he wasn't there (SPRAGG-PORTRAIT).
10. The teletype: "missing and unreported since departure Twinwood Field 1355 hours" (RECORD).
11. Eisenhower's cable set the announcement for 6 p.m. Christmas Eve because of the Christmas broadcast (RECORD).
12. His recorded voice stayed on the air until that moment (Spragg).
13. 138 Lancasters were dumping bombs over the Channel that same afternoon (BCWD; GUARD01).
14. A teenage plane spotter's notebook: "1 Norseman going ESE" (FORCES17).
15. A fisherman says he pulled up a high-wing plane off Portland Bill and let it go (TIGHAR; BBC2019).

## 17. Hooks (5 candidates; first sentence names Glenn Miller + a concrete detail)

1. **"Glenn Miller's last flight has an official Army report. Weather: Unknown. Cause: Unknown. Last seen by: Unknown."** Then: "Friday, December 15th, 1944. 1:55 p.m." [RECORD]
2. **"Glenn Miller, the most famous bandleader in America, climbed into a single-engine plane whose engine never stopped running, and for three days, nobody noticed he was gone."** [REPORTED: TIGHAR; Spragg]
3. **"Glenn Miller reportedly shouted one question as the cabin door closed: 'Where the hell are the parachutes?'"** "...'What's the matter, Miller,' the colonel shouted back. 'Do you want to live forever?'" **(reportedly; from Don Haynes's account)** [REPORTED]
4. **"Glenn Miller's voice was still on the radio at six o'clock on Christmas Eve 1944, the moment the Army told the world he had been missing for nine days."** [RECORD (6 p.m.) / REPORTED (voice, per Spragg)]
5. **"Glenn Miller vanished over the English Channel the same afternoon 138 RAF bombers were dumping their bombs into it."** Then: "Forty years later, one of their navigators said he saw it happen." [RECORD (BCWD) / CLAIM (Shaw)]
