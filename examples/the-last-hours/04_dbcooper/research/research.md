# The Man in Seat 18E: verified research (D.B. Cooper, Flight 305)

Channel: The Last Hours (US / UK / Canada audience). Format: hour-by-hour countdown, ~14 min, told from the original records.
Spine: the clock of Wednesday 24 November 1971, as written down that night by Northwest Airlines' flight-operations desk in Minneapolis and by FBI agents in Seattle, Reno and Philadelphia.
Compiled 2026-10-20. All clock times are **Pacific Standard Time (PST)** unless marked. Conversions used: the Northwest Airlines (NWA) log is in Central time (CST), so PST = CST minus 2 h; GMT / "Zulu" minus 8 h = PST. 24 Nov 1971 was a Wednesday, the day before Thanksgiving.

## How to read this file

Every fact carries a status tag and one or more source IDs (section 0).

- **[CONFIRMED]**: primary record or official source: FBI documents in the FBI Records Vault that we opened and read page by page (cited as Vault part + PDF page + Bates number "DB Cooper-nnnnn"), FBI.gov / archives.fbi.gov pages, the FBI's photo of the ticket, the National Archives, court rulings, FAA records.
- **[REPORTED]**: reputable press or secondary history (Britannica, Smithsonian, HistoryLink, Seattle Times, AP, CNN, NPR, etc.), and claims by private researchers. Wikipedia is used only as a lead-finder.
- **[DISPUTED]**: sources disagree (both sides given), or the claim is contested.
- **[LEGEND]**: myth, folklore or popular error. Do not narrate as fact.
- **[COMPUTED]**: our own arithmetic from confirmed inputs (time differences, unit and time-zone conversions, inflation). Safe if worded "about".

Rules kept: no invented numbers, dates, quotes or sources. Long quotes come only from public-domain US government records (FBI documents, 1971–1972). Modern press is paraphrased; any press quote is under 15 words and attributed.

Important about the FBI records: in the released files the names of the crew, flight attendants and many witnesses are blacked out (FOIA exemptions b6/b7C). Where this file says "the flight attendant who sat with him", the FBI page does not print her name; secondary sources identify her as Tina Mucklow. We say so each time.

Content flags for the edit:
- **Women (hard rule): no images of any woman.** This covers the three flight attendants (Tina Mucklow, Florence Schaffner, Alice Hancock), and women in the suspect stories (e.g. Jo Weber, Marla Cooper, Barbara Dayton, McCoy's wife or daughter). They appear only in narration. Do NOT use the well-known AP/Getty photo of the Flight 305 crew talking to press: it includes the women. Safe visuals: the FBI sketches (a man), the ticket, the tie, the money, the parachute, the 727, maps, FBI documents (crop away any line that is only about a woman if needed).
- **Real people:** suspects are real people, most of them dead, with living families. Only say what the FBI or reputable sources say about them (section 14). Never "X was D.B. Cooper".
- **Copyright:** FBI documents, FBI sketches and FBI evidence photos are US government works (public domain); still check each image's credit line on FBI.gov. The French-language "Dan Cooper" comic-book covers shown in the FBI's 2009 story are copyrighted (credited there to Albert Weinberg / Le Lombard): do not show them. News photos (AP, UPI, Getty) are copyrighted: do not use.

### 5.1 Note 1: the bomb note
The original was never kept: he asked for "the note and envelope back" while circling, and the crew member said he "insisted on the return of the original note and the envelope and appeared especially careful to see that nothing of his was left behind". **No photo of the note exists. | Fact | Status | Source |
|---|---|---|
| FAA: Cooper's was "the first in a series of hijackings involving extortion"; another ransom-and-parachute hijacking followed on 24 Dec 1971, then "17 more extortion attempts on U.S. air carriers" in the next 6 months | CONFIRMED | FAA-CHRON |

## 20. Title and thumbnail caution notes

- Do NOT claim: "solved", "identified", "the real D.B. Cooper", "FBI finally names", "he survived", "he died", "body found", "his parachute found", "FBI reopens". None is supported.
- "D.B. Cooper" is fine as the case name (the FBI uses it), but not as "his real name".
- Time words: "8 hours" is the hijack to Reno (~2:58 PM to ~11:01 PM). From the note to the jump is **about 5 hours 10 minutes** (~3:00 to ~8:10 PM) [COMPUTED]. Don't put "8:13" in a title; "8:10" or "after 8 PM" is safer.
- Money: "$200,000" is safe; a "today's dollars" figure must say "about" and match section 1 (BLS CPI).
- "Only unsolved hijacking in US history": avoid in titles; it is a press claim, not an FBI one.
- Thumbnails: no flight attendants, no women. Use the FBI sketch (public domain), the ticket, the tie, a 727 rear stair, a dark sky. No AP/Getty crew photo.
- Don't name living or dead suspects in a title as "D.B. Cooper".
- "FBI files reveal..." is OK if the fact is from the Vault pages cited here.
