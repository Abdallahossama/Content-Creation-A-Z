# THE BENGHAZI STADIUM HANGING: verified research (Muammar Gaddafi's Libya; the attack on Bab al-Aziziya, Tuesday 8 May 1984; the public hanging of al-Sadiq Hamid al-Shuwehdi in Benghazi's basketball stadium, Tuesday 5 June 1984, the sixth day of Ramadan)

Channel: The Last Hours (@TheLastHoursDocs; US / Canada / UK audience). Format: day-by-day countdown with the clock times the record has, told only from the record. Folder 12_gaddafi_stadium, release date: owner to set.
Spine: Monday 16 April 1984: two students hanged at Tripoli University → Tuesday 17 April, 10 a.m.: shots from the Libyan People's Bureau in London → Sunday 6 May: three men stopped at the Tunisian border; the commando leader dies → **Tuesday 8 May, about 8:30 a.m.: the NFSL attack near Gaddafi's Bab al-Aziziya barracks; by afternoon the attackers are dead; at 6:30 p.m. Gaddafi is riding a horse** → 9-10 May: the CIA's National Intelligence Daily: "Firefight in Tripoli" → 15 May: Benghazi's Berka people's congress votes to "physically eliminate" the "agents of America" and razes the exile leader's building → 31 May: Ramadan begins → **3-10 June: eight public executions in eight days, announced one by one on Libyan radio** → **Tuesday 5 June, evening: in Benghazi's basketball stadium, before bussed-in schoolchildren and students and the TV cameras, al-Sadiq Hamid al-Shuwehdi, 30, an aeronautical engineer back from the United States, is hanged** → Wednesday 6 June, 7:30 a.m.: Tripoli radio announces it → Monday 11 June: Gaddafi: "We are capable of exporting terrorism to the heart of America" → Wednesday 13 June: Amnesty International protests → February 1985: the State Department tells Congress the purge was "unprecedented in its breadth and brutality" → July 1986: the CIA notes "lingering popular resentment over the public hanging of students during Ramadan" → 2011: Benghazi rises first; the tapes resurface; Gaddafi is killed in Sirte on Thursday 20 October 2011, 9,998 days after the hanging.
Compiled 2026-10-25.

**Clock note (read first):** Libya in May-June 1984 was on **UTC+2** (Central European Summer Time; Libya kept UTC+1 standard time with summer time from 1982 to 1990) [REPORTED: TZ (IANA Africa/Tripoli via Python zoneinfo + tzdata)]. FBIS logs Libyan broadcasts in GMT: **add 2 hours** (e.g. Tripoli radio "0530 GMT 6 Jun 84" = 7:30 a.m. Libyan time) [COMPUTED]. London on 17 April 1984 was on BST (UTC+1) [COMPUTED: TZ]. Libya's own news agency dated by the lunar month: "Tripoli, Ramadan 3, June 2" (JANA) [CONFIRMED: FBIS-0431], so **1 Ramadan 1404 = Thursday 31 May 1984** in Libya and **5 June = 6 Ramadan** [COMPUTED]. Tripoli radio itself said the Benghazi session took place on "Tuesday, 6 June, corresponding to 5 June during Ramadan" (FBIS translation; the "6" is the Ramadan day) [CONFIRMED: FBIS-0433]. IANA's historical Libyan clock rules are not certain; one clock conflict on 8 May (8:30 vs 9:30 a.m.) may come from that (4.8).

**Record note:** The best record of this story is again **American**: FBIS translations of Libyan radio and the Libyan news agency JANA (which announced each hanging); the State Department's *Country Reports on Human Rights Practices for 1984* (printed for Congress, February 1985); the CIA's National Intelligence Daily and later assessments (CREST); the Library of Congress *Libya: A Country Study* (1989); and the Congressional Record. **No US document names al-Shuwehdi or the stadium**: the US papers give the crackdown, the numbers and, in a July 1986 CIA paper, "the public hanging of students during Ramadan". The stadium, the schoolchildren, the TV and the woman who pulled his legs come from the Guardian (2011, whose reporter and Human Rights Watch saw the recovered tapes), the Daily Telegraph (2011, interview with his cousin) and Amnesty International (1985). The footage itself was **not watched** for this file (copyright and our own rules: section 18). State Department cables for 1984 are not online (NARA's AAD stops at 1979) and the FRUS volume on Libya is not published (section 19).

## How to read this file

Every fact carries a status tag and one or more source IDs (section 0).

- **[CONFIRMED]**: primary/official record: FBIS translations of Libyan state broadcasts (the Libyan government's own words), CIA/State/LoC publications, the Congressional Record, Hansard.
- **[REPORTED]**: credible secondary or second-hand: Guardian, Telegraph, Amnesty, HRW, Le Monde (via FBIS), the NFSL's own statements (via FBIS), Britannica; Wikipedia only as a lead-finder (tagged WIKI).
- **[DISPUTED]**: sources disagree (both sides given).
- **[COMPUTED]**: our arithmetic (weekdays, time zones, sun/moon, intervals, distances, sums). Method shown.
- **[LEGEND]**: popular claim not supported by the record we read. Do not narrate as fact.

Rules kept: no invented numbers, quotes or sources. Long quotes only from US government text (public domain). Libyan state texts as translated by FBIS: short quotes (Libya is a Berne country, see 18; news items are low risk). Guardian, Telegraph, Amnesty, HRW, Le Monde, Washington Post: short quotes, attributed. FBIS spellings kept where quoted ("Qadhdhafi", "ash-Shuwayri").

**Content flags for the edit (hard rules):**
- **A man was hanged in public** (and seven others in the same eight days). **No images of the hanging, the gallows, a rope or a body, ever.** No frames from the 1984 broadcast. Narration and documents only, and narration stays plain: "he was hanged". No description of the death struggle.
- **Bodies on TV, 8 May:** Libyan TV showed the dead attackers (FBIS editorial report). Narrate in one line at most ("Libyan television showed their bodies that night"); never show that FBIS page in full (assets H).
- **Torture:** Amnesty and the State Department report torture and televised confessions with "marks of torture" (HRR84; AI85). One line, attributed, no detail.
- **Women and girls: no images, ever.** The person said to have pulled his legs is a woman, **Huda Ben Amer**: narration only, attributed (3.4). WPC Yvonne Fletcher: narration only.
- **The charge is the regime's word.** Say "Libyan radio called him an agent of America" / "what the regime called". The record has strong doubt (8).
- **Children:** the crowd in the stadium reportedly included schoolchildren. Narrate; never imply any child is shown.
- **The 1984 footage is not shown** (section 18). Do not describe the competitor's video; every line comes from this file.

## 18. THE COPYRIGHT QUESTION: the 1984 Libyan TV footage

**Who made it.** Libyan state television (FBIS calls the service "Tripoli Television Service"); copies survived on four Beta video tapes kept by the family and digitised by Human Rights Watch in 2011 [REPORTED: GUARD110718]. Author = a state broadcaster (a legal person); no individual cameraman is credited.

**US law** (sources: CIRC38A read; statute and Libyan-law summaries as noted):
1. **Circular 38a** (current PDF dated 14 Oct 2026): **"Libya — Berne (Paris) Sept. 28, 1976."** Libya is a Berne country (no WTO, no other treaty) [CONFIRMED: CIRC38A].
2. **If the 1984 broadcast counts as unpublished in US law** (a broadcast is a performance, not a "publication": 17 U.S.C. § 101), 17 U.S.C. § 104(a) protects unpublished works "without regard to the nationality or domicile of the author", for 120 years from creation for a work made for hire: to the end of **2104** [REPORTED: USC17 / COMPUTED].
3. **If it counts as published in 1984**, it had no US protection then (no treaty relation in 1984; the US joined Berne in 1989), but **17 U.S.C. § 104A restored** foreign works from Berne countries that were still protected at home on the restoration date: **1 January 1996** for Libya (a Berne member since 1976) [REPORTED: COMMONS-LY; USC17]. Under Libya's Law No. 9 of 1968 a work of a legal person lasts **30 years from publication** (to about 2014), so in 1996 it was still protected in Libya → **restored**, US term 95 years from publication: to the end of **2079** [REPORTED: COMMONS-LY (Art. 20) / COMPUTED]. Only if Libya treated it as a work "limited to the mere mechanical transmission of scenery" (5 years) would it have been free in 1996; an edited broadcast with a prosecutor, a confession and commentary is unlikely to qualify.
4. **Either way it is very likely protected in the US today** (to 2079 or 2104). Its Libyan term may have expired (about 2014), which does not matter for a US channel.

**Who else holds it (Content ID risk):** the Guardian ("Libya archive reveals pictorial history of Gaddafi's brutal reign", video, 18-19 Jul 2011) and Al Jazeera (19 Jul 2011) published edited versions; HRW holds the digitised masters; copies circulate on YouTube and on sites such as Executed Today. Big publishers commonly register Content ID references. AP, Getty and Reuters: no listing found in our searches; British Pathé's search did not work for us (unreliable). A competitor's upload staying up proves nothing. [REPORTED]

**YouTube policy and our rules:** the clip shows a real execution: violent graphic content that YouTube restricts or removes, and our own hard rules forbid any execution imagery.

**Verdict (plain): NONE.** No clips, no stills, no frame grabs, no "blurred" versions, no audio from the 1984 broadcast. It is probably US-copyrighted (restored or unpublished) to 2079-2104, it sits inside other publishers' videos that can trigger Content ID, and it is a real hanging.
