Muammar Gaddafi's 1984 Benghazi basketball-stadium hanging, told from Libyan radio's own words (as translated by the U.S. government), declassified CIA and State Department files, and the UN's official record of his 2009 speech. On Tuesday, June 5, 1984, the sixth day of Ramadan, thousands of schoolchildren and students were bussed into Benghazi's basketball stadium to watch a "trial". On the court knelt al-Sadiq Hamid al-Shuwehdi, 30, an aeronautical engineer just back from studying in America. Libyan radio called him an agent of America. He was one of eight men executed in public in eight days.

This file follows it from the record: two students hanged at Tripoli University in April, the shots from Libya's embassy in London, the exiles' attack on Gaddafi's Bab al-Aziziya barracks on May 8 (and where Gaddafi was that evening), the CIA's Top Secret "Firefight in Tripoli", the Benghazi congress that voted on May 15, the State Department analyst who warned that Gaddafi "will lash out", the radio bulletins one a day, the stadium, Gaddafi's threat to export terrorism "to the heart of America", Washington's verdict, and 2011.

No frame of the 1984 Libyan TV broadcast is shown. The stadium is described from The Guardian, The Daily Telegraph and Human Rights Watch (2011) and shown with labelled illustrative footage. Photos of Gaddafi are from 1970 and 2004-2010, as labelled on screen. The 2009 UN speech is quoted from the UN's official record.
