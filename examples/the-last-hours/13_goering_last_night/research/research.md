# GÖRING'S LAST NIGHT: verified research (Nuremberg prison, Tuesday 15 October 1946, 10:45 p.m.: Hermann Göring dies of cyanide in his cell; Wednesday 16 October, 1:11-2:57 a.m.: the ten other condemned men are hanged in the prison gymnasium)

Channel: The Last Hours (@TheLastHoursDocs; US / Canada / UK audience). Format: hour-by-hour countdown with the clock times the record has, told only from the record. Folder 13_goering_last_night; slot Sun 29 Nov 2026 (PLAN.md).
Spine: **Tuesday 1 October 1946, afternoon**: "the International Military Tribunal sentences you to death by hanging" → Wed 9 - Thu 10 Oct: the Allied Control Council meets on the appeals; the condemned hear "last Thursday" that they are refused → **Fri 11 Oct: Göring dates a pencilled note to the prison commandant: he has had the poison all along** → Mon 14 Oct: told officially → **Tue 15 Oct**: he refuses his exercise; 8:00-8:30 p.m. the chaplain talks with him and refuses him communion; 9 p.m. the prison lights are dimmed; he "seemed to be sleeping" → **10:44 p.m. the guard at his door looks at his watch; 10:45 p.m. official time of death; glass in his mouth, a brass cartridge case, three pencilled notes** → midnight-1 a.m.: the others' last meal → **Wed 16 Oct, 1:11 a.m. Ribbentrop enters the gymnasium; 1:14 the first trap; 2:45 the tenth man drops; between 2:45 and 3:00 Göring's body is carried in on a stretcher and set between the gallows; 2:57 the last man pronounced dead ("Ten men in 103 minutes")** → 16 Oct: cremated in Munich; Thu 17 Oct: the ashes go into a 1 km creek → Sat 26 Oct: the four-power commission: he had it "from the beginning", nobody to blame → 1951, 1985, 2005, 2026: an ex-SS general, a book about an American lieutenant, a former guard and a new book each claim the answer. None has been proved.
Compiled 2026-10-29 (two weeks after the 80th anniversary, Thu 15 Oct 2026).

**Clock note (read first):** Nuremberg was on **Central European Time, UTC+1**, on 15-16 October 1946. German summer time that year ran **14 April to 7 October 1946**, so it had ended eight days earlier [REPORTED: TZ (IANA Europe/Berlin, tzdata 2026e via Python zoneinfo)]. The record confirms it: Andrus's official statement gave the death as "10:45 p.m. (4:45 p.m. EST yesterday)", a 6-hour gap = UTC+1 against New York on standard time [RECORD: ANDRUS-STMT via AP/CRIMSON; COMPUTED]. So: **Nuremberg = London (GMT) + 1 h = New York (EST) + 6 h = Los Angeles (PST) + 9 h.** 10:45 p.m. in Nuremberg = 9:45 p.m. London = 4:45 p.m. New York = 1:45 p.m. Los Angeles [COMPUTED: TZ]. All clock times in this file are **Nuremberg local time as given by the US Army, the four-power communiqué and the pool reporters** (INS: "1:11 a.m. Nuremberg time") unless marked. Note: 1 October 1946 (the sentences) was still on summer time, UTC+2 [COMPUTED: TZ]. 1945 dates (Ley, Conti) were on summer time too.

**Record note:** The night is told by four kinds of record: (1) **US Army words**: Col. Andrus's official statement of 16 Oct (printed by AP, the Harvard Crimson) and Chaplain Gerecke's own October 1946 report to the Army (NARA, RG 247); (2) **the four-power communiqué** (as printed in the Wiener Kurier, 16 Oct 1946); (3) **the pool reporters** chosen by lot (Kingsbury Smith, INS; AP, UP, Reuters, Time); (4) **the IMT transcripts** (Avalon Project). The US Army board's own report ("Report of Board of Proceedings in Case of Hermann Göring (Suicide)", NARA) and the four-power commission's report were **not read** (not online as far as we found; section 15). The guard's testimony is known to us only through a Russian state-run site quoting "the records of the military investigation" (RIA-NM1): treat as single-source. Several 1946 newspapers were read only as **transcriptions on the TimeGhost community forum** (PRESS-TG): good for wording, but check the scan before putting a line on screen.

## How to read this file

Every fact carries a status tag and one or more source IDs (section 0).

- **[RECORD]**: primary/official: IMT transcripts, US Army statements and reports (Andrus, Gerecke, the 1945 Army interview), the four-power communiqué, Hansard, NARA.
- **[REPORTED]**: contemporaneous press (INS, AP, UP, Reuters, Time, Telegraph, Wiener Kurier) and reputable later reporting/books.
- **[CLAIM]**: one person's later claim (Bach-Zelewski 1951, Swearingen/Wheelis 1985, Stivers 2005, Hooley 2026). Never narrate as fact.
- **[DISPUTED]**: sources disagree (each version given; none picked unless one is clearly the record).
- **[COMPUTED]**: our arithmetic (weekdays, time zones, sun/moon, intervals, distances). Method shown.
- **[LEGEND]**: popular claim not supported by the record we read.
- **(snippet)** = seen only in a search-result snippet, not read on the page. **(agent)** = read on the page in an assistant pass, not re-opened by me. **(transcr.)** = read in a forum transcription of the 1946 paper.

Rules kept: no invented numbers, quotes or sources; "unknown" where the record is silent. Long quotes only from public records (IMT, US Army, Hansard). Books (Gilbert 1947, Kelley 1947, Andrus 1969, Swearingen 1985, Townsend 2014), Time, AP, INS, the Saturday Evening Post (Gerecke 1951): short quotes, attributed.

**Content flags for the edit (hard rules):**
- **No corpse imagery, ever.** US Army photographs of Göring's body and of the ten hanged men exist (Time: "Photographers took pictures of the bodies both dressed and naked") and circulate online: **never show them, not blurred, not cropped.** Do not narrate his face ("contorted", "greenish tinge"): one plain line, "he was dead".
- **No execution imagery and no execution detail.** No gallows, rope, hood or trap graphics; no description of the drop or the struggle (YouTube ads: "Graphic images or accounts of ... executions" = no ad earnings, section 13). Say "he was hanged".
- **Suicide:** say "he took cyanide" once; no "how-to" detail (dose, where to bite). The capsule story is about the hiding place, not the method.
- **Women and girls: no images, ever.** Emmy Göring (wife) and Edda Göring (young daughter) are narration only.
- **No glorification.** Göring's lines are evidence, not wisdom. Any Göring denial is answered by the record in the next line (section 6.4). Never "outsmarted", "legend", "genius".
- **Thumbnail:** no swastikas, no uniform with insignia, no Nazi flags (the 1946 dock photos show him in a plain grey jacket: use those).

### 4.5 Record problems (pick one version; say "about")

1. **The minute:** 10:44 (guard's watch, single source) / **10:45 (official)** / "about 10:30" (Gerecke 1951, memory) / "6:45 p.m. (4.45 E.S.T.)" (the Crimson's misprint: 4:45 EST = 10:45 p.m. local) / "22:44" (some later sites). **Use "10:45 p.m."**
2. **Gerecke's talk:** "between 2000 hrs and 2030 hrs" (his own report, Oct 1946) vs "around 8:30" (his 1951 article). **Use "around 8 o'clock" or "that evening".**
3. **The last meal:** 5:30 p.m. porridge (AP, 15 Oct) / 9 p.m. "potato salad, sausage" (Time) / 11:45 p.m. (RIA) / "midnight to 1 a.m." canned pork (AP, 16 Oct). **Don't give a supper time.**
4. **Told of the rejection:** Thu 10 Oct unofficially (AP) / Mon 14 Oct officially (AP) / Tue 15 Oct (RIA). **Use "by Monday they had been told officially."**
5. **First drop:** 1:11 is when Ribbentrop **entered**; 1:14 is the trap. The Telegraph's "began at one a.m. G.M.T." (= 2 a.m. local) is wrong.
6. **Last drop:** 2:45 (INS) / 2:46 (WK, RIA); last pronounced dead 2:57 (Time, AP).
7. **Container:** brass (Andrus, Papers Past) vs copper (INS, AP).
8. **Board size:** six vs three.
9. **Guard's clothing note:** RIA's Bingham says Göring lay "wearing boots, trousers and a jacket"; Kingsbury Smith saw the body in "black silk pyjamas with a blue jacket shirt over them". Possibly a translation problem. Don't describe his clothes.

## 5. The capsule

### 5.1 What the record says was found

- **Glass:** "pieces of glass in his mouth" [RECORD: ANDRUS-STMT]. Lt. Willis's family: "particles of glass on Goering's lips" [CLAIM: PBS110114].
- **The container:** "a copper cartridge shell" (INS); "a vial concealed inside in a copper tube made of an old cartridge" (AP); "a small cartridge case similar to those found before on other Nazis" and "small brass cartridge case" (Andrus, Buffalo News); "patronenartigen Behälter, der Zyankali enthielt", like the one taken from him on entering Mondorf (Andrus, WK); size "about 2 inches by ½ inch" (snippet only, not confirmed) [REPORTED / DISPUTED].
- **The same type before:** a container taken from him at Mondorf in 1945 (Andrus, WK); a vial in a can of powdered coffee "found shortly after his surrender" (AP via CN461016) [REPORTED].
- **Later:** the container (or one said to be it) was in the collection of Dr. John K. Lattimer, an Army medical officer at Nuremberg, and was offered at auction in Munich on Thu 16 Jun 2016 (estimate $28,376-39,727); reportedly sold for €26,000 [REPORTED: PFC160609; REUT160621 (agent)]. Provenance = the auction house's claim.
- **A third capsule in his luggage:** the 2005 reporting of the Army findings says another vial was found in his stored luggage [REPORTED: NZH050208 (agent)], matching his note's "third capsule" (5.2).

## 6. "They asked Göring": documented questions and answers

Copyright key: **PD** = US government or IMT official record (quote freely, cite date and page). **(C)** = copyrighted (short quote, attributed). **UNVERIFIED** = no primary source found.

### 6.1 The question the 851K video sells: "Why did Germany attack America?" (it is on record)

- **Wed 25 Jul 1945, "Ashcan" (Mondorf):** Maj. Kenneth W. Hechler, US Army Historical Division, interpreter Capt. Herbert R. Sensenig. Q: **"Why did Germany declare war on the United States?"** Göring: **"I was astonished when Germany declared war on the United States."** / "We should rather have accepted a certain amount of unpleasant incidents." / "After Pearl Harbor, although we were not bound under our treaty with Japan to come to its aid" ... "Hitler said we were in effect at war already" ... / **"For this reason, a step was taken which we always regretted."** Q: "That being so, I still don't understand why you wanted war with us." A: **"The war was, in fact, already going on. It was only a question of form."** On American shipbuilding: it "surprised and upset us" [RECORD (US Army interview, PD) as quoted in HECHLER450725, **read by me**]. The article says the interview "was overlooked for more than 60 years" and does not say where the transcript is held; lead: the Army's postwar "ETHINT" historical interviews (NARA) and Hechler's report "The Enemy Side of the Hill" (a copy reportedly at the Liddell Hart Centre, snippet). Quote the Q&A words; don't copy the article's prose (C).
- **Thu 14 Mar 1946, IMT vol. 9 p. 282:** "I desired most urgently to have a bomber which, loaded with the necessary bombs, could fly to America and back" [RECORD, PD: IMT-0314 (agent)].
- **Fri 15 Mar 1946, vol. 9 pp. 344-345:** what he says he told Hitler in late 1940: "sooner or later the second great world power, the United States, will march against us" [RECORD, PD: IMT-0315 (agent; joined fragments, re-check)].
- **Thu 10 May 1945, Augsburg (Spaatz):** Q "When did you know that the Luftwaffe was losing control of the air?" A: when "the American long-range fighters were able to escort the bombers as far as Hanover, and it was not long until they got to Berlin" [RECORD, PD, **(snippet only)**: SPAATZ450510]. Read the transcript before using.

### 6.2 Courtroom beats (PD, read on Avalon)

1. **Tue 19 Mar 1946, pp. 506-507** [read by me]: JACKSON: "But were of a character which had to be kept entirely secret from foreign powers?" GÖRING: "I do not think I can recall reading beforehand the publication of the mobilization preparations of the United States." JACKSON (to the court): the witness has "an arrogant and contemptuous attitude toward the Tribunal which is giving him the trial which he never gave a living soul, nor dead ones either."
2. **Thu 21 Mar 1946, p. 627** [read by me]: GÖRING: "As an old hunter, I acted according to the principle of not dividing the bear's skin before the bear was shot." ... RUDENKO: "Luckily, this did not happen." GÖRING: **"Luckily for you."**
3. **Mon 18 Mar 1946, pp. 416-417** (agent): JACKSON on overthrowing the Weimar Republic; GÖRING: "That was, as far as I am concerned, my firm intention." ... on abolishing parliament: "We found it to be no longer necessary."
4. **Thu 21 Mar 1946, pp. 618-619** (agent): MAXWELL-FYFE: "You did not know to what degree, but you knew there was a policy that aimed at the extermination of the Jews?" GÖRING: "No, a policy of emigration, not liquidation of the Jews." **Must be answered in the next line by the record (6.4).**
5. **Sat 31 Aug 1946** (agent): "I did not want a war, nor did I bring it about." / "The German people are free of guilt." (Use only with the verdict next to it.)

### 6.3 On death, the sentence, the night

- **Sentence, 1 Oct 1946, p. 587:** "the International Military Tribunal sentences you to death by hanging" [RECORD, PD].
- Reaction in his cell that afternoon (Gilbert): **"Death!"** (one word; *Nuremberg Diary*, 1947) [REPORTED (C)].
- 15 Oct: "I have no fear, and will to the last try to show dignity" [REPORTED: TEL461016; AP461015-E paraphrase].
- His note: "None of those charged with searching ... is to be blamed, for it was practically impossible to find it" [REPORTED (translation): SANDFORD26].
- To Gerecke on the last evening: "I have never been refused communion by a German pastor" [REPORTED (C): GERECKE51].
- **Nothing in the IMT record covers the capsule or the night** (the trial had ended).

### 6.4 The record that answers his denials (use right after any Göring denial)

- **Document 710-PS**, Göring to Heydrich, 31 Jul 1941, read into the record on 20 Mar 1946 (p. 519): "for bringing about a complete solution of the Jewish question in the German sphere of influence in Europe"; Göring argued only over the translation: "'for a total solution,' not 'for a final solution.'" [RECORD, PD: IMT-0320, read by me].
- **The judgment, 1 Oct 1946:** "His guilt is unique in its enormity. The record discloses no excuses for this man." Guilty on all four counts [RECORD, PD: IMT-1001].

## 8. KNOWN vs THEORY / what nobody knows

**Known (record):** He died at 10:45 p.m. on Tuesday 15 October 1946 of potassium cyanide, with glass in his mouth, about two and a half hours before the first hanging; a cartridge-case container and three pencilled notes were found; his note says he had the poison since his capture; a four-power commission agreed he probably had it all along and blamed no guard. [RECORD / REPORTED: 4PC-COMM; ANDRUS-STMT; BEN461016; CN461026]

**Not known:** where exactly he kept it on the last day; whether anyone helped; the exact minute he bit it; the full text of the board report (NARA, not online); the date of his last family visit; the guard's full name (as far as we read).

**The payoff line:** "His note said he'd had it all along. The Army said the same. Four men later said they knew better. Not one of them has ever been proved right."
