Saddam Hussein's 1979 al-Khuld Hall purge, told from U.S. State Department cables, Iraq's own broadcasts and Saddam's 2004 FBI interview. On Sunday, July 22, 1979, six days after he became president, Saddam Hussein chaired an extraordinary meeting of his party in Baghdad. A man at the microphone confessed to a plot. As the names were read, about 66 of them by the count of the FBI agent who later watched the tape, each man was ordered to stand and was walked out of the hall. Seventeen days later, 21 men had been shot.

This file follows it from the record: the man who asked for a vote, the tape made in a minister's house, Iraq's official account of the meeting, what the FBI agent saw on the film (the cigar, the tears, the fear), Tariq Aziz and "Chemical Ali" in the room, the American diplomats cabling Washington as the arrests began, the "foreign side" Iraq never named, a court of seven that sat for six days, the morning of August 8, what the U.S. diplomats thought the real crime was, and what Saddam told the FBI in 2004: "we were all scared."

No footage of the 1979 meeting is shown. Photos of Saddam Hussein are from 2003-2004. Rooms shown are other Saddam-era buildings or illustrative, as labelled on screen.
