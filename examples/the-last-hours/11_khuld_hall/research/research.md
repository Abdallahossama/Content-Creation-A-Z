# THE AL-KHULD HALL PURGE: verified research (Saddam Hussein's extraordinary Ba'ath Party meeting, Baghdad, Sunday 22 July 1979, and the executions of Wednesday 8 August 1979)

Channel: The Last Hours (@TheLastHoursDocs; US / Canada / UK audience). Format: day-by-day countdown with the few clock times the record has, told only from the record. Folder 11_khuld_hall, release date: owner to set.
Spine: al-Bakr's resignation goes to the party (Tue 10 / Wed 11 July 1979) → the RCC session where Mashhadi asks for a vote (Thu 12 July) and is arrested → Bakr's farewell speech on the radio, 8:40 p.m. Mon 16 July; 9:09 p.m. Saddam is president → Sunday 22 July: the extraordinary meeting at al-Khuld Hall: Mashhadi's "confession", the names, men walked out one by one, the cigar, the tears, the camera → 24-31 July: the US Interests Section cables Washington in real time (arrests, rumours, "Saddam Hussein openly wept", 2,000 detained) → Sat 28 July, 8:40 p.m.: the official statement names the "plot" and a special court → court sits Wed 1 - Mon 6 August → Tue 7 August: 22 death sentences → **Wed 8 August, morning: 21 men shot by party members, one from each branch, with Saddam present** → 8 August rally; amnesty 16 August → Saddam rules until 2003; in February 2004 an FBI agent who had watched the tape asks him about it: "we were all scared."
Compiled 2026-10-24.

**Clock note (read first):** No source gives the clock time of the 22 July meeting. The "hours" in this file are broadcast and cable time groups. **Baghdad time in 1979 = UTC+3, no daylight saving** (Iraq first used DST in 1982) [REPORTED: TZ (IANA Asia/Baghdad, checked in Python zoneinfo)]. FBIS gives Iraqi broadcast times in GMT: add 3 hours (e.g. INA "1740 GMT 28 Jul" = 8:40 p.m. Baghdad) [COMPUTED]. US cable date-time groups are Zulu (GMT): "251435Z JUL 79" = 25 July, 14:35 GMT = 5:35 p.m. Baghdad [COMPUTED]. Ramadan 1399 began about **Thu 26 July 1979** (tabular calendar; local sighting may differ by a day) [COMPUTED], so the trial and the executions fell inside Ramadan (USINT noted the July holidays "are immediately followed this year by the month of Ramadan") [CONFIRMED: AAD-1543].

**Record note:** The best record of this event is now **American**: State Department telegrams from the US Interests Section (USINT) in Baghdad, released 2014 in NARA's Access to Archival Databases (AAD); FBIS translations of Baghdad radio and the Iraqi News Agency (INA); FRUS; a 1982 CIA handbook; and the **FBI's 2004 interview of Saddam Hussein (Session 8), in which the agent says he had watched the tape**. Iraqi Ba'ath Party archives (Hoover Institution) and the books of Karsh, Makiya, Coughlin, Aburish, Tripp, Batatu and Sassoon were **not read** (section 19). The hall film itself was **not watched** for this file: it is excluded on copyright grounds (section 18), and our account of what it shows comes from the FBI agent who saw it, from Saddam, and from Egyptian press of August 1979.

## How to read this file

Every fact carries a status tag and one or more source IDs (section 0).

- **[CONFIRMED]**: primary/official record: AAD State Department telegrams; FRUS; FBIS translations of Iraqi state broadcasts (the Iraqi government's own words); the FBI interview report; CIA publications.
- **[REPORTED]**: credible secondary or second-hand: rumours and informants as relayed in the cables (the cable is confirmed, the content is reported); Egyptian, Kuwaiti and Paris-Arab press as translated by FBIS/JPRS; Britannica; PBS Frontline; Wikipedia used only as a lead-finder (tagged WIKI).
- **[DISPUTED]**: sources disagree (both sides given).
- **[COMPUTED]**: our arithmetic (weekdays, time zones, sun/moon, intervals, distances, sums). Method shown.
- **[LEGEND]**: popular claim not supported by the record we read. Do not narrate as fact.

Rules kept: no invented numbers, quotes or sources. Long quotes only from US government text (public domain) or Iraqi state text as translated by FBIS. Egyptian/Arab press and modern writers: short quotes, attributed. Cables are quoted in their original capitals converted to sentence case; spelling as in the cable ("Mashadi", "Majhoub").

**Content flags for the edit (hard rules):**
- **21 men were executed** (8 Aug 1979). No images of executions or bodies, ever; narration and documents only. No description beyond what the record says ("by firing squad", "using their own weapons").
- **Torture:** one cable reports Soviet diplomats praising Muhammad Ayish's "steadfast comportment under torture" (AAD-1839). It is second-hand. If used: one line, attributed, no detail.
- **Women and girls: no images, ever.** No woman is named among the accused in any source we read. Women in the story are narration only (3.4).
- **The "plot" is the regime's word.** Say "what the regime called a plot". The record contains strong doubt (USINT, the CIA's INR summary, an Iraqi diplomat, the Interior Minister as relayed). See KNOWN vs THEORY (section 8).
- **The hall film is not shown** (section 18). Do not describe the competitor's video; build every line from this file.
- **Shia/Sunni framing:** the record notes "a Shia factor" (USINT, CIA). Keep it factual and brief; no sectarian generalisation.

### Suggested chapter clock (all defensible)
1. Cold open (section 21): Sunday 22 July 1979, al-Khuld Hall: a name is read, a man stands, guards walk him out.
2. Monday 16 July, 8:40 p.m.: Bakr's farewell on the radio; 9:09 p.m.: Saddam is president. Six days.
3. Thursday 12 July: one man asks for a vote (Mashhadi; the meeting at Ayish's house). Loop: "Remember the tape Ayish made."
4. Two lines of context: Syria and Iraq, enemies until October 1978, talking unity; the party unity question.
5. Back to the hall: the official words (A3 page), then the FBI agent's description (cigar, tears, fear, "shouted their innocence", the Himaya).
6. 24-31 July: the Americans find out (cable by cable, with Baghdad clock times): "if they have indeed taken place", "openly wept", "1500 of the party faithful", "the story Saddam wants ... to perceive as real", "over 2,000 detained", "Baghdad is calm".
7. Saturday 28 July, 8:40 p.m.: the word "plot" goes public; a court of seven. USINT: they will not "escape execution".
8. 1-7 August: six days of court; 22 sentences at 8:30 p.m.
9. Wednesday 8 August, morning: 21 shot, one comrade from each branch; Saddam present (documents + texture only).
10. The rally, the judges, the amnesty that excluded them; "55 top-level Baathis".
11. February 2004: an FBI agent who had watched the tape asks Saddam about it. "We were all scared."

## 18. THE COPYRIGHT QUESTION: the hall footage

**Who made it.** The Iraqi state / Ba'ath party filmed the session (Al-Ahram quotes Saddam: "This session is also being videotaped"); Saddam told the FBI the video "was made in order to inform the Party" and "was given to all Party members", with copies "sent to Iraqi ambassadors in other countries" [CONFIRMED: FBI-S8; REPORTED: FBIS-0808].

**US law** (sources read in an assistant pass unless marked):
1. **Circular 38a** (Copyright Office, current PDF dated 14 Oct 2026): the Iraq row reads only "**VIP July 23, 2024**" (Marrakesh Treaty for the blind); no Berne, UCC, WTO, WCT or WPPT relation [CONFIRMED: read the row]. Iraq is not a WTO member (acceding since 2004) and not in Berne (WIPO list).
2. **17 U.S.C. § 104(a):** **unpublished** works are protected "without regard to the nationality or domicile of the author." **§ 104(b):** a **published** foreign work is protected only if, "on the date of first publication", the author was a national of a treaty party or it was first published in the US or a treaty party. In 1979 Iraq was neither.
3. **§ 101 "publication"**: distribution of copies "to the public"; "A public performance or display of a work does not of itself constitute publication." Broadcast is not publication (Compendium III § 1908). A "limited publication" (copies to "a definitely selected group with a limited purpose", Compendium § 1905.1) is not publication either.
4. **So:** if the tape was only shown, broadcast, or given to party members under party discipline, it is probably **unpublished** and protected in the US for 120 years from creation (anonymous/work-for-hire term): to about **2099**. If copies counted as a general publication in Iraq in 1979, it has **no US copyright** (published in a non-treaty country; § 104A restoration does not apply because Iraq is not an "eligible country"). **Nobody can now prove which.**
5. **Future risk:** if Iraq joins Berne or the WTO while the film is still protected in Iraq, US protection could be restored automatically (§ 104A).

**Iraqi law** (Commons' summary of Law No. 3 of 1971 and CPA Order 83 of 2004): a work of a legal person (the state/party) ran 30 years from publication; still running in 2004, it was extended to 50 years: **possibly protected in Iraq until about 2029**; if treated as a "mechanical" film, PD since about 1984. Unclear. [REPORTED: COMMONS-IQ]

**Wikimedia Commons:** requires PD in **both** the US and Iraq; {{PD-Iraq}} warns "Unpublished works from these countries may be fully copyrighted." Commons hosts **five stills, no video** ("Saddam Hussein during 1979 Ba'ath Party Purge" 1-5, uploaded Sept 2025, source a YouTube upload, tag {{PD-Iraq}} only, no US tag, no deletion request yet). Commons is deleting other unproven PD-Iraq Saddam images. [REPORTED: assistant pass]

**Internet Archive:** YouTube mirrors only (e.g. `youtube-XKBN_uh7MEI`, "تسجيل كامل اجتماع قاعة الخلد 1979"; `1979_20260705`), **no licence stated**, no provenance. Hosting there proves nothing. [REPORTED: assistant pass]

**Content ID risk:** AP (incl. British Movietone): no clip of the meeting found (earliest "saddam" item 1977, then 1981). Getty (incl. ITN/BBC material): none found ("saddam purge": 189 results, none from the meeting in the first 40). Shutterstock editorial: none. British Pathé: search returned nothing for any term (unreliable). Reuters Connect: login required (not checked). **But the footage sits inside other people's documentaries** (Frontline 2000 credits "Iraqi Television" among archival sources; BBC John Simpson piece, 13 Dec 2013; an official American Heroes Channel upload "Saddam Hussein's Very Public Purge", YouTube kLUktJbp2Ug). Any of their owners could have Content ID references that match our copy. **A legal public-domain status would not stop a claim**, and claims can't be seen from outside (a competitor staying up proves nothing). [REPORTED: assistant pass]

**Verdict (plain): none.** Show **no excerpts and no stills** of the hall footage. Its US status turns on an unprovable 1979 "publication" question (unpublished = protected to ~2099), it may still be protected in Iraq to ~2029, and documentary owners can trigger Content ID either way. Stills carry the same risk as clips. Fair use is a defence, not clearance. Tell the hall scene with the **FBIS page (official date/place), the FBI agent's page (cigar, tears, fear, Himaya)**, the cables, and Pexels texture (dark auditorium, microphone, cigar smoke, VHS static).
