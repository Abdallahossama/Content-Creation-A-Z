DESCRIPTION
-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Baghdad, Saturday, December 30, 2006: at half past one in the morning, fourteen phones rang. One belonged to a judge, Munir Haddad: be at the prime minister's office at 3:30, to carry out an execution. Four days after the appeal, and the court had said it could take thirty, Saddam Hussein was handed over by the Americans who had held him for 1,113 days. This is the file, hour by hour, from the record.

You'll see how it began in a town called Dujail in 1982 (a decree, 148 names), why it was done before sunrise on the first day of Eid, who signed and who would not, the flight from Camp Cropper, the small room with a refrigerator, the green Koran he carried, the phones that should have been taken at the door, the two videos that came out of that room, and the coffin's flight north to Awja. The execution is described in one line. No footage or stills from either video, and no images of the execution or the body, are shown.

An educational documentary built from the Iraqi High Tribunal's Dujail judgment and appeal (2006), Human Rights Watch's "Judging Dujail" (2006), the US Embassy Baghdad cable 07BAGHDAD146 (2007), US government statements and briefings, and contemporary reporting.
