They Lost Their Steering Ropes in the First Minute #arctic

July 11, 1897, 1:46 p.m. Three Swedes cut a hydrogen balloon loose from an Arctic island to fly over the North Pole. Within minutes, the ropes they meant to steer with were lying on the beach. From the men's own diaries.

The full file, hour by hour: "3 Men Vanished in a Balloon. Their Film Waited 33 Years." on this channel.

36 Carrier Pigeons. Only 1 Was Ever Found. #arctic

July 15, 1897. An exhausted bird lands on the rigging of a Norwegian sealing ship. The harpooner says don't shoot it. The skipper shoots it anyway. Under its tail feather is a message from a balloon lost over the Arctic.

The full file, hour by hour: "3 Men Vanished in a Balloon. Their Film Waited 33 Years." on this channel.

Sources: G. Retzius, Ymer 1897; Med Örnen mot polen (Stockholm, 1930). Images: Tekniska museet, Royal College of Surgeons copy of Ymer (public domain). Music by Kevin MacLeod (incompetech.com), licensed under Creative Commons: By Attribution 4.0.

Their Film Sat in the Arctic Ice for 33 Years #arctic

1930. On White Island, a ship finds a canvas bag of film canisters from a balloon that vanished in 1897. The film should have been developed by 1898. In Stockholm, a photographer tries anyway. These are the frames that came back.

The full file, hour by hour: "3 Men Vanished in a Balloon. Their Film Waited 33 Years." on this channel.

Sources: John Hertzberg's report in Med Örnen mot polen (Stockholm, 1930). Images: Nils Strindberg's 1897 photographs, developed in 1930 (Tekniska museet, public domain). Music by Kevin MacLeod (incompetech.com), licensed under Creative Commons: By Attribution 4.0.
