# THE ANDRÉE BALLOON EXPEDITION: verified research (balloon *Örnen* / "The Eagle", Danes Island to White Island, 11 July – October 1897; found 6 August 1930)

Channel: The Last Hours (@TheLastHoursDocs; US / Canada / UK audience). Format: hour-by-hour countdown told only from the record, ~11-13 min. Folder 08_andree.
Spine: from the morning of **Sunday 11 July 1897** on Danes Island (Danskøya), Svalbard, through the 65-hour balloon flight, the landing on the drifting ice at 82°56'N, 83 days of marching and drifting, the landing on White Island (Kvitøya) on 5 October, the last written words on 17 October, 33 years of silence, the find by two Norwegian sealers on **Wednesday 6 August 1930**, the film developed in Stockholm that autumn, the state funeral on **Sunday 5 October 1930**, and the question that is still open: how did they die?
Compiled 2026-10-23.

**Clock note (read first):** the expedition kept **Greenwich Mean Time (GMT)**. The 1930 book *Med Örnen mot polen* converted every time in the diaries to GMT, because the men's watches disagreed: Andrée's chronometer (Kullberg 5567) ran about **52 minutes** ahead of GMT and Strindberg's (Kullberg 5566) about **9 minutes** ahead. That is why secondary sources give different clock times for the same moment (e.g. "1:55 p.m." for the lift-off = Strindberg's watch; the book's GMT is 13:46). **All 1897 times below are GMT unless marked.** Local sun time on Danes Island (10.9°E) = GMT + 44 min; on White Island (31.4°E) = GMT + 2 h 06 min; Swedish normal time = GMT + 1 h; New York (EST, no daylight saving in 1897) = GMT − 5 h. 1930 Norwegian and Swedish times are local (CET). Full table in section 2.

**Text note:** the diaries were written in Swedish (Strindberg's letters to his fiancée in shorthand). All quotes below are **our own translations from the 1930 Swedish edition** (the writers died in 1897; their words are public domain). Do **not** quote the 1931 English translation (*The Andrée Diaries*, trans. Edward Adams-Ray): a 1931 US publication stays in US copyright until 1 January 2027.

## How to read this file

Every fact carries a status tag and one or more source IDs (section 0).

- **[CONFIRMED]**: primary/official record: the expedition's own diaries, almanac, logbooks and dispatches as printed in *Med Örnen mot polen* (1930); the 1930 eyewitness chapters by the discoverers (Horn, Stubbendorff) and the Swedish-Norwegian commission's account in the same book; the 1897 pigeon-post investigation (Retzius, *Ymer* 1897, including the skipper's own statement); museum catalogue records; Norwegian Polar Institute place names.
- **[REPORTED]**: credible secondary: contemporary 1897/1930 newspapers, later scholarship (Edberg 2017, Lüdecke 2018), museum and university news (Karolinska Institutet), Swedish public broadcaster SVT, reviews. Wikipedia used only as a lead-finder and tagged WIKI.
- **[DISPUTED]**: sources disagree (both sides given).
- **[COMPUTED]**: our arithmetic from confirmed inputs (time zones, weekdays, intervals, distances, sun positions, unit conversions). Method shown.
- **[LEGEND]**: rumour, press error, novel or film invention, popular myth. Do not narrate as fact.

Rules kept: no invented numbers, quotes or sources. Diary quotes are our translations of public-domain Swedish text. Modern writers paraphrased; any modern quote under 15 words.

**Content flags for the edit (hard rules):**
- **Three men died.** Respectful tone. **No images of remains or bones, ever, and no description of the state of the bodies** beyond "found where they died" / "buried in a crevice". Several 1930 photos show remains or possible bone fragments: they are excluded or flagged in assets.md.
- **Women and girls: no images.** Strindberg's fiancée **Anna Charlier** is part of the story (letters, the locket, her later life): **narration only**. Also narration only: the Norwegian woman who found buoy No. 4 on a Finnmark beach in 1900; Bea Uusma (the Swedish doctor whose research is the main modern theory); Andrée's and Strindberg's mothers; Anna Hierta-Retzius (donor). Full list in section 18.
- **Animals:** the men shot about ten polar bears, seals and birds for food. Photos of a shot bear exist (Strindberg's own frames). Use at most one, briefly, factually ("food"), no lingering.
- **Real images only.** Strindberg's 1897 frames, 1896-97 base photos, 1930 photos without remains, real maps, NASA satellite images. No drawings presented as photos (one Tekniska item is a drawing: flagged). The famous 1930 prints were **retouched** by the developer: say so when it matters (section 11).

### Suggested chapter clock (all defensible; no backstory block)
1. Cold open (section 20): the film. Frame 1 = the balloon on the ice.
2. Sunday 11 July 1897, 3 a.m., Danes Island: the wind turns south. (One line: two years of waiting, Nobel's money, a failed summer in 1896.)
3. ~8 a.m.: "Shall we try or not?" Andrée says nothing.
4. 1:46 p.m. Greenwich: "Cut everywhere!" The dip. "The drag ropes are lying on the beach!"
5. 2:14 p.m.: the tin for Anna on Vogelsang; into the cloud. (Loop: the tin.)
6. 5:36 p.m.: four pigeons. 10 p.m.: "Spirits excellent." (Loop: which message got home?)
7. Monday 12 July: the long fall; the polar buoy thrown with no message; 10:53 p.m.: "we can well face death".
8. Tuesday 13 July, 1:08 p.m.: the third pigeon post. Ice on the ropes.
9. Wednesday 14 July, 7:19 a.m.: "We jumped out of the gondola." 82°56'N. 488 miles from the Pole.
10. The march: Cape Flora; the drift wins; 4 August, the turn. (Myth beat: they had food.)
11. 17 September: land. 2 October, 5:30 a.m.: the floe breaks under "Home".
12. 5-17 October: White Island. "Snowstorm. Reconnoitring." "home 7.05 a.m."
13. 15 July 1897 (flashback): the troll bird. Greely: "will never return". 1898: a kilometre away.
14. Wednesday 6 August 1930: an aluminium lid. 
15. The film: developed 33 years late; what was retouched.
16. 5 October 1930: home. Then: nine theories, one museum box, and what is still unknown.

## 8. Known vs theory

### 8.1 Known (the record)
- The launch went wrong within minutes: the drag ropes, the steering system, were left behind. [CONFIRMED: MOMP]
- The balloon came down because of ice and wet, not a "crash"; they opened the valves themselves. [CONFIRMED: MOMP-TECH]
- They walked toward one depot, then another; the ice drifted faster than they walked. [CONFIRMED]
- They reached solid land on 5 October with food, fuel, matches, guns and ammunition. [CONFIRMED]
- They wrote almost nothing after about 7-8 October. Strindberg's last note is dated 17 October. [CONFIRMED]
- Strindberg died first and was buried by the others. [CONFIRMED]
- Nobody knows the dates of death or the cause. [CONFIRMED (unknown)]

### 8.2 The big questions MOMP itself asked in 1930 (still open)
- Why had they not used the two bear skins to lie on, or the sleeping bag? [CONFIRMED (question): MOMP p. 266]
- Why was the third sledge still loaded with the most important belongings: about to leave, or just back from a failed attempt? [CONFIRMED (question)]
- What happened between 7 and 17 October? [CONFIRMED (question)]

## 15. What nobody knows: how did they die?

**The record:** no note, no farewell letter, no diary entry describes illness or crisis. Food, fuel, matches, guns and ammunition were all there. They died within days or weeks of landing, before the polar night. Bodies cremated in 1930 without samples. [CONFIRMED: MOMP; EDBERG17]
