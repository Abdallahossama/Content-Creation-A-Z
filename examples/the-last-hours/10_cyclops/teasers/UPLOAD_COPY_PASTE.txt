Her Last Message Said Baltimore. Then She Steamed South. #usscyclops

Monday, March 4, 1918, 11:45 a.m. A Navy office in Washington stamps a short cable from Barbados: the USS Cyclops will arrive in Baltimore on the 13th. That evening she backs out of the bay and, as one witness remembered it, steams south. Baltimore is north. She is never seen again.

The Navy Told 306 Families: There Was No Bad Weather. #usscyclops

March 13, 1918: the USS Cyclops doesn't arrive in Baltimore. On April 14 the Navy sends telegrams to the families: disappearance cannot be accounted for, as there has been no bad weather nor fighting in the ship's zone. Later analysis found a front across her likely route and a gale off Virginia.

23 Years Later, Two of Her Sister Ships Vanished Too. #usscyclops

The USS Cyclops vanished in 1918. In late 1941 two of her sister ships, Proteus and Nereus, sailed from the Virgin Islands seventeen days apart. Neither was ever seen again, and no U-boat ever claimed them. In 1968 a Navy diver found an old wreck off Chesapeake Bay. He couldn't say it was the Cyclops.

The full file, hour by hour: "306 Men Vanished at Sea. Their Last Message Was About Coal." on this channel.

Her Captain Said He Was Born in San Francisco. He Wasn't. #usscyclops

Lieutenant Commander George Worley commanded the USS Cyclops from 1910. On his 1917 Navy medical form he wrote: born in San Francisco. After the ship vanished, Navy investigators reported he had been born in Germany under another name. In the middle of a war with Germany, that was enough.
