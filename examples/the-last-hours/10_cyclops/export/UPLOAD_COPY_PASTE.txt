USS Cyclops, March 1918, told from the Navy's own records. A 542-foot Navy collier carrying about 10,800 tons of manganese ore stopped at Barbados, cabled Washington that she was there "for bunker coal" and would reach Baltimore on the 13th, and sailed away. By the Navy's count, 306 people were aboard. Nothing was ever found.

This file follows her from the record: the unscheduled stop at Barbados, the coal she may not have needed, the engine that blew in January, the ore loaded at Rio, the captain who told the Navy he was born in San Francisco, the families' telegram ("no bad weather nor fighting"), the spies, squids and U-boats that weren't, Germany's answer after the war, the three explanations the record supports, her two sister ships in 1941, and the diver who went down 180 feet in 1968.
