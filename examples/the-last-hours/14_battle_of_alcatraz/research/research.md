# THE BATTLE OF ALCATRAZ: verified research (Alcatraz Island, Thursday 2 May 1946, about 2 p.m. PST: Bernard Coy breaks into the west gun gallery; Saturday 4 May, late morning: the bodies of Coy, Cretzer and Hubbard are carried out of the C Block utility corridor; Friday 3 December 1948: Shockley and Thompson are executed at San Quentin)

Channel: The Last Hours (@TheLastHoursDocs; US / Canada / UK audience). Format: hour-by-hour countdown with the clock times the record has, told only from the record. Folder 14_battle_of_alcatraz. Sequel to "What the FBI Found When 3 Men Vanished From Alcatraz" (folder 01_alcatraz, June 1962).

Spine: **Thu 2 May 1946, about 1 p.m.**: Miran Thompson gets a sick-call "lay-in" for the afternoon → **1:30 p.m.** Officer Ernest Lageson leaves the cellhouse; Officer William A. Miller is on the floor → **about 2 p.m.** Bernard Coy climbs the bars of the west gun gallery, spreads them with a tool made of stolen toilet fittings, knocks out Officer Bert Burch and takes a .30-06 Springfield rifle, a .45 pistol and the gallery keys → **2:15-2:20 p.m.** nine officers are seized and locked in cells 403 and 402 → the yard door needs **key 107**; Miller has hidden it; "Miller told him 107" → the siren → **"at about 3 p.m."** (Miller's own sworn statement) Joseph Cretzer fires into the hostage cells → **3:23 p.m.** the warden's telegram reaches the United Press: "Serious trouble." → **3:30 p.m.** a cordon of boats → **4:30 or 6:18 p.m.** U.S. Marines from Treasure Island land → evening: Officer Harold Stites is killed in the west gun gallery during the retake → late Thursday night: the hostages are carried out → **Fri 3 May**: Miller dies in hospital; grenades arrive from Benicia "on the authority of" Gen. Joseph Stilwell; a Marine warrant officer drills the cellhouse roof and drops grenades; **12:35 p.m.** a lull; the convicts phone the warden: "We'll make a deal when you throw out your guns and ammunition"; **10:30 p.m.** guards pulled back → **Sat 4 May**: Hubbard dies "around 8 o'clock" (doctor's estimate); the three bodies come out of the C Block utility corridor; **12:10 p.m.** Johnston: "It seems quite certain that no prisoner escaped." → **Sat 21 Dec 1946** verdict → **Fri 3 Dec 1948** the gas chamber → Carnes stays on the Rock (reported to 1963) → **Mon 11 Jun 1962**: three men vanish from the same cellhouse.
Compiled 2026-10-29. 80th anniversary was Sat 2 May 2026; 81st is Sun 2 May 2027 [COMPUTED: CAL].

**Clock note (read first):** In May 1946 California was on **Pacific Standard Time, UTC-8. No daylight saving.** IANA tzdata (America/Los_Angeles) has no California summer time between the end of War Time (30 Sep 1945) and **14 Mar 1948** [REPORTED: TZ]. The 1946 press confirms it: UP gives "3:23 p.m. PST", AP "about 3 p.m. PST", UP "12:30 p.m. Pst" [REPORTED: UP460502-PI; AP460503-ES; UP460503-SLT]. So: **Alcatraz 2:00 p.m. = 6:00 p.m. New York (EDT, UTC-4) = 11:00 p.m. London (BST, UTC+1)**, and = 3:00 p.m. on a modern summer Pacific clock [COMPUTED: TZ]. Watch out: Washington's *Evening Star* printed "about 11:15 a.m. today" for the attack UP timed at "about 8:15 a.m. PST", a 3-hour gap, i.e. Eastern **standard** time [COMPUTED]. **Two other clocks differ:** (1) the executions on Fri 3 Dec 1948 fell inside California's one-off daylight-saving period (14 Mar 1948 - 1 Jan 1949), so "10:04 a.m." was very likely **PDT** [COMPUTED: TZ]; (2) the 1962 escape (file 01) was on **PDT**. All clock times in this file are **Alcatraz local (PST)** as printed by the wire services, the court and the BOP, unless marked.

**Record note:** The event is told by five kinds of record: (1) **the court record**: *Shockley v. United States*, 166 F.2d 704 (9th Cir., 10 Mar 1948), which summarizes the trial evidence with clock times and cell numbers (public domain; read in full); (2) **Bureau of Prisons pages** (Miller's sworn statement; the 14-attempt list; casualty counts); (3) **Warden Johnston's own words** as printed by UP and AP: his telegrams of 2 May, his 4 May bulletin and his guided account to newsmen of 4 May; (4) **the 1946 wire stories** (UP, AP, INS) read as **OCR text in Utah Digital Newspapers** (scans exist) and as **transcriptions on the TimeGhost forum**; (5) later histories (USNI 2015; Wikipedia as lead-finder only). The BOP's own printed report, *The Alcatraz Revolt Report* (James V. Bennett, 1946; NPS GOGA 18308), exists but was **not read** (only its exhibit captions). **No FBI Vault file for 1946 was found.** Press stories of 2-3 May are full of errors (a "machine gun", "16 armed convicts", "mortars"); use them for clocks and colour, the court for facts.

## How to read this file

Every fact carries a status tag and one or more source IDs (section 0).

- **[RECORD]**: primary/official: the 9th Circuit opinion, BOP pages, NARA, NPS exhibit captions, and Warden Johnston's telegrams and bulletins **as printed** by the wire services.
- **[REPORTED]**: contemporaneous press (UP, AP, INS, Time 1946) and reputable later reporting/books.
- **[CLAIM]**: one person's later claim, or a dramatization. Never narrate as fact.
- **[DISPUTED]**: sources disagree (each version given).
- **[COMPUTED]**: our arithmetic (weekdays, time zones, sun/moon, intervals, distances). Method shown.
- **[UNKNOWN]**: the record we read is silent; what would settle it is named.
- **(OCR)** = read in the Utah Digital Newspapers OCR text (scan exists; OCR is messy). **(transcr.)** = read in a forum transcription. **(fetch)** = read through a page-summary tool, not word for word. **(snippet)** = search-result snippet only. **WIKI** = Wikipedia, used only to find leads.

Rules kept: no invented numbers, quotes or sources; "unknown" where the record is silent. Long quotes only from public records (the 9th Circuit opinion; BOP; Johnston's official bulletins are short anyway). Press and books: short quotes, attributed.

**Content flags for the edit (hard rules):**
- **No bodies, ever.** Press photos of the corridor and of the dead exist in archives; never show them. One plain line: "Three convicts were found dead in the utility corridor."
- **No wound detail.** The press printed where each officer was hit; do not narrate it. "Cretzer fired into the cells. Several officers were hit. William Miller was fatally wounded."
- **Executions:** one line, no process: "On Friday, December 3rd, 1948, Shockley and Thompson were executed in the gas chamber at San Quentin." No description of the chamber, the gas, their last minutes (the UP pool story has all of it: do not use).
- **Women and girls: no images, ever.** Widows, the jury's six women, Cretzer's ex-wife, officers' families: narration only.
- **Profanity in the record** (Shockley's "s.o.b.s"): paraphrase or bleep (section 18).
- **No glorification**: never "legend", "badass", "last stand", "went out fighting". The plan failed in its first hour.

### 4.6 Record problems (pick one version; say "about")

1. **Start time:** "about 2 p.m." (court, Burch) / "2 p.m. Thursday" (UP) / "about 3 p.m. PST" (AP) / "about 1:40" (brief; **not found**). **Use "around two in the afternoon".**
2. **Thompson's lay-in requests:** the court pairs "Monday" with April 28, but 28 Apr 1946 was a **Sunday**; Monday was the 29th. Off by one. **Say "that week".**
3. **Key 107, where it went:** passed to Burdett, who hid it in cell 404 (court) / Miller "hid ... the key in the cell's toilet" (BOP) / "threw it into an empty cell" (Johnston via AP) / "threw away the key" (Time) / Miller "held out the important key" (Baker). BOP adds "by the time the inmates found it, the escape attempt had been discovered"; the court says they "could not locate" it. **Say "Miller had hidden the key; the convicts never used it."**
4. **Hostages:** 9 (BOP; the court names nine) / 10 (AP 3 May, adds "Fred S. Roberts") / 7 (AP 6 May). **Use nine.**
5. **Who was not hit:** Lageson and Sundstrom (court; Baker) vs "injuring all but one" (AP). **Say "almost all".**
6. **The pistol:** ".45 caliber automatic pistol" / ".45 caliber Colt automatic" (court; Johnston) vs ".45 Colt revolver" (Miller's sworn statement; Johnston's 4 May bulletin). **Say "a .45 pistol".**
7. **Stites:** "shot and killed by inmate Cretzer while in this gallery" (court, from trial evidence) / "killed by friendly fire" (DeNevi 1974 p.164 via WIKI) / "killed by machine-gun bullets at the outset" (AP and UP, 3 May) / "kicked to death" (AP, 5 May) / "shot, kicked and killed" (Time). Coroner's jury: "murdered". **Say "Stites was shot and killed in the gun gallery." Never name the shooter.**
8. **Marines:** arrival "about 4:30" (a Marine) vs "6:18 p.m." (UP); number 20 / 30 / 33 / 83 / "a company" (Time) / "two platoons" (WIKI). **Say "a detachment of Marines from Treasure Island" (no number), or "about thirty" (Buckner's 33).**
9. **Grenades:** "150" (AP, Arsenault's estimate) vs "500" (USNI15). Shipment: 300 (AP) vs 1,000+ (UP). **Say "about a hundred and fifty, by the Marine major's count".**
10. **Mortars:** reported 3 May (UP, AP) but absent from both Buckner accounts, Johnston's account and the court. **Do not say mortars were used.**
11. **Wounded:** 13 / 14 / 15 / "about 18". **Say "more than a dozen officers were wounded".**
12. **Bodies found:** "09:40" (WIKI, no source) vs "We have just taken bodies" relayed 12:10 p.m. (UP). **Say "by late Saturday morning".**
13. **Duration:** 36 / 40 / "some 45" hours. **Say "nearly two days".**
14. **Ages:** Johnston 71 (Time; COMPUTED from WIKI birth date) not 72 (AP); Carnes 19 not 18 (Time); Hubbard 34 or 36; Shockley 36 (press) or 37 (WIKI birth date); Buckner 32 or 35.
15. **Shockley's arrival:** May 1938 (AP) vs 23 Sep 1938 (WIKI).
16. **The 1941 attempt:** 21 May 1941 (BOP) vs "March 1941" (INS).
17. **Carnes's sentence for 1946:** "life imprisonment" (court) vs "99 years" (NPS exhibit; the 99 years was his 1945 kidnapping term).
18. **NPS exhibit page** says the three survivors "After their convictions ... attempted escape": not found in any other source. **Do not use.**
19. **Miller's title:** "deputy warden" (UP 3 May): wrong; court: "senior officer or instructor".
20. **Coy's job:** "cleaning gun galleries" (AP) / "magazine orderly" (court) / cleaning the C Block floor (Johnston) / sweeping A, B, C (Time). **Say "a cellhouse orderly".**
21. **"Coy threw a master switch to open all cells"** (AP) vs Johnston: he used Burch's keys on D Block; "Cretzer was unable to free some of the more desperate convicts". **Say "he used the guard's keys to let out Cretzer and others".**
22. **Crimemagazine (2013)** prints "Saturday, May 6, 1946": wrong (Saturday was 4 May).

## 6. "They asked...": documented questions and answers

### 6.1 The question the whole break turned on (court record, PD)
- Coy, at the hostage cell, "demanding to know what key opened the door to the courtyard. **Miller told him 107.** The inmates were then all looking for that key." [RECORD: CA9-1948]
- Usable line: "The man with the rifle asked which key opened the yard door. Miller told him: one-oh-seven. He didn't tell him where it was."

### 6.2 The exchange just before the shooting (court record, PD; contains profanity and threats)
- Weinhold: tell your men not to go outside, "if they do they Will get hurt." Cretzer: **"you mean they will get killed, don't you?"** [RECORD: CA9-1948]
- Shockley: "We are going to kill all of you ... We won't have any testimony against us." Thompson: **"Yes, we want no living witnesses."** [RECORD: CA9-1948; profanity omitted here]
- Use: one line max, as evidence for why two men who fired no shot were sentenced to death (the court: their words "counseled murder").

### 6.3 They asked the warden (Friday)
- A convict phoned to bargain. Johnston: **"We'll make a deal when you throw out your guns and ammunition."** "Silence followed." [REPORTED: AP460504-ES] (UP's version: "The only deal I will make is for you to throw out your guns and ammunition." [UP460504-SLT])

### 6.4 They asked Coy (1937)
- Judge Elwood Hamilton, Louisville: "Do you get a thrill out of criminal activities?" Coy: **"No, I'm just hardened to it."** [REPORTED: AP460503-BIO]

### 6.5 Baker's account (AP/UP; copyrighted wording: short quotes only)
- Cretzer: "We don't need hostages ... We want the keys." [REPORTED: AP-BAKER]

## 8. KNOWN vs THEORY / what nobody knows

**Known (record):** On Thursday 2 May 1946, about 2 p.m., Bernard Coy got into the west gun gallery and took a rifle and a pistol; six convicts held nine officers in two cells; the door they needed took key 107, and they never had it; about 3 p.m. Cretzer fired into the cells; Officer Miller died the next day and Officer Stites was killed in the gallery; Marines and prison officers dropped and fired grenades; on Saturday the three armed men were found dead in the C Block utility corridor; nobody left the cellhouse. [RECORD: CA9-1948; BOP; JOHNSTON-TEL]

**Not known:** the exact minute Coy climbed (no source for "1:40"); exactly when the siren sounded; who fired the shot that killed Stites; how Coy, Cretzer and Hubbard each died (the coroner's jury: killed by guards, "justifiable homicide"; Johnston: rifle bullets for two, "A pistol bullet finished Coy"; UP: officials "believed the mutineers had killed in cold blood some of their band": do not speculate on air); how many Marines; how many grenades; whether mortars were fired; the planned route after the yard (the launch appears only in later accounts); the sentencing date; whether officers or Marines received medals for this action (none found).

**The payoff line:** "Two guns. Nine officers in two cells. One key they never had. Not one of them reached the yard."
