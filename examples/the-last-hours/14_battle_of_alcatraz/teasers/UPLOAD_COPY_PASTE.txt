He Broke Into Alcatraz's Gun Cage With Stolen Toilet Parts #alcatraz

Thursday, May 2, 1946, about 2 p.m. Above the cells ran the west gun gallery, a steel cage for one armed officer. Bernard Coy climbed the bars with a tool made from stolen toilet parts and spread two bars from five inches apart to seven. When the officer came back, Coy had a rifle and a pistol: the only two guns the convicts would ever have.

They Locked 9 Guards in 2 Cells. Then Asked for Key 107. #alcatraz

2:15 p.m., Alcatraz. Within twenty minutes, nine officers were locked inside two cells, 403 and 402. The convicts needed one more thing: the door to the yard, key number 107. Officer William Miller told them the number. He didn't tell them where it was. The court record says the inmates were all looking for the 107 key. They never found it.

A Marine Drilled Into Alcatraz's Roof and Lowered Grenades In #alcatraz

Friday, May 3, 1946. The papers reported mortars. The Marine who did the firing described something else: Warrant Officer Charles Buckner climbed onto the cell-house roof, drilled holes and lowered grenades inside, about 150 by the Marine major's count. He fired rifle grenades through the windows until noon. The explosives came on the authority of General Joseph Stilwell.
