DESCRIPTION
-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
USS Indianapolis, Monday, July 30, 1945: two torpedoes from the Japanese submarine I-58 sank the heavy cruiser in about twelve minutes. Around 800 men went into the Philippine Sea. For four days, nobody reported her missing. This is the file, hour by hour, from the U.S. Navy's own records.

You'll see the routing order that said "Zigzag at discretion of the Commanding Officer", Captain McVay's own report ("We have been hit by two torpedoes"), the decoded I-58 message American codebreakers read within hours, the one rule that kept her off the missing list, the court-of-inquiry page on the pilot who went aft to fix a broken antenna and looked down, what the record really says about the sharks, and what the man who sank her told an American court. Of 1,195 men, 316 came home.

An educational documentary told from the U.S. Navy's own records: the court of inquiry, the court-martial record, Navy dispatches, deck logs and survivors' accounts. No images of the dead or the wounded are shown.
