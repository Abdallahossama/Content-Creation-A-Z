# USS INDIANAPOLIS (CA-35): verified research (Philippine Sea, Monday 30 July 1945, about 12:05 a.m. ship's time: two torpedoes from I-58; Thursday 2 August, 11:18 a.m. King time: a patrol bomber pilot looks down and sees an oil slick; Friday 3 August, about noon: the last survivors are out of the water; Wednesday 19 December 1945: the captain is convicted; Wednesday 11 July 2001: the Navy files Congress's exoneration in his record)

Channel: The Last Hours (@TheLastHoursDocs; US / Canada / UK audience). Format: hour-by-hour countdown with the clock times the record has, told only from the record. Folder 15_uss_indianapolis. Narrator: the File Keeper.

Spine: **Mon 16 Jul 1945, about 8 a.m.** the cruiser leaves San Francisco with the uranium and gun parts of the first atomic bomb → **Thu 26 Jul** cargo landed at Tinian → **Sat 28 Jul, 9 a.m.** sails alone from Guam for Leyte, "Zigzag at discretion of the Commanding Officer" → **Sun 29 Jul, about 1 p.m.** passes LST-779, the last American ship to see her → **evening** zigzagging stops (time disputed: 2000 or ~2230) → **about 10:29 p.m. ship's time** the moon rises, three-quarters full → **11:35 p.m. ship's time** I-58 surfaces and sees "something" under the moon → **11:56 p.m. - 12:02 a.m.** six torpedoes → **Mon 30 Jul, about 12:05 a.m.** two hits on the starboard bow (0005 or 0015: DISPUTED) → **12 to 15 minutes later** she rolls over and goes down by the bow → roughly 800-900 men in the water, most with only a life jacket → **Tue 31 Jul, 11 a.m.** she is due at Leyte; nobody reports her missing → **Thu 2 Aug, 11:18 a.m. (King)** Lt (jg) Wilbur Gwinn, fixing an antenna, sees the oil slick → **5:05 p.m.** Lt Adrian Marks lands a PBY in 12-foot swells → **10:42 p.m.** USS *Cecil J. Doyle* points her searchlight at the sky → **Fri 3 Aug, by noon** all survivors picked up: **316 of 1,195** lived → **6 Aug** Hiroshima (the cargo) → **13 Dec 1945** the Japanese captain testifies at the American captain's court-martial → **19 Dec 1945** guilty of not zigzagging → **20 Feb 1946** sentence remitted → **6 Nov 1968** McVay takes his own life → **1996-2001** a sixth-grader's history project, Congress, and a line in his file → **19 Aug 2017** the wreck is found 18,000+ feet down.

Compiled 30 Oct 2026. The 81st anniversary was Thu 30 Jul 2026; the 82nd is Fri 30 Jul 2027 [COMPUTED: CAL].

**Clock note (read first; this story runs on four clocks):**
- **King (K) = UTC+10.** Guam and the Marianas. Indianapolis left Guam "0900 KING, 28 July" [RECORD: ROUTE450728; COI450813]. The Peleliu patrol planes and the rescue dispatches also used King: Gwinn took off "0910 KING", the Doyle report is all "K" [RECORD: COI450813; DOYLE-AAR; DISP].
- **"Zone minus 9½" (Item-King) = UTC+9:30. The ship's own clocks.** "Indianapolis crossed the line into -9 1/2 Item-King time the afternoon of 29 July" [RECORD: LST779 editorial note, GM2018 p.32]. McVay's action report: "(All times used are zone (-9 ½)." [RECORD: AAR450826]. The court of inquiry and the court-martial charges also use -9½ [RECORD: COI450813; NAVY451203-CH].
- **Item (I) = UTC+9.** Leyte: "ETA 1100 I, 31 July" [RECORD: ROUTE450728]. The rescue ship *Bassett*: "All times I (-9)" [RECORD: DISP]. **I-58 kept "Minus nine"** (Tokyo time) [RECORD: CM451213-HASH].
- **Home:** Washington was on Eastern War Time (UTC-4), San Francisco on Pacific War Time (UTC-7) [COMPUTED: TZ].
- Conversions [COMPUTED: TZ]: hit at **0005 ship's time Mon 30 Jul = 2335 I-58 time Sun 29 Jul = 1435 UTC Sun 29 Jul = 10:35 a.m. Sunday in New York = 7:35 a.m. Sunday in San Francisco.** In America it was still Sunday morning when she was hit. Gwinn's sighting **11:18 a.m. K, Thu 2 Aug = 10:18 a.m. Leyte time = 9:18 p.m. Wed 1 Aug in New York.**
- Two clocks inside one document disagree: the court of inquiry narrative says Gwinn sighted survivors "about 1125 ITEM", its findings say "about 1125 KING"; the 1946 Navy narrative says "11:25 A.M." and "about 10:25 A.M., Leyte time". Only **1125 King = 1025 Item** makes all of them agree; the "1125 ITEM" is an error [COMPUTED].
- **All clock times in this file give their zone.** Where a document gives none, it says "(zone not stated)".

**Record note:** The story is told by (1) **the Navy's own record**, much of it transcribed and published online by the Naval History and Heritage Command (NHHC) in 2016 ("Documents Relating to Loss of USS Indianapolis") and in its 2018 documentary history *A Grave Misfortune: The USS Indianapolis Tragedy* (Hulver and Luebke, GPO; a US government work, public domain; **read in large parts** for this file); (2) **the court-martial transcript** (Hashimoto's testimony read in full; McVay's in extract); (3) **the 1945-46 Navy press releases**; (4) **Congress**: Public Law 106-398 §545 (2000) and Public Law 115-338 (2018), both read in full at govinfo/congress.gov; (5) **Japanese records**: Hashimoto's own postwar report and attack sketch (National Institute for Defense Studies, Tokyo, via NHHC translation); (6) later histories. The two big trade books (Vincent and Vladic 2018; Stanton 2001) were **not read**; nothing in this file depends on them alone.

## How to read this file

Every fact carries a status tag and one or more source IDs (section 0).

- **[RECORD]**: primary/official: Navy reports, dispatches, court records, deck logs, Navy press releases, statutes, NHHC/NARA documents, Hashimoto's own report.
- **[REPORTED]**: contemporaneous press and reputable later reporting/books.
- **[CLAIM]**: one person's later claim, or a dramatization. Never narrate as fact.
- **[DISPUTED]**: sources disagree (each version given).
- **[COMPUTED]**: our arithmetic (weekdays, time zones, sun/moon, intervals, distances). Method shown.
- **[UNKNOWN]**: the record we read is silent; what would settle it is named.
- **(GM p.N)** = page in *A Grave Misfortune* (2018). **(fetch)** = read through a page-summary tool, not word for word. **(snippet)** = search-result snippet only. **WIKI** = Wikipedia, lead-finder only.

Rules kept: no invented numbers, quotes or sources; "unknown" where the record is silent. Long quotes only from public records (Navy, court, Congress). Press and books: paraphrase or a few words, attributed.

**Content flags for the edit (hard rules):**
- **No bodies, ever.** Rescue and recovery records describe bodies in detail (Helm, French, Marks). Never narrate it, never show it. One line: "In the days that followed, ships recovered and buried the dead at sea."
- **No wound detail.** Burns, fractures and bites are in the record; do not describe them. "Many were badly burned when the torpedoes hit. Very few of the badly hurt survived the water."
- **Sharks: no gore, no "feeding frenzy" language.** Say what the record supports (section 7, myth 1).
- **McVay's death:** one plain line, no method, no place detail: "In November 1968, Charles McVay took his own life." Optional on-screen: a support line in the description (section 13).
- **Women and girls: no images, ever.** Navy nurses (hospital ship *Tranquility*, Guam), the woman war correspondent at Peleliu, Mrs. Haynes, Ruth Donnor, the ship's 1931 sponsor, historians Sara Vladic and Lynn Vincent: narration only.
- **No *Jaws* footage, stills or audio.** Name the film; paraphrase the speech.
- **No glorification of the attack**: Hashimoto is a person in the record, not a villain or a hero.

### 4.8 Record problems (pick one version; say "about")

1. **The minute of the hit:** 0005 (McVay's report; court of inquiry) vs 0014/0015 (casualty summation; Peleliu; McVay's own 3 Aug dispatch; 1946 release; DANFS). Hashimoto's clock (23:32-23:33 at -9) converts to **0002-0003** ship's time, which supports 0005. **Say "just after midnight"; on screen "about 12:05 a.m. ship's time".**
2. **How fast she sank:** 12 min (Navy 1946) / 15 (McVay's timeline; Peleliu) / "fifteen or twenty" (court). **Say "about twelve minutes".**
3. **Hits:** 2 (McVay; court; Congress 2018) vs 3 + 1 heard (Hashimoto). **Say "two torpedoes hit".**
4. **When zigzagging stopped:** 2000 (McVay's report) vs ~2230 (Hulver; the charge's "10:30 p.m." is when the duty began). **Say "after dark she stopped zigzagging."**
5. **Aboard / dead / survived:** 1,195 / 879 / 316 (2018) vs 1,196 / 880 / 316 (1945) vs 317 survivors (older books). **Use 1,195 / 316 / "almost 880".**
6. **Down with the ship vs into the water:** ~300-400 vs ~800-900. **Say "about three hundred went down with her; around eight hundred went into the sea."**
7. **Gwinn's clock:** 11:18 (sighting) / 11:25 (message) King = 10:25 Leyte time; the court narrative's "1125 ITEM" is a slip. **Say "late Thursday morning".**
8. **Marks's pickups:** 56 / 58 / 53+1. **Say "fifty-six".**
9. **Bassett's count:** 145 / 148 / 149 / "152" (book title). **Say "about a hundred and fifty".**
10. **Where she sank:** the 1945 position (12°02'N, 134°48'E) was an estimate; LST-779's log moved the search west; the wreck's position is restricted. **Say "in the Philippine Sea, roughly halfway between Guam and Leyte".**
11. **Depth typo:** the court of inquiry says "more than 12000 fathoms" (= 72,000 ft, deeper than any ocean). The real depth is ~18,000 ft. **Never quote the 12,000 fathoms.**
12. **I-58's radio report:** 30 Jul 0048 (decrypt) vs 1 Aug 0115 (Hashimoto's memory, "Idaho class battleship"). **Say "within hours, US codebreakers read the submarine's report that it had sunk something".**
13. **The C-54 sighting time:** "1900 GCT on 30 July" (report) vs "around 1900 on 31 July" (GM intro). **Say "on one of the first nights".**
14. **Atteberry's arrival:** 2:15 p.m. (Navy 1946; Gwinn) vs "12:15" (interview transcript, apparently a slip). **Use 2:15 p.m.**
15. **Marks's arrival:** 15:50 visual contact (Marks) vs "1520" (GM intro). **Use "mid-afternoon".**
16. **Delivery date:** 26 Jul (Navy; DANFS; Congress 2018) vs "July 25, 1945" (Congress 2000, §545 (12)(D)). **Use 26 July.**
17. **"Five days in the water":** survivors were in the water from just after midnight 30 Jul to the night of 2-3 Aug / noon 3 Aug: **four to four-and-a-half days** (~83-107 h). **Say "almost four and a half days".**
18. **Hunter Scott's age:** 11 when he began (his statement); 13 in April 1999 (Sen. Smith); "12-year-old" at the hearing (GM) vs ninth-grader (his statement). **Say "a ninth-grader from Pensacola" or "a boy who started at eleven".**
19. **The wreck date:** found 18 Aug (DANFS) / announced 19 Aug 2017 (NHHC). **Say "August 2017".**
20. **McVay's birthday:** 31 Aug 1898 (WIKI/NHHC bio) vs a claim that the sinking fell on his birthday (30 Jul; tracesofwar, IrishCentral). **Do not say she sank on his birthday.**
21. **Doctor's name spelling** in the 1945 summation: "Lt. Cmdr. Lewis L. Hayes" = Haynes. **Use Haynes.**
22. **Gibson's first name:** Stuart (Navy 1946) / Stewart (court of inquiry). **Use "Lieutenant Stuart Gibson" or no first name.**

## 6. "They asked...": documented questions and answers

### 6.1 The question at the court-martial (record, PD)
- Defense counsel: "Would it have made any difference to you if the target had been zigzagging on this attack?" Hashimoto (through interpreter Cdr. Bromley): **"It would have involved no change in the method of firing the torpedoes, but some changes in maneuvering."** [RECORD: CM451213-HASH]
- "How long after sighting this target did you fire this salvo?" **"About twenty-seven minutes."** [RECORD]
- Why no kaiten? "First, because he was delayed in determining the type of target; secondly, because it was night; and thirdly, because torpedoes were considered to be sufficient." [RECORD]

### 6.2 They asked the captain (court-martial, record)
- "Will you give the court the reasons ... for not zigzagging?" McVay: he was on the bridge "when the moon rose. The visibility was poor, and conditions were such that in my opinion ... conditions were such as not to require the ship to zigzag." [RECORD: CM-McV]

### 6.3 They asked the pilot (6 Aug 1945, record)
- "What time did you spot the oil slick?" Gwinn: **"We spotted the survivors at 11:18 and got dispatch off at 11:25."** [RECORD: INT450806]
- "What was your first impression?" "I don't know—it was a funny feeling." [RECORD]

### 6.4 They asked Marks (6 Aug 1945, record)
- "What was their first reaction?" Marks: **"Their first one was to ask for water."** [RECORD: INT450806]

### 6.5 They asked the doctor (BUMED oral history)
- About sharks: Haynes saw "one shark"; **"in the entire 110 hours I was in the water I did not see a man attacked by a shark."** He added that recovery ships reported many bodies damaged. [RECORD: OH-HAYNES, GM p.83]

### 6.6 They asked Leyte (dispatch, 2 Aug)
- **"Indianapolis (CA 35) has not arrived Leyte. Advise."** [RECORD: DISP]

## 8. KNOWN vs THEORY / what remains unknown (as of October 2026)

**Known (record):** On Monday 30 July 1945, just after midnight ship's time, two torpedoes from I-58 hit Indianapolis on the starboard bow; she sank in about twelve minutes; a distress message was ordered and keyed but probably never left the ship; around 800 men went into the sea; nobody reported her overdue at Leyte on 31 July because of how a reporting rule was read; a patrol pilot found the survivors by accident on 2 August; 316 of 1,195 lived; McVay was convicted only of not zigzagging, the sentence was remitted, and in 2001 Congress's exoneration was placed in his record; the wreck lies about 18,000 feet down, found in August 2017. [RECORD: AAR450826; COI450813; NAVY460223-N; HULVER-DONNOR; ENGLAND2001; NHHC170819]

**Not known (and what would settle it):**
- **The exact minute of the hit and of the sinking** (0005 vs 0015; 12 vs 20 min). Settle: the court-of-inquiry transcript (NARA RG 38) with each witness's watch time.
- **How many went down with the ship** (~300-400) and **how many died of each cause** in the water. No count was ever possible; the bodies recovered were a fraction.
- **How many men sharks killed.** No record counts it. Unknowable now.
- **Whether any distress signal actually got out.** No receiving station found in 1945; later claims unconfirmed.
- **The wreck's exact position**: restricted by the Navy; only "west of the historic position" is public.
- **Which I-58 message is "the" report**: the 30 Jul 0048 decrypt vs Hashimoto's 1 Aug 0115 memory.
- **What the C-54 crew saw, and when** (flares from rafts? 30 or 31 Jul?).
- **The red and green lights** Haynes's group saw on the night of 30 July (Haynes thought enemy submarines). Unexplained.
- **Why King and Forrestal overruled Nimitz and Spruance**: Congress found "no explanation on the public record".
- **Whether McVay asked for an escort**: Congress says yes; the Navy's 1946 narrative does not mention a request.
- **The text and author of the 24 Nov 1999 letter** to the Senate (reported as Hashimoto's): not read.
- **The precise make-up of the cargo** (which parts were in the crate vs the canister): not in the Navy documents read.
- **Whether Harold Bray is still living** after mid-2026: official site lists him on 30 Oct 2026; no later news found.

**The payoff line:** "Two torpedoes sank her in twelve minutes. A rule about paperwork kept her missing for four days. And a broken antenna found her."
