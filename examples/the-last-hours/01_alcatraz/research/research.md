# The Night 3 Men Vanished From Alcatraz: verified research

Channel: The Last Hours (US audience). Format: hour-by-hour countdown, ~20 min.
Compiled 2026-10-19. All times are local Pacific Daylight Time (PDT, UTC-7). The FBI teletypes also use PDT.

## How to read this file

Every fact below carries a status tag and a source ID. Source IDs are defined in section 0.

- **[CONFIRMED]**: stated in a primary or official record (FBI case file, FBI history pages, BOP, USMS, NPS, NARA, US Coast Guard/Army Engineers data quoted in the FBI file).
- **[REPORTED-1962]**: printed in the 1962 newspaper record (San Francisco Chronicle). It was contemporary, but it is press, and some of it was later contradicted.
- **[SINGLE-SOURCE]**: one person's account inside the FBI file. Officials recorded it, but it has no corroboration.
- **[COMPUTED]**: computed by us (sun and moon times, straight-line distances). Fine for narration if worded as "about".
- **[DISPUTED]**: official sources disagree, or the claim is contested.
- **[LEGEND]**: family claims, TV claims, deathbed confessions, hoaxes. Narrate only as "claims".

Local copy: `_sources/fbi_vault_ocr/alcatraz_fbi_partNN_ocr.txt` holds the OCR text of all 17 FBI Vault parts (public domain), so you can search for quotes. OCR has errors; check the wording against the PDF before putting anything on screen.

Access note: fbi.gov, vault.fbi.gov and the USMS PDFs return "403 / request blocked" from this connection. The FBI history pages were read through Wayback Machine snapshots. The FBI Vault file was read through its full mirror on archive.org (same PDFs, OCR text). The USMS profile pages loaded normally in the browser.

## 6. Water, tides, light and distances

### Tides on the night (official, quoted in FBI-V5)
- US Coast Guard, Captain of the Port, San Francisco: high water 8:22 p.m. (June 11); low water 2:55 a.m. (June 12); high water 8:43 a.m. Maximum **ebb 2.7 knots at 11:46 p.m.**; maximum flood 1.8 knots at 6:44 a.m.
- US Army Engineers (Sausalito Bay model): slack at 10:00 p.m.; ebb from just after 10, peaking at midnight at ~2.7 knots; ~2 knots at 10:30–11:00 p.m.; slack again at 4:00 a.m.
- FBI summary: the bodies were "possibly carried out to sea by the outgoing tide which was existent during pertinent period."

### Temperature
- ~50°F that night (FBI-V5). The Warden told the press 54°F (SFC62). BOP gives an average of 50–55°F.
- Context, not specific to the case: the IMO table (MSC/Circ.1046) for 50–60°F water without protective clothing gives exhaustion or unconsciousness in 1–2 hours and expected survival of 1–6 hours. Cold-water safety groups say these tables understate cold shock. Narrate it as "within an hour or two, the cold starts to win" and cite it as general guidance.

## 7. Case outcome

- 1963: the FBI Investigative Summary Report (SF 76-2887) concludes that searches and inquiries "developed no information indicating that Escapees arrived on the mainland." [CONFIRMED, FBI-V11]
- FBI reasons for presuming drowning (FBI-H/FBI-B): strong currents and frigid water; no thefts of cars or clothes despite the publicity; families lacked the money to help; "for the 17 years we worked on the case, no credible evidence emerged".
- **December 31, 1979**: the FBI closes its case and turns over responsibility to the US Marshals Service. [CONFIRMED, FBI-H]
- BOP today: "Morris and the Anglins are officially listed as missing and presumed drowned." [CONFIRMED, BOP]
- USMS: the warrants and posters remain. The profile pages are live as of 2026-10-19. [CONFIRMED, USMS] Wikipedia says they "remained on its wanted list until September 2026" (Morris would have turned 100 on Sept 1, 2026). No official USMS statement confirms a removal, and the pages are still up. Tag this **[UNVERIFIED]**. Narrate: "As of this video, the US Marshals still list all three as wanted."
- The claim that the case stays open "until they would be 99/100" was **not found** in any official source. Do not use it.

## 10. Hook facts (strongest documented details)

1. "We either have a dead man or a dummy in the cell block": an officer at 7:20 a.m. Then a head "felt like it crumbled… and it rolled on the floor." (FBI FD-302)
2. At 10:30 p.m. a guard heard something like an empty oil drum being struck, three times in seven minutes. A 45-minute search found "everything… normal." (FBI FD-302)
3. The left-behind fourth man to the guards: "you may as well lock me up too, I planned the entire escape." (FBI FD-302)
4. The drill was made from the prison **vacuum cleaner** motor, plus a second drill made from **barber clippers**. They also built a periscope and a soap bolt on the roof. (FBI)
5. In 1962 officials publicly "ruled out" raincoats because inmates had no access to them. The raft used 50+ raincoats. (SFC62 vs FBI)
6. Footprints on the roof were 4 ft 1⅛ in apart: "someone apparently was running." (FBI photo caption)
7. Two days later, two Olympic Club swimmers did the crossing in 53 minutes and still had to be pulled out of the whirlpools at Angel Island. (FBI teletype)
8. A pouch of family photos, waterproofed in raincoat rubber, came up from a whirlpool half a mile from the island. (FBI)
9. Five weeks later, a Norwegian freighter passed a body in prison-like trousers 20 miles out at sea and never picked it up. (FBI)
10. 64 years later the Marshals still show three age-progressed faces under "Wanted For: Escape." (USMS)

## 11. The 3 biggest uncertainties

1. **When they launched.** The FBI says ~10 p.m. A guard heard noises at 10:30. The tide model says only a launch around 11–midnight gave any chance. Nobody saw them in the water.
2. **Whether they lived.** No bodies, no raft, no crimes; an inconclusive 2013 letter; a disputed 1975 Brazil photo; one unrecovered body seen at sea.
3. **The evidence trail.** FBI documents differ on dates by hours and on places (paddle, pouch, vests). Leads like the stolen Marin car, the Angel Island "raft" and the boat sighting were never resolved.

## 12. Narration safety lines

- "Sometime after 9:30, likely around ten, according to the FBI…"
- "The FBI never learned exactly when they hit the water."
- "Officially, they are presumed drowned. Officially, they are also still wanted."
- Never state survival or death as fact. Say "the evidence suggests" or "claims".
- Family members (mother, sisters) appear in narration only. Use objects (letters, envelopes, a Christmas card shape) instead of photos of people.
