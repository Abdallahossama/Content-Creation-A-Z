## Best 5 for the thumbnail (Hakawi style: lighthouse on a dark island at the top, three faces ghosted in front, big question text)
1. **C1, 'The Flannen Islands Light-Station'** (Talbot, *Lightships and Lighthouses*, 1913; 1898×3174 page, the photo fills most of it): the **real Flannan tower**, lantern and gallery, dressed overall in signal flags, with **three uniformed keepers standing at its foot** (unidentified; not the 1900 men). Licence-safe (anonymous 1913 agency photo, author of the book died 1924). It is printed sideways: rotate 90° clockwise. Crop the tower and lantern for the top of the thumbnail; the three small figures give the "three men" idea without claiming they are Ducat, Marshall and McArthur.
2. **H4, Eilean Mor from the East** (Eagle Clarke, 1912, photo C. Dick Peddie): **exactly the Hakawi layout**, a black island with the lighthouse standing on its crown, open sea below. Real Flannan, c.1904-11. **Needs your decision:** public domain in the US (published 1912), but the photographer's death date could not be found, so UK status is unconfirmed. If you say no, use C1 alone.
3. **E2, waves exploding on a black sea cliff** (Pexels 15023322, B&W, 1920×1280): the storm layer under the island. Backup **E1** (B&W storm swell from water level).
4. **E10, a lone rock island under a black lid of cloud** (Pexels 25853722, aerial, 1920×1279): a stand-in "dark island" base if you want colour; label it illustrative in the video, never call it Flannan.
5. **Faces: no licence-safe authentic portraits exist (see verdict).** The only period photo of the three men is H1 (NRS blog, owner unknown, published by NRS for non-commercial use): use it **only after NRS (education@nrscotland.gov.uk) agrees**. Until then, the safest "three men" element is the three keepers already in C1, or **B1** (a 1913 keeper cleaning the lamp) as an unnamed "keeper of the era".

**Keeper-portrait verdict:** no authentic, provenanced, licence-safe portrait of Ducat, Marshall, McArthur, Muirhead or Moore was found. The widely shared c.1900 group photo (captioned Ducat, Marshall, McArthur and Muirhead) is on the National Records of Scotland blog: NRS say it is probably out of copyright by date but **the owner is unknown**, and they published it "for an educational, non-commercial purpose". It is in **H1** only. No photo of Joseph Moore or Captain Harvie was found. NLB / Northern Lighthouse Heritage Trust holdings were not used.

## Hard rules applied (everything in A-F passed them)
- **No women or girls.** Every image was opened and checked by eye, zooming into anything with small figures (the flag-dressed tower in C1, the boat crew in C2, the cards on the kitchen table in C3). No keeper's wife, widow or daughter appears anywhere. Excluded: the 1881 census image naming Mary Grierson, any image from *The Vanishing* (2018) or *Doctor Who*, Pexels raincoat photos (nearly all of women), and Geograph photos with visitors.
- **Real only.** Period photographs, charts, maps, book pages and engravings; real modern photographs (Pexels, checked for AI: one 3D render excluded); NASA satellite images. No AI, no cartoons, no film stills, no modern illustrations.
- **Truth.** Stand-ins are marked "not Flannan" in the row; label them "illustrative" on screen. C1's three keepers are not identified in the source and must not be captioned as the vanished men. Talbot's 1913 account (A2) has errors (it says the last entry was "about 4 a.m." and calls the wave theory "surmised"): caption it as a 1913 retelling.
- **Copyright-safe.** No NLB archive images, no Ian Cowe photos, no Manx National Heritage/Bridgeman Hesperus photo, no British Newspaper Archive or Scotsman Archive scans. Gibson's 1912 poem is in H (UK copyright to the end of 2032).
