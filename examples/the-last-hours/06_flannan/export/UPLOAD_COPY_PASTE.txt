In December 1900, the three lighthouse keepers of the Flannan Isles, a rock 20 miles off Scotland's Isle of Lewis, vanished. The relief keeper found the gate shut, the fire cold, the clock stopped, the beds empty, and one man's coat still on its peg.

This is what the real records say happened, hour by hour: the last note chalked on a slate at 9 AM on December 15, the lamp that was never lit, the steamer that saw no light at midnight, eleven dark nights, the search, the telegram, the damage 110 feet up the west cliff, the superintendent's report, and how the famous "logbook" about crying and praying was made up decades later.
