# CROATOAN: verified research (The Lost Colony of Roanoke, John White's return, August 1590)

Channel: The Last Hours (US / UK / Canada audience). Format: hour-by-hour / day-by-day countdown told from the original records. Episode #4, release target Sat 31 Oct 2026 (Halloween).
Spine: the five days John White spent off Roanoke in August 1590, in his own words ("The fift voyage of M. Iohn White into the West Indies and parts of America called Virginia, in the yeere 1590", printed by Richard Hakluyt, *Principal Navigations*, vol. 3, London 1600, pp. 288–295), plus his covering letter to Hakluyt dated 4 February 1593.
Compiled 2026-10-21.

**Dates in this file are Old Style (Julian), exactly as White wrote them**, unless marked NS. Add 10 days for today's (Gregorian) calendar: 18 Aug 1590 OS = 28 Aug 1590 NS (section 2). **There were no clocks on the beach**: White's times are sun-and-sky cues ("towards Euening", "at day-breake", "ten of the clocke aforenoone"). Where this file gives a modern clock equivalent it is marked [COMPUTED].

## How to read this file

Every fact carries a status tag and one or more source IDs (section 0).

- **[CONFIRMED]**: primary record or official/institutional source: White's own narratives as printed by Hakluyt in 1589/1600 (we read the 1600 printing page by page via the Internet Archive scan, and the Gutenberg reprint), the National Park Service (Fort Raleigh National Historic Site), the British Museum (map facts only), First Colony Foundation, Encyclopedia Virginia, NCpedia, peer-reviewed papers.
- **[REPORTED]**: reputable press or secondary history, and claims by researchers or groups. Wikipedia is used only as a lead-finder.
- **[DISPUTED]**: sources disagree (both sides given), or the claim is contested by specialists.
- **[LEGEND]**: myth, folklore, hoax or popular error. Do not narrate as fact.
- **[COMPUTED]**: our own arithmetic from confirmed inputs (calendar conversion, weekdays, day counts, sun and moon times, unit conversions). Safe if worded "about".

Rules kept: no invented numbers, dates, quotes or sources. Long quotes come only from public-domain texts (White 1587/1590/1593, Lane, Barlowe, Hakluyt, Strachey, Smith). Modern press and modern historians are paraphrased; any modern quote is under 15 words and attributed.

Content flags for the edit:
- **Women and girls (hard rule): no images of any woman or girl.** That includes Eleanor (Elyoner) Dare, Virginia Dare (a baby girl), Margery Harvie and the other 15 women of 1587, Queen Elizabeth I, Native women in John White's watercolours and de Bry's engravings (e.g. White's "wife of a chief of Pomeiooc and her daughter"), the Virginia Dare statue (Elizabethan Gardens), the 1937 Roanoke Island half dollar reverse (Eleanor holding Virginia), the 1937 5-cent Virginia Dare stamp, photos of *The Lost Colony* outdoor drama, and any "Virginia Dare" brand art. They may appear **in narration only** (facts). Full list in section 18.
- **Native peoples:** the Roanoke, Croatoan (later called Hatteras), Secotan, Chowanoke, Weapemeoc and Powhatan were real nations with living descendants and present-day communities. Use their names, not "savages" (White's word: quote it only inside a period quote, and consider cutting it). Do not portray them as villains of the mystery; the record shows both alliance (Croatoan, Manteo) and violence on both sides (the English killed Wingina in 1586; Roanoke-area men killed George Howe in 1587). Label legends as legends.
- **Copyright:** White's 1590 text, Hakluyt (1589, 1600), Hariot/de Bry (1590), Strachey and Smith are public domain. **Scans** of 16th-century pages and engravings from the Internet Archive, Library of Congress and similar PD-friendly holders are usable. **British Museum photographs of White's watercolours and of his map are licensed CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 (non-commercial)**; a monetised channel needs a BM Images licence (section 18). Modern photos (NPS staff, press, First Colony Foundation, Croatoan Archaeological Society) are copyrighted unless marked otherwise.

### Suggested chapter clock (all defensible)
1. Cold open: night of Mon 17 Aug 1590, no moon. A trumpet call across black water, then English songs. No answer.
2. Rewind: 27 Aug 1587. The colonists beg the governor to go home for supplies. He leaves his daughter and his 9-day-old granddaughter. (Narration only; no images of them.)
3. 1588: the Armada; the stay on shipping; White's failed attempt.
4. 20 Mar 1590: Plymouth. No colonists allowed aboard; "not so much as a boy".
5. Five months of privateering. 12 Aug: anchored off Croatoan without knowing it mattered.
6. Sat 15 Aug, towards evening: smoke on Roanoke Island.

8. Mon 17 Aug, 10 a.m.: the bar. Soon after (White gives no time): Spicer's boat rolls. Seven drown.

14. 28 Aug: the wind turns. 24 Oct: Plymouth. 1593: "I leaue off".

## 6. The agreed sign: what the code meant (and didn't)

| Point | Detail | Status | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| The rule (1587) | Carve on trees or doorposts **the name of the place** they had gone to | CONFIRMED | HAK1600 p. 292 |
| The distress signal | If "distressed", carve **a cross** over the letters or name | CONFIRMED | HAK1600 p. 292 |
| Why a sign was needed | When White left, they "were prepared to remoue from Roanoak 50 miles into the maine" | CONFIRMED | HAK1600 p. 292; PG13 XXX |
| What White found | "C R O" on a tree at the top of the bank; "CROATOAN" on the palisade gatepost; **no cross on either** | CONFIRMED | HAK1600 pp. 292–293 |
| What White concluded | They had gone to Croatoan, among friends, and were safe | CONFIRMED (his inference) | HAK1600 p. 293 |
| What it does NOT prove | Whether all went to Croatoan, or only some; whether the 50-mile move inland happened first; when they left; whether they stayed. "CROATOAN" names a place White expected; it is not a message about what happened afterward | COMPUTED (logic) | — |
| Small puzzle | Two carvings: three letters "C R O" on a tree, the full word on the gatepost. White read both as the same message. Why the tree carving stops at three letters (unfinished? an abbreviation?) is unknown; any explanation is speculation | CONFIRMED (text) / speculation | HAK1600 pp. 292–293 |
| The colony had time | The houses were "taken downe" (dismantled, not burned), a palisade had been built after White left, heavy goods left behind, boats gone: signs of an organised move, not a sudden attack (NPS: no signs of a struggle or hasty departure) | CONFIRMED (observations) / REPORTED (NPS reading) | HAK1600 p. 293; NPS-1590 |

## 11. Theories and archaeology: what is KNOWN vs what is THEORY

### 11.1 Known (documented)
- The colonists were on Roanoke in late August 1587 and gone by 18 August 1590. [CONFIRMED: PG13; HAK1600]
- They had dismantled their houses, built a palisade, buried chests, and taken their boats and small guns. [CONFIRMED: HAK1600 p. 293]
- They left "CRO" and "CROATOAN", and no distress cross. [CONFIRMED: HAK1600 pp. 292–293]
- In 1587 they had told White they planned to move "50 miles into the maine". [CONFIRMED: PG13 XXX; HAK1600 p. 292]
- 1587–1589 was the most extreme drought in 800 years in the region's tree-ring record. [CONFIRMED: STAHLE98]
- No graves, bodies, buildings or village have been **definitively** linked to the colonists anywhere. [CONFIRMED: NPS-THEORIES]
- NPS: "no one has definitively proven what happened to the Lost Colony." [CONFIRMED: NPS-THEORIES]

## 21. Five candidate cold-open moments

1. **The trumpet (night of 17 Aug 1590).** Black water, no moon, a fire in the trees. A trumpet call. Then English songs, one after another. "but we had no answere." Cut to title.
2. **The gatepost (dawn, 18 Aug 1590).** Footprints in the sand from the night. A peeled post, five feet up: CROATOAN. A man runs his eyes over it looking for one more mark, a cross. It isn't there.
3. **The bar (10 a.m., 17 Aug 1590).** A northeast gale; the boat rolls keel-up; two men hang on "vntill they sunke, and were seene no more." The rescue party is seven men short before it has even reached the colony.
4. **The promise (27 Aug 1587).** Over a hundred people ask their governor to leave them. He says no, then yes. He sails at half a day's notice, leaving his daughter and his nine-day-old granddaughter. He agrees one code with them: a name, and a cross if in danger. Narration only, no women on screen.
5. **The anchors (morning, 19 Aug 1590).** The wind is finally fair for Croatoan. The capstan turns; the cable snaps; a second anchor, a third. "one Cable and Anchor left vs of foure." About fifty miles from Croatoan, he turns for home.

## 22. Title and thumbnail cautions

- **Do NOT claim:** "solved", "found", "the truth revealed", "finally", "DNA proves", "they were killed by...", "they became..." None is established; two 2026 "found" claims are unreviewed and contradict each other.
- "Vanished without a trace" is false (there was a message). "Vanished" alone is acceptable; "the word they left" is better.
- Numbers: use "115" or "more than 110"; avoid "117" or "118" as hard figures. "3 years" is fine (1,087 days). "7 drowned" is safe. "50 miles" is the colonists' own figure.
- Dates: "18 August 1590" (Old Style, as NPS uses) is safe; don't add "Tuesday" without "(Old Style calendar)" in notes. "1587" for "vanished" is wrong.
- Halloween framing: "mystery", "the last word", "no answer" are fine; "curse", "ghosts", "monsters" must be labelled legend if used at all; never cast the Croatoan or Roanoke people as the threat.
- Thumbnails: no women or girls (no Virginia Dare, no Eleanor, no Queen Elizabeth, no drama photos, no women in White's art). Safe: the word CROATOAN (labelled reconstruction if carved), the 1600 printed page, a trumpet, a dark shoreline, a palisade, de Bry's 1590 map, an anchor, a broken cable.
- Don't put Dawson, FCF, Horton or Luccketti in a title as having "found" the colony; attribute: "archaeologists say...", "one team claims...".
- "CROATOAN" carved prop or graphic: label "reconstruction"; the original does not survive.
- Avoid "first European child"; use "first English child".
- Don't use BM watercolour images in thumbnails without a commercial licence.
