## Best 5 for the thumbnail (Hakawi style: one big real scene + huge words on solid blocks)
1. **A14, 'CROATOAN' engraving, 1876** (3664×2282, PD): the only strong picture of the moment itself. Men only: four armed Englishmen at the post carved CROATOAN, an opened chest and a trumpet behind them. Crop the post and the two men beside it (left half); it holds up in pure B&W. No period (1590) picture of the post exists, so this is the hero.
2. **A1, John White's 'La Virginea Pars'** (1354×2500): the real 1585 map with Roanoac and Croatoan written by the man who lost them. Crop the band from Roanoac down to Croatoan (lower middle of the sheet). For words-only backup use **A12** (Hakluyt p. 293, the printed word CROATOAN, 3418×4926).
3. **C5, Bakhuizen 'Ships in Distress off a Rocky Coast'** (1667; 1920 px copy, original 15918 px): black storm sky, ships near rocks. Backups: **C3** (van de Velde, English ship clawing off a lee shore, portrait) and **E1** (Pexels storm sea).
4. **D1, Fort Raleigh earthwork in black and white** (NPS, 1920×1079; original 4837×2719): the actual site, snow-white banks against black trees, very high contrast.
5. **E2, a lone leafless tree on an empty beach at dusk** (Pexels, 1920×1281): the 'tree' without fake carving; put the word CROATOAN on a block beside it. For a carved trunk use **E5** (blur the modern initials).

## Licence key
- **PD-old / PD-art**: 16th-17th-century maps, drawings, prints and paintings (White, de Bry, Hakluyt, Hariot, portraits, sea paintings) and 19th-century engravings. Faithful photos/scans of 2D public-domain works carry no new copyright in the US; Commons hosts them as PD-art.
- **PD (US gov work)**: National Park Service (Fort Raleigh NHS, Cape Hatteras NS), Historic American Buildings Survey / Library of Congress, NASA, USGS.
- **Public Domain Mark**: the Wellcome Collection copy of Hakluyt on the Internet Archive.
- **IA scans with no stated licence** (the 1588 Hariot on microfilm, the 1590 de Bry/Hariot at the John Carter Brown Library): the books are from 1588/1590, and the scans are faithful copies of 2D pages, so they are PD-art.
- **CC0** (public-domain dedication): The Metropolitan Museum of Art Open Access; ncwetlands.org (North Carolina wetlands program) photos on Commons.
- **Pexels licence**: free commercial use, attribution optional.
- **CC BY / CC BY-SA**, and anything with women or unverifiable figures: only in section H, flagged in bold.

## Hard rules applied (everything in A-F passed them)
- **No women or girls.** Every image was checked by eye, zooming into anything with small figures. John White's album has many portraits of women and girls; all were excluded, as were the de Bry plates that show women, the de Bry title page and Adam-and-Eve frontispiece, every Virginia Dare and Eleanor Dare image, the Virginia Dare statue and every Queen Elizabeth I portrait. The Hakluyt pages name Eleanor and Virginia Dare in the text only (no pictures). Maps with women among their decorations are in H as crop-only.
- **Real only.** Period maps, documents, drawings, engravings and paintings; 19th-century engravings of the CROATOAN scene (A14, I9, I10), labelled as later imaginings; real photos and satellite images. No modern illustrations (the NPS 'artist's reconstruction' of the fort was excluded), no AI, no cartoons.
- **Respect.** White's portraits of Algonquian men are used as he drew them, one dignified figure each. Left out: the ossuary/tomb images with bodies (White's 'ossuary temple', de Bry plate XXII), and 19th-century scenes of killing or burning (George Howe shot with arrows, 'Burning of Aquascogoc').
- **Copyright-safe.** No British Museum downloads (their licence is non-commercial): White's drawings come from the public-domain copies on Wikimedia Commons. No press, Getty or AP photos. CC BY/BY-SA only in H.

## Could not find
- **A 1590 picture of the CROATOAN post or the CRO tree.** None exists: de Bry's 1590 plates show the 1585 voyage, not White's return. The earliest images are 19th-century engravings (A14, I9, I10, H10). The carved 'CRO' tree at Fort Raleigh is a prop from the outdoor play (H7, CC BY).
- **Authentic portraits of Manteo, Wanchese, Ralph Lane, Simon Fernandes or Thomas Harriot.** None survive. The Trinity College, Oxford portrait once called Harriot is catalogued as 'allegedly but not' him. Lane appears through his signed preface (B5).
- **A PD photo of the Site X dig (First Colony Foundation) or of the Croatoan Archaeological Project at Buxton.** Only press and project photos (copyrighted). Site X is covered by A2 (the patch), A15 (1679 chart), F5 (aerial) and H5 (Bertie County swamp, CC BY).
- **PD landscape photos in Bertie County itself.** Only CC BY (H5, Roanoke River NWR). D11 (Chowan River, CC0) is upstream, in the Chowan Swamp Game Land.
- **An oblique PD aerial photo of Roanoke Island.** None found; F4 (USGS vertical aerial) and F2/F3 (Landsat) stand in.
- **Real 16th-century anchors, chests or trumpets from the colony.** Nothing from Roanoke survives in a PD photo; the Met's period chest, armour and trumpet (E10-E12) and Pexels anchors (E8) stand in. The NPS copper ornaments (D4) are real finds from the site.
- **The original Zúñiga map (Archivo General de Simancas) at good size.** Only a 456×600 copy; A16 is Ashe's 1908 redrawing, clearer for our marks.
- **Library of Congress / NARA catalogues** were not searched directly beyond what Commons carries (the LOC de Bry plates and maps are on Commons; the JCB scans are sharper).
