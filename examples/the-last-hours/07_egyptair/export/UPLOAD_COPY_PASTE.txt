DESCRIPTION (no links)
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
EgyptAir Flight 990, a Boeing 767 from New York to Cairo with 217 people aboard, dived into the Atlantic 60 miles south of Nantucket on October 31, 1999. Minutes earlier the captain had stepped out of the cockpit. He was gone 107 seconds.

This is what the recorders captured, second by second, from the NTSB's own transcript and flight data: the night the clocks fell back an hour, the early seat change, the last "good morning" to New York, the autopilot switched off, the dive, the captain's return, the split elevators, the engines switched off, and "pull with me". Then the radar, the search, the recorders brought up from 230 feet, and why Washington and Cairo still disagree. The NTSB found that the dive resulted from the relief first officer's flight control inputs and said the reason was not determined; Egypt's investigators rejected that and said the evidence could not identify a cause. Both positions are printed in the same final document.
