Compiled 22 Oct 2026 for **The Last Hours**, folder 07_egyptair (file #6). **Nothing has been downloaded into the project.** The owner approves every item. (Small preview copies and the source PDFs were opened in a temp folder only, to check each picture by eye.)
- **px** is the pixel size of that exact URL (Commons API, or read from the image itself). For **PDF pages** (NTSB report and docket) px is a **200 dpi render** of that page: render it yourself (`pdftoppm -f N -l N -r 200 -jpeg file.pdf out`); 300 dpi is fine for the text pages.
- **size** comes from an HTTP HEAD (Content-Length) on that exact URL. Commons originals: the API's byte count (Commons answered 429 to bursts). Generated images (NASA Worldview, NOAA chart export): byte count of a GET. PDF pages: size of a 200 dpi JPEG (quality 90) render, for planning.
- MB = 1,048,576 bytes.
- The **licence** cell links to the source page (Commons file page, NTSB docket, NASA, NOAA, Pexels page). Use it to check before downloading.

## Best 5 for the thumbnail (one strong real image filling the frame + 2 short text lines)
1. **C4, the crushed flight data recorder** (NTSB docket photo, PD): the orange "FLIGHT RECORDER / DO NOT OPEN" casing torn open and crumpled, with a handwritten recovery tag. Instantly says "black box". Crop the photo out of the page (about 1700 px wide at 200 dpi; render at 300 dpi for more). Text lines go on the dark top-left.
2. **H1, SU-GAP itself** (simon butler, Zurich, June 1991, 4117x2712, **CC BY 2.0**): the actual aircraft, full side profile, registration SU-GAP readable on the rear fuselage. Grade it dark/night. **Needs your decision:** credit line required ("simon butler / Flickr, CC BY 2.0"), and his upload doesn't say who shot the slide. If you say no, use C4 or C1.
3. **C1, USS Grapple leaving port in the dark, 31 Oct 1999** (US Navy, PD): the salvage ship lit up, a searchlight beam across the water, on the night of the crash. Very "last hours" mood; no readable people.
4. **A9, the last page of the cockpit voice recorder transcript** (NTSB, PD): "what is this? what is this? did you shut the engine(s)?" ... "pull with me." x3 ... "END OF RECORDING". Text-as-image thumbnail; crop tight to the last six lines.
5. **C9, the fuselage "window belt"** (NTSB docket photo #7, PD): a green-primed fuselage panel with two empty window frames, on the hangar floor at Quonset Point. Haunting, no people. Crop the photo out of the page.

**Crew-portrait verdict:** no public-domain photo of Captain Ahmed El-Habashi, relief first officer Gameel Al-Batouti or any crew member was found (only press-agency photos exist). None is listed. Use the cockpit, the transcript and the recorders instead of faces.

## Hard rules applied (everything in A-F passed them)
- **No women or girls.** Every image was opened and checked, zooming into any figures. People in A-F: the two named men in C3 (FBI Agent Duback, Lt. j.g. Jason S. Hall) with two standing crew seen only as torsos (crop note in the row); three men on Grapple's deck in C2 (plus one tiny figure at the right edge: crop). Everything else has no people. Excluded: the NOAA OPC desk photo (a woman is named in it), Pexels photos with crew or passengers, docket hangar photos with Tyvek-suited figures in the background (Structures App. H pages 8, 11, 14).
- **No victims, no remains, nothing gory.** Wreckage photos are bare aluminium, insulation, engine parts and recorders; checked by eye at full size. No personal effects, no photos of the passengers, no family photos. The docket's Human Performance and FBI-interview files were not used.
- **Real only.** Period photographs, NTSB plots and transcripts, real charts and satellite images; Pexels stand-ins are real photos. Excluded: fan-made FDR graphics and German route maps on Commons (Anynobody, Niabot, kaʁstn), a 1999 events montage.
- **Truth.** The transcript pages are the NTSB's English translation of the CVR (the docket also holds the Arabic transcript). The ECAA disputes the NTSB's reading of the evidence: if the ECAA pages are used (H10-H12), caption them as the Egyptian position. Stand-ins (another 767, another cockpit, Pexels) must be labelled.

The two docket photo PDFs that are on Commons (Structures App. H, Powerplants App. 1) render per page as JPG through `thumb.wikimedia.org` (`pageN-1920px-...pdf.jpg`), which are the direct URLs given.

- **Pexels:** CDN URLs at w=1920 returned 200. The pexels.com site shows a bot check to scripts; search was done in the app's browser, nothing was bypassed.
- **navsea.navy.mil** (Navy SUPSALV "Faceplate" newsletters) answered 403; the archived Dec 1999 issue only mentions EgyptAir 990 in text.
