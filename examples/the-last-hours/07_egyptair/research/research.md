# EGYPTAIR 990: verified research (Boeing 767-366ER SU-GAP, JFK to Cairo, Sunday 31 October 1999)

Channel: The Last Hours (@TheLastHoursDocs; US / Canada / UK audience). Format: hour-by-hour countdown told only from the record. Folder 07_egyptair (file #6), release target **Saturday 7 November 2026**.
Spine: from the crews' van ride to JFK (about 11:30 p.m. EDT, Saturday 30 October 1999) through the 31-minute flight, the 2 minutes 45 seconds between autopilot disconnect and impact, the search off Nantucket, the recovery of the recorders, the FBI question, and the split between the U.S. National Transportation Safety Board (NTSB) and the Egyptian Civil Aviation Authority (ECAA) that has never closed.
Compiled 2026-10-22.

**Clock note (read first):** U.S. daylight saving time ended at **2:00 a.m. EDT on Sunday 31 October 1999**, while SU-GAP sat at the JFK gate. Clocks went back to 1:00 a.m. EST at 0600 UTC. Everything the NTSB brief times after midnight is **EST (UTC-5)**; the inbound landing and the crew's hotel departure are **EDT (UTC-4)**; ATC and meteorology reports are in **UTC**. The hour 1:00-1:59 a.m. happened twice that night. Full table in section 2.

**Transcript note:** the CVR transcript quoted here is the NTSB's English translation (Attachment A of the brief, and docket item 111), prepared by a 10-person group that included 5 Arabic speakers. The NTSB itself warns that direct translation sometimes produced "awkward or seemingly inappropriate phrases". The audio has never been released (U.S. law bars the NTSB from releasing CVR audio). Do not present any audio as the real recording.

## How to read this file

Every fact carries a status tag and one or more source IDs (section 0).

- **[CONFIRMED]**: primary/official record: the NTSB brief AAB-02/01 and its CVR transcript, the NTSB public docket (DCA00MA006), the ECAA's own report and response, FAA/Federal Register documents.
- **[REPORTED]**: credible secondary: contemporary AP / VOA / NYT / PBS / CBS / Maclean's reporting, USNI Proceedings, court-related law-firm notes, later press. Wikipedia used only as a lead-finder and tagged as such.
- **[DISPUTED]**: sources disagree (both sides given).
- **[COMPUTED]**: our arithmetic from confirmed inputs (time zones, weekdays, intervals, distances, unit conversions). Method shown.
- **[LEGEND]**: rumour, myth, conspiracy, or popular error. Do not narrate as fact.

Rules kept: no invented numbers, quotes or sources. Long quotes only from U.S. government text (public domain). ECAA and EgyptAir documents quoted briefly, attributed. Modern writers paraphrased; any modern quote under 15 words.

**Content flags for the edit (hard rules):**
- **217 people died.** Factual, respectful tone. No gore. No speculation presented as fact. No details of human remains on screen or in VO beyond "no one survived".
- **Women and girls: no images, ever.** That includes the 10 flight attendants, the New York Center controller who worked the flight, women passengers, widows and mothers at memorials, the Red Cross chaplain whose account we use, and any girl. **Narration only.** List in section 18.
- **Religion:** the relief first officer's repeated phrase is part of the record (section 6). It is an everyday expression. The video stays neutral about religion; this is not a story about Islam.
- **Unproven personal allegations about any crew member are excluded as fact.** They appear only in section 17 (do-not-say), labelled unproven, with the reason.
- **Both official positions get their own exact words** (NTSB section 5.3, ECAA section 5.4).

## 8. KNOWN vs THEORY

### 8.1 Known (documented, both sides agree)
- The flight was normal for 29 minutes; ATC handling had irregularities that the NTSB judged not relevant. [CONFIRMED: NTSB-AAB; ECAA-RPT findings 18-19]
- The captain left the cockpit at 1:48:18 a.m. and came back as the dive was under way. [CONFIRMED]
- The autopilot was disconnected without a warning tone, the throttles went to idle, the elevators moved nose-down, the engines' start levers went to cutoff, and the elevators split. These are FDR facts. Who moved what is the dispute. [CONFIRMED / DISPUTED]
- The relief first officer said "I rely on God" 11 times, quietly. [CONFIRMED]
- The airplane partly recovered and climbed before a second dive. [CONFIRMED]
- No fire, no explosive, no fatigue. Weather and other traffic not involved (NTSB); ECAA keeps the radar question open. [CONFIRMED / DISPUTED]
- Nobody found a written note, statement or other evidence of intent; the NTSB states no motive. [CONFIRMED: NTSB-AAB; ECAA-RPT]

### 8.2 The theories

| Theory | For | Against / problems | Status | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **A. Relief FO's control inputs (NTSB probable cause)** | Alone in cockpit; no anomaly before the first phrase; seat moved forward; manual autopilot disconnect; throttles faster than autothrottle and past its limit; dive began after throttle move; **no significant nose-up elevator movement in the first 28 s of the sequence**, until the captain returned (NTSB); calm voice, no call for help, no answer to "what's happening?"; elevator split matches opposing columns; engine cutoff by the right-seat pilot (captain's surprised "did you shut the engine(s)?"); simulations: all failure scenarios recoverable by one pilot | **No motive or intent established** (NTSB: not determined); FDR records no column force/position; simulator could not reproduce near-zero/negative G or transonic data (extrapolated above Mach 0.91 from 777 tunnel data); ECAA: only about a third of nose-down authority used; throttles to idle and speedbrakes slow a dive; door unlocked; wings kept within 10 degrees of level | CONFIRMED (official US finding) | NTSB-AAB; ECAA-RESP |
| **B. Mechanical elevator failure (ECAA)**: dual right-elevator PCA jam, bellcrank rivet failure, or aft cable break with forward jam | Sheared rivets (opposite directions); sheared PCA pin and misplaced spring; FAA ADs 00-17-05 and 01-04-09 on 767 bellcrank rivets (ECAA: 152+ defective rivets found fleet-wide, 52 replaced); other 767 elevator incidents (United 1994/1996, AeroMexico 2000, Gulf Air and American Mar/Apr 2001); jams can leave no mark (USAir 427); ECAA: first 13 s close to dual-jam curve; one cable break/jam variant matched initial positions (NTSB concedes "a few seconds"); autopilot "hunting" report from 30 Oct | Initial deflection about 3.6 degrees vs about 6 degrees predicted for the PCA scenarios (hinge data would need to be 79% off); after the captain returned **both elevators moved nose-up together**; right elevator later exceeded what a failed surface could reach; no single latent PCA failure in pre-accident FDR data; no jam marks and ample clearance; Boeing found no matching failure; all scenarios recoverable; the relief FO made no recovery attempt; the AA/767 incidents' FDR data showed "no similarities" | DISPUTED (ECAA's preferred theory; NTSB ruled out) | ECAA-RPT; ECAA-ADD; NTSB-AAB |
| **C. Evasive manoeuvre (ECAA alternative)** | Unidentified primary radar returns on tracks meeting the flight path at about Mach 1.4 (ECAA); 8 of 18 radar data requests refused as classified; military flight ARISE57 nearby in the sector | USAF Radar Evaluation Squadron: Riverhead returns were radio interference from the Bucks Harbor, Maine radar; 4 of 5 radar sites saw nothing near the flight; warning area not in use; no conflicting ATC traffic; nothing on the CVR about traffic | DISPUTED (NTSB rejects; ECAA "cannot rule out") | NTSB-AAB; ECAA-RPT; D-ATC |
| **D. | **E. Struggle vs "differing perceptions"** | NTSB: opposing inputs, captain nose-up, relief FO nose-down, "pull with me" | ECAA: no argument on the CVR; different inputs could reflect different readings of pitch; in the last 10 s the captain eased nose-up while the right side moved toward nose-up | DISPUTED | NTSB-AAB; ECAA-RESP 43-44 |
| **F. Bomb, missile, sabotage** | Early speculation; Egyptian newspaper claims | No fire or explosive damage; FAA explosives unit negative; radar shows no projectile; airplane intact into the climb | **No evidence** (LEGEND as claimed) | D-FAAEXP; NTSB-AAB; SALON991119 |
| **G. A motive story (revenge, personal life)** | Claims by a defecting pilot and unnamed sources, 2000-2002 | Unproven; never adopted by the NTSB; denied by Egyptian officials | **Excluded**: section 17 | TAHA00; NYT020316 |

### 8.3 Context, not proof
- The 767 elevator: each elevator half driven by 3 hydraulic PCAs; captain's column drives the left elevator, first officer's the right; override mechanisms let one side split from the other if one jams. [CONFIRMED: NTSB-AAB]
- Engine start levers are spring-loaded switches that must be lifted out of a detent to move; engines stop 5-6 s after cutoff. [CONFIRMED: NTSB-AAB fn 19]
- The NTSB has never had cockpit video; it asked for it in April 2000, partly because of this case. [CONFIRMED: NTSB-AAB fn 109]

## 13. Glossary

| Term | Meaning | Source |
|---|---|---|

| Blowdown | The limit air loads put on how far an elevator can deflect at high speed | NTSB-AAB |
| Load factor / G | Force relative to weight: 1 G level flight, 0 G weightless, negative G loose objects rise to the ceiling | NTSB-AAB fn 16 |

| Autopilot disconnect (double-click) | Pressing the yoke switch twice within 0.5 s cancels the warning siren | NTSB-AAB |
| Throttle (thrust lever) / engine start lever | Sets engine power / switches fuel on (RUN) or off (CUTOFF) | NTSB-AAB |
| Speedbrakes | Spoilers raised on the wings to slow the airplane | NTSB-AAB |
| Primary vs secondary radar | Reflected echo (no identity, rough altitude) vs transponder reply with code and altitude (mode C) | NTSB-AAB fn 22 |

## 21. Five candidate cold-open moments (channel hook: 2-second "imagine" or shock line, then weekday + date + clock)

1. **The doubled hour (recommended).** "Imagine a night when 1:53 a.m. happens twice." → "Saturday night into Sunday, October 31, 1999. 1:53 a.m., Eastern Daylight Time. JFK." The pilots of EgyptAir 990 call for their clearance to Cairo. Seven minutes later the clocks fall back. "By the time New York's clocks read 1:53 a second time, this plane, and 217 people, were in the Atlantic." (Impact "about 1:52:30" EST.) (Every element CONFIRMED; the doubling COMPUTED.)
2. **Good morning.** "The last words anyone heard from this plane were 'good morning.'" → "Sunday, October 31, 1999. 1:47 and 40 seconds a.m., Eastern Standard Time. Thirty-three thousand feet over the Atlantic." Thirty-eight seconds later the captain steps out of the cockpit.
3. **The 107 seconds.** "Imagine leaving a cockpit for 107 seconds." → "Sunday, October 31, 1999. 1:48 a.m." "Excuse me... a quick trip to the toilet... before it gets crowded." The door, unlocked, closes behind him at 1:48:18.
4. **The dive.** "Imagine a 767 falling 39,000 feet a minute, in the dark." → "Sunday, October 31, 1999. 1:49 and 54 seconds a.m." The nose drops. Twelve seconds later the captain is back: "What's happening? What's happening?"
