**0:00 HOOK**
You've never seen it, but one in five barrels of oil on Earth needs this two-mile lane.
`[ON SCREEN: old-paper world map, fast zoom onto the Gulf; a tanker with the captain potato (nervous) sails in; caption]`

**0:04**
This is the Strait of Hormuz. Iran on top. The tip of Oman at the bottom.
`[ON SCREEN: Iran fills muted red, Oman green; serif italic names write on]`

**0:08**
At its narrowest, it's twenty-one miles wide. Sounds like plenty.
`[ON SCREEN: a red marker ruler line across the strait: "21 miles"]`

**0:12**
But tankers don't get twenty-one miles. They get two lanes. Two miles in. Two miles out. And a two-mile gap in the middle.
`[ON SCREEN: two lane arrows draw on with a gap between them; little tankers queue nose to tail; "2 mi · 2 mi · 2 mi"]`

**0:21**
In 2024, about twenty million barrels a day went through here. Roughly a fifth of all the oil the world uses.
`[ON SCREEN: counter rolls to 20,000,000 / day; the oil-worker potato (hard hat) rolls barrels onto a tanker; tag "EIA, 2024"]`

**0:29**
And the whole north shore? That's Iran. Its coast, and the islands it holds, sit right beside the route.
`[ON SCREEN: Iran's coast glows; island dots pop along the lanes (Qeshm, Hormuz, Larak, Abu Musa, the Tunbs; no labels)]`

**0:36**
This week, at least twelve tankers were attacked in and around the strait. Oil is back above a hundred and six dollars.
`[ON SCREEN: red markers blink on the map (no characters near them); a "$106 / barrel" sign; date tag "this week, 13 Oct 2026"]`

**0:44**
That's why a strip of water you'll never see shows up at your pump.
`[ON SCREEN: the host potato (suit, yellow tie) at a gas pump, side-eye at the price]`

**0:49 LOOP**
Two lanes. Two miles. A fifth of the world's oil.
`[ON SCREEN: back on the map, the two lanes glowing; zoom out to the opening frame so the loop is seamless]`
