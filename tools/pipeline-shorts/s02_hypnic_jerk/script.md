**Sources** (checked 18 Oct 2026)
- Sleep Foundation, "Hypnic Jerk: Why You Twitch When You Sleep", updated 25 Jul 2025, medically reviewed by John DeBanto, MD:
  "up to 70% of people experience them at some point"; "Researchers don't know for certain what causes hypnic jerks";
  theory "your brain mistakenly assumes you're really falling"; triggers stimulants (caffeine, nicotine), vigorous
  exercise at night, sleep deprivation, stress/anxiety; "completely normal and generally harmless".
- Y. Fatima, A. Metse (UniSC), D. Sullivan (Griffith), The Conversation via UniSC, 30 Sep 2025: "An estimated 70% of
  people…"; "we don't know exactly why it happens"; "the brain misreads this relaxation as a sensation of falling";
  "normal and generally no cause for concern"; triggers incl. sleep deprivation, stress, caffeine/nicotine, exercise.
- Background: Chiaro et al., Sleep Medicine 2016 (PMID 28007358) cites 60-70% general-population prevalence.
