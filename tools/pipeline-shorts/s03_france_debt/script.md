# News with Habibi — Short #3 (map style): France's debt — draft v1, 18 Oct 2026

Source idea: a Facebook reel by "المجلة بيزنس" ("France is burning under a mountain of debt", "$3.6 trillion",
"is the state collapsing / bankruptcy?"). We do NOT reuse its footage. Our angle corrects it: the debt is €3.6
TRILLION EUROS (~$4.2T), and France is not going bankrupt; it pays more to borrow.

**Title** (title.py): `France Owes €3.6 Trillion. Here's The Problem 😬` (94) · alt `France Now Pays More To Borrow Than Greece 😮` (76)
**Hook** (hookscore.py, 14 written in 3 rounds; we keep the fact up front per our retention data):
`You think Greece is Europe's debt problem? This month, France pays more to borrow than Greece.`

Style: Short #1 map engine (old-paper Europe, France filled, Paris dot), potato cast in ROLES only (the lender in a suit,
a student in a hoodie, the host). No fire/riot imagery, no characters near clashes. Numbers as big pops.

1. You think Greece is Europe's debt problem? This month, France pays more to borrow than Greece.
   `[frame 1: Europe map, France glowing red, Greece blue; two price tags "FRANCE ~5%" > "GREECE"; top text "IS FRANCE BROKE? 🤔"]`
2. Its debt just hit three point six trillion euros. That's a hundred and nineteen percent of everything France makes in a year.
   `[counter rolls to €3,600,000,000,000; "119% of GDP"; INSEE tag]`
3. And next year it needs to borrow about three hundred and forty billion more.
   `[a stack of euro bills grows over Paris]`
4. The problem? Lenders now want up to five percent interest. The highest since two thousand and two.
   `[lender potato (suit, side-eye) holding a "5%" sign; a line chart jumps]`
5. Next year, the interest alone is set to cost about ninety-one billion euros. That's around two hundred and fifty million a day.
   `[€91 BILLION / €250 MILLION A DAY; coins drain out of the map]`
6. So the government wants fifty-four billion euros in savings and new taxes, including a pay freeze for teachers.
   `["€54 BILLION"; a teacher-pay chip with a lock]`
7. Students walked out first. On October seventeenth, about five hundred and fifty thousand people marched across France.
   `[student potatoes (hoodies) with blank signs walking across the map; "550,000" (French authorities)]`
8. So, is France going bankrupt? No. It can still borrow. Its lenders just want to be paid more.
   `[question → "NO"; the lender potato counting coins]`
9. And that's how France ended up paying more than Greece.
   `[back to frame 1 for the loop]`

~145 words ≈ 55 s.

  in 2026; 2027 plan ~€54bn of savings and extra revenue; €340bn of debt issuance in 2027; 10-year yield up to 5%, highest since
  July 2002; "France now pays more to borrow than Italy and Greece"; "France is not a country that has lost access to markets.
  It is a country whose lenders want to be paid more."
- Government 2027 budget (via Dette de la France / La Clé républicaine, 1-2 Oct 2026): interest charge of public
  administrations €79.2bn (2026) → €91.2bn (2027). €91.2bn / 365 ≈ €250m a day (our arithmetic).
- CNBC (2 Oct 2026): 2027 budget freezes wages for public-sector workers like teachers; student movement began 18 Sep in Créteil.
- Al Jazeera / NPR (17 Oct 2026): ~550,000 protesters nationwide incl. ~110,000 in Paris, per French authorities.
- Fitch (12 Oct 2026, via Anadolu/others): A+ stable kept on 28 Aug; warned of a negative outlook / downgrade risk.
